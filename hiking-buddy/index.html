Zone 1 · Foothills & Gold Country

The close-in walks — Pine Grove, Jackson, and a run up Hwy 4 to the big trees. Most days, this is where the answer lives.

Lake Tabeaud Loop

Lake Tabeaud, off Tabeaud Rd · Jackson / Pine Grove
2.4 mi loopEasy~46 min avg85 ft gain

A mostly flat, wide circuit of a quiet PG&E reservoir lake — pine shade, water views the whole way, wildflowers in spring. The very first trail on this list, and still the one I reach for when a friend asks for "something easy and pretty."

Good to know: dogs welcome on leash · picnic areas and restrooms at the trailhead · great for kids · gates close at dusk · year-round. No swimming or motorized boats on the lake.

Source: AllTrails — Lake Tabeaud Loop · 📍 GPX for inRoute

Indian Grinding Rock — North & South Trails

Indian Grinding Rock State Historic Park · Pine Grove
1 mi + 0.5 miEasy~40 min (est.)Year-round

Two short loops through meadow and oak woodland at Chaw'se — the largest collection of bedrock mortars in North America (1,185 of them, plus petroglyphs), a reconstructed Miwok village, the ceremonial roundhouse, and the Chaw'se Regional Indian Museum. More cultural site than workout — and all the better for it.

Good to know: $8 day-use fee · dogs are NOT allowed on the trails (service animals excepted) · wonderful for kids · treat the grinding rock and village with respect — it's a living cultural site, still used for gatherings like the September Big Time.

Source: California State Parks — park trails · 📍 GPX for inRoute

Calaveras North Grove Trail

Calaveras Big Trees State Park · Arnold, up Hwy 4
1.7 mi loopEasy30–60 min95 ft gain

A gentle, ADA-accessible loop past 100+ giant sequoias — the Discovery Stump, numbered interpretive stops, and trees you have to lean back to see the top of. The farthest drive on this list and worth every mile when company's in town.

Good to know: park entry fee applies · stroller-friendly when dry · open year-round (the one grove trail that stays open in winter; expect snow) · dog policy not yet verified — check park rules before bringing one.Check first AllTrails noted a partial trail closure for repairs in fall 2026 — check current conditions before you go.

Sources: State Parks infosheet · AllTrails · 📍 GPX for inRoute

Zone 2 · Mokelumne River Canyon

Down in the gorge, where the river runs cold and the Gold Rush left its plumbing behind. Rougher edges, bigger payoff — and permits on some of it.

Electra Road Flume Walk

Electra Rd, off Hwy 49 · south of Jackson
~4 mi round tripEasy~1.5 hr (est.)Best in spring

A level walk along the Mokelumne River following the historic 1900s hydroelectric flume to the still-working PG&E Electra Powerhouse. River beaches along the way, and in spring the hillsides go gold with poppies — this is Amador's wildflower walk.

Good to know: free public access (CDFW) · the access road is rough — higher-clearance vehicle recommended, especially after rain · shared with the occasional car, walk facing traffic.Single source The 4-mile figure comes from one county hiking guide; treat it as approximate until I verify it on the ground.

Source: California Hiking Trails — Amador County guide · 📍 GPX for inRoute

Rich Gulch Trail → Middle Bar

Mokelumne Coast to Crest Trail · Rich Gulch access, near Mokelumne Hill
4.4 mi out & backModerate2–2.5 hrBest in spring

Foothill oak woodland along Rich Gulch Creek, opening into the Mokelumne River canyon and ending at the historic Middle Bar Bridge (early-1900s, a classified historic site). Spring wildflowers are the headline act — there's a reason the trail folks run a wildflower hike here every April.

Good to know: EBMUD Trail Use Permit required before you go · no potable water on trail · trail closes at sunset · canyon gets hot in summer — spring and fall are its seasons · no dogs allowed on this EBMUD trail section.

Sources: Mokelumne Coast to Crest Trail — segment guide · AllTrails · 📍 GPX for inRoute

Zone 3 · High Sierra — Highway 88

Up the hill past Kirkwood to the crest. Different world: thin air, granite, alpine lakes. Summer and early fall only — and give yourself grace on the climbs, the trailheads start at 7,800–8,500 ft.

Lake Winnemucca via the PCT

Carson Pass trailhead · Hwy 88
5.0 mi out & backModerate2–2.5 hr~630 ft gain

South from Carson Pass on the Pacific Crest Trail, past little Frog Lake, to an alpine lake sitting under Round Top's shoulder. The best wildflower show in the high country come mid-summer, and the friendliest introduction to Sierra crest hiking I know.

Good to know: trailhead at ~8,500 ft — take the first mile easy if you're coming from the foothills · Carson Pass day-use trailhead permit required · summer–early fall season (snow holds late up here) · dog policy not yet verified.Check first

Source: AllTrails — Lake Winnemucca from Carson Pass · 📍 GPX for inRoute

Emigrant Lake Trail

Emigrant Lake trailhead · west end of Caples Lake · Hwy 88, near Kirkwood
8.6 mi out & backModerate3.5–4 hr1,000 ft gain

The long day on the list. An easy, nearly flat stroll along Caples Lake's shore for the first stretch — then the trail turns up and climbs in earnest to Emigrant Lake, a clear alpine bowl under snow-patched peaks. Earns its lunch.

Good to know: no permit needed for a day hike (Mokelumne Wilderness permit only for overnight) · trailhead ~7,800 ft · summer–early fall · the steep finish means this one's for steady legs, not little kids · dog policy not yet verified.Check first

Source: AllTrails — Emigrant Lake Trail · 📍 GPX for inRoute
