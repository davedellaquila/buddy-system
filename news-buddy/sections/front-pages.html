’Nvidia Is Preparing to Oust CEO Jensen Huang’ — the Journal’s lead: two affairs, one with a suspected Chinese spy, one with an employee; the board met Wednesday and has announced nothing

The Wall Street Journal

The Journal’s front-page lead, from people familiar with the matter: the board is preparing to remove the founder who has run Nvidia since 1993, after Huang’s Sunday letter to staff acknowledged unfaithfulness; the Times earlier reported the second affair, with an executive who reports to him. Huang has about 60 direct reports and has said he does not believe in succession planning — there is no clear successor. The proxy statement’s own warning is now the story: the company is ’highly dependent on the services of our longstanding executive team.’ The Justice Department is leading multiple investigations involving Alice Huang. Shares fell about 8% premarket Thursday. Full market treatment in Markets; the succession market in AI & Technology.

Why it matters: The board preparing to remove a founder-CEO with no succession plan is the week's central corporate fact: earnings Nov. 17, no announced decision, and a stock down 8% premarket. Dave's Nvidia file is now a governance file.

The directors deciding Huang’s fate: 11 members, no chair, lead director Stephen Neal — and a governance structure 1% of U.S. companies share

The Wall Street Journal

From the Journal’s live file: the board deciding the founder’s future has no chair — Neal, Cooley’s chairman emeritus and a director since 2019, presides when Huang is absent and acts as conduit between the board and the CEO. The other nine include Tench Coxe and Caltech’s John Dabiri. The Journal’s kicker is the investor mood: as hundreds of billions in market value evaporate, ’a few more checks on the powerful CEO wouldn’t have gone amiss.’

Why it matters: Who decides matters when there is no chair: Neal's conduit role and a 1%-of-companies structure explain how a founder ran unchecked -- and how he can still be removed. The investor line in the piece is the market's own governance verdict.

Huang’s no-strings contract: Nvidia’s executives are at-will, with no employment, severance or change-in-control agreements — and a code of conduct that never quite bans the relationships at issue

The Wall Street Journal

Also in the Journal’s Thursday file: the May proxy’s plain language means an ouster needs no negotiated exit package — and no golden parachute cushions it either. The published code of conduct does not explicitly ban relationships with subordinates or suppliers, saying only that personal relationships can present a potential conflict of interest; a more detailed policy exists but links to an employees-only site. Governance, not gossip, is the Journal’s second-day frame.

Why it matters: At-will employment cuts both ways: no parachute dispute, no contractual brake. The code-of-conduct gap (conflicts named, relationships not banned) is the detail a post-mortem will quote.

The New York Times
