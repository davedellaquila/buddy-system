The fast-cash advances draining America’s small businesses: MCA costs can reach 2,000%, and funders stand behind over half of 2026’s small-business bankruptcies

The Wall Street Journal

The Journal’s lead investigation, featured in its Oct. 30 10-Point: merchant cash advances promise cash in 24 hours with past credit troubles no problem — but because advances are not legally loans, funders evade lending rules and pull money straight from company bank accounts daily or weekly. A Journal analysis found more than half of the federal bankruptcies filed by small businesses in the first nine months of 2026 owed money to MCA companies. Many firms seeking short-term help instead enter what the paper calls a debt-driven death spiral.

Why it matters: The Journal's investigation reframes 'alternative lending': when over half of small-business bankruptcies trace to one product, the product is the story. Effective costs to 2,000% are legal because advances are not 'loans.'

Officials in the U.S., Singapore and Taiwan fear a data-center executive close to Nvidia’s Jensen Huang is a Chinese intelligence source

The Wall Street Journal

From the Journal’s Oct. 30 headlines list (the story is also the Times’: U.S. intelligence agencies reportedly believe a Megaspeed executive was a Chinese intelligence asset; Nvidia disputes the diversion allegations and says they were investigated and debunked). The allegation lands at the center of the AI hardware trade — a data-center builder with close ties to Nvidia’s chief executive, watched by three governments.

Why it matters: The AI supply chain's trust problem in one headline: a builder close to Nvidia's CEO, watched by three governments. Nvidia's denial is on record; the concern is the story.

The Times Digest’s roster adds ’In Grim Mood, Trump Awaits Elections,’ a broadening U.S. voting inquiry in California, and ’Fed Risks Faltering in Its Fight Vs. Inflation.’

U.S. oil industry earned over $130 billion in profit during the Iran war

The New York Times

Also on the Times’ Oct. 30 front: the war that pushed Brent above $110 has been a windfall at home — more than $130 billion of profit for the U.S. oil industry while the conflict runs into its ninth month. With no clear winner, how could Israel get a prime minister? — deadlock, and a record showing for Arab parties

The New York Times

Bank of Japan holds at 1.25% in an 8-1 vote — and calls inflation ’generally consistent’ with the 2% target for the first time since 2013

Nikkei Asia

Aju Press, Oct. 30, reporting the BoJ’s decision (the Nikkei’s account is the home paper’s): the board kept the overnight call rate at 1.25%; Hajime Takata dissented for 1.5%, arguing underlying inflation has reached the target and policy should move toward neutral. The Outlook Report raised FY2027 core inflation to 2.6% from 2.4%, and the board voted unanimously to step ETF sales up to about 860 billion yen a year (book value) from January 2027. OIS markets price roughly 80% odds of a December hike.

Why it matters: A hold that is actually a turn: 'generally consistent with 2%' for the first time in 13 years, plus ETF sales stepped up. December at ~80% is the market's translation.
