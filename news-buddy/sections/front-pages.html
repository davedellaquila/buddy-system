Wall Street pledged $500 billion for Nvidia — Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock, Brookfield, Goldman, KKR — and not a dollar has been deployed, the Journal reports

The Wall Street Journal

The Journal’s Thursday finance lead: in August, then-CEO Jensen Huang lined up pledges to mobilize $500 billion so Nvidia’s smaller, weaker customers could finance chip purchases. The memorandum was a few pages — a fundraising target, a customer profile, case-by-case backstops — and the firms are not committed to raising anything. To date, per people familiar, no capital has moved, finance chief (now interim CEO) Colette Kress has reined in backstops, and investors doubt the program survives Huang’s ouster. The financing question — how customers keep buying — is now central to the stock’s valuation debate.

Why it matters: A $500B pledge that was never capital: the MOU's fine print is the AI-financing story in miniature. If the circle's funding was always case-by-case, the circle was always smaller than the headline.

The Jensen Huang saga has reinvigorated Washington’s China hawks — new chip-access rules, disclosure weighing, and a fine, as the Journal traces the policy turn

The Wall Street Journal

The Journal’s national-security lead: claims that Nvidia customers funneled billions in sophisticated chips to Chinese AI companies have collided with the outage wave to give export-control hawks new momentum. In recent weeks the administration released a rule making it harder for Chinese firms to access U.S.-chip compute in Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Singapore, backed legislation restricting advanced-chip sales, fined a U.S. firm for funding a Chinese AI company, and is weighing requiring U.S. companies to disclose use of Chinese open-weight models. Huang a year ago called ’China hawk’ a badge of shame; his ouster is now the hawks’ Exhibit A.

Why it matters: Policy moved while the summit smiled: chip-access rules tightened across Southeast Asia and a disclosure rule for Chinese models is being weighed. The hawks' momentum is the durable outcome of the Huang saga.

Nvidia is the central bank to AI’s circular economy: a $100 billion-plus portfolio, $48 billion in eight public names, $518 billion in commitments — the Journal maps the money

The Wall Street Journal

The Journal’s money map, on Thursday’s front conversation: Nvidia owns more than $48 billion of stock in eight public firms as of Sept. 30 — 13% of CoreWeave among them — plus private bets on Anthropic and OpenAI, a $10 billion commitment to buy ANT shares in today’s IPO, a reported $35 billion Anthropic lease with Nvidia-backed Lambda, and up to $105 billion backstopping a SoftBank Ohio data center. Since the Oct. 6 record high, those eight public holdings have shed a combined $820 billion. Kress told Tuesday’s call Nvidia will stay ’disciplined’ — the Journal’s question is what happens to the circle if the center stops funding it.

Why it matters: $48B in eight public names, $518B in commitments, an $820B drawdown since Oct. 6: the Journal's map makes Nvidia's circular economy legible -- and shows exactly who gets hurt if the center stops funding it.
