’October’s Inflation Report Is Coming... With a Key Number AWOL’ — the Journal’s CPI-morning explainer leads its live coverage

The Wall Street Journal

The Journal’s Tuesday framing of the print: the familiar 12-month inflation rate cannot be reported because October 2025 was never measured; the paper will compute its own rough year-ago baseline to approximate one. For traders, it says, the monthly numbers (expected +0.3%, core +0.2%) make it largely business as usual. September’s official annual rates — 3.7% headline, 2.6% core — stand as the last published pair.

Why it matters: The Journal explaining its own missing headline number is the cleanest account of why today's CPI is a monthly-only print -- and why every 'annual rate' quoted today is somebody's estimate.

California voters reject Prop. 40, the billionaire tax — the Times’ result, called by AP Monday at 52.8% no with 83% counted

The New York Times

Via KFF’s First Edition digest of the Times: the one-time 5% tax on residents worth over $1 billion — the nation’s first wealth tax, most revenue to health care — was rejected even as Democrats swept the national midterms. Gov. Newsom and governor-elect Becerra both opposed it; Sergey Brin’s counter-measure, Prop. 42 (banning retroactive/wealth taxes), led 51.6% but had not been called. Full treatment in the California file below the front pages.

Why it matters: California's exception to its own blue wave: the wealth tax lost with Democrats, the governor and the governor-elect against it, and Prop. 42 may salt the earth behind it. Backers say 2028; the money says otherwise.

Supreme Court seems likely to limit prisoners’ suits over poor medical care — the Post’s Monday-argument report

The Washington Post

Via the Post via KFF: at Monday’s argument the justices appeared inclined to nix Kekai Watanabe’s suit — a former federal inmate whose tailbone was broken in a Honolulu detention-center riot and who alleges a prison nurse failed to treat him — further eroding the Bivens precedent that lets people sue federal officials over constitutional violations. A ruling either way lands by summer; the lean reported Tuesday is toward narrowing.

Why it matters: Bivens erosion is slow, technical and nearly irreversible -- the kind of ruling that outlasts election cycles. The Post's lean-reporting is the early warning; the decision lands by summer.

Krakow attack: Polish unity on show as thousands join the silent march — Nawrocki and Tusk walk together on the last night of national mourning

BBC News

The BBC’s Monday-night file, via relay: thousands marched silently through Krakow from 8 p.m., led by President Nawrocki, PM Tusk, Mayor Gibala and Cardinal Rys, starting at Slawkowska Street where the first shots were fired, the St Mary’s bugle sounding as they reached the square. The gunman is named by Polish media as 24-year-old Michal Oksinski; he died early Monday without regaining consciousness. Monday ended national mourning; Krakow’s own mourning runs to Wednesday. One victim identified so far in the BBC file: a Polish painter in her 50s, a mother of four, in town for an exhibition opening.

Why it matters: Rivals walking together behind the coffins' route is Poland's answer to the ideology question: the investigation continues, but the country's public face on Monday night was unity, bugle and silence.
