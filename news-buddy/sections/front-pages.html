How Trump’s War in Iran and Missteps Flipped Washington Overnight — the Journal’s Nov. 5 lead analysis: gas up 40%, all but ~170 of ~3,000 counties swung, median county 11 points toward Democrats

The Wall Street Journal

The Journal’s 10-Point lead, Nov. 5: Tuesday’s losses amount to one of the most decisive repudiations of an American president, and the blowout was a direct result of Trump’s decision to go to war in Iran, which sent gas prices up 40% and left many loyal supporters disillusioned; Trump also lost his advantage on immigration. County by county: of about 3,000 counties where at least half the expected House vote was tallied, all but about 170 saw Republican candidates run behind Trump’s 2024 margin against Kamala Harris, and the median county shifted about 11 points toward Democrats. Chuck Schumer emerges as one of Tuesday’s biggest winners. Also on the Journal’s morning slate: the two uncalled Senate races (Alaska, Mississippi), anti-data-center candidates and ballot measures that could rein in AI expansion, and China blocking SoftBank’s $5.4 billion acquisition of ABB’s robotics unit.

Why it matters: The Journal's causal account, with the county math attached: an 11-point median swing is not a wave metaphor, it's a measurement. Schumer's win and the data-center backlash are the two threads to follow.

The Journal, Nov. 5: two years after handing Republicans control of Washington, voters flipped Congress blue, resetting the balance of power for Trump’s final years and giving Democrats decisive leverage over his nominees and government spending. The same morning edition: stock futures rebounded as investors weighed the sweep; North Korea accelerated missile tests, spurning U.S. overtures; hackers are cranking the heat on Europe’s supermarket freezers above 120 degrees; and 3M agreed to pay $7.35 billion over nine years for PFAS ’forever chemicals’ personal-injury claims.

Treasury and European bond yields tumble to multiweek lows as markets cheer the sweep — 10-year at 4.877%, Brent below $100 at $95.20, gridlock read as fiscal restraint

The Wall Street Journal

The Journal (Emese Bartha), Nov. 5: yields on long-dated Treasurys, German Bunds and U.K. gilts fell to multiweek lows Thursday after the Democratic sweep and a sharp fall in oil, as bond markets cheered the prospect of fiscal restraint under divided government. Brent crude for January delivery traded below $100, last at $95.20. The 10-year and 30-year Treasury yields hit eight-week lows, last trading 4.877% and 5.256% per Tradeweb. ’A Democratic Congress puts a stronger brake on further fiscal expansion, taking some upside risk out of long-term Treasury yields,’ said Shari Hensrud, CIO of MissionSquare. The eurozone 10-year Bund fell to 3.252%, its lowest since late August; the U.K. 10-year gilt hit its lowest in over two months ahead of the Bank of England decision.

Why it matters: Your AAPL-and-rates card: yields at eight-week lows on a gridlock-equals-restraint read, with Brent under $100 doing the inflation work. The Bund and gilt moves say this is a global repricing, not a U.S. one.

Toyota raises its annual profit outlook to 3.9 trillion yen on U.S. hybrid sales — up 500 billion yen from a forecast that had called for a 10% fall; the stock surges 16.7% in Tokyo

Nikkei Asia

The Nikkei’s report, via Seoul Economic Daily, Nov. 5: Toyota sharply raised its consolidated operating-profit forecast for the year ending March 2027 to 3.9 trillion yen (+4% year on year), up 500 billion yen from a prior forecast of 3.4 trillion that had called for a 10% decline, on a weak yen and strong U.S. hybrid sales; the net-profit forecast rose to 3.57 trillion yen from 3.25 trillion. First half (April-September): revenue +9% to 26.9624 trillion yen, net profit +26% to 2.2364 trillion; hybrid sales of 2.36 million units, +8%. Global vehicle sales fell 4% to 5.05 million in the half, and the annual sales forecast was cut to 10.3 million from 10.5 million after flooding in Thailand shut a local plant. Toyota was the Nikkei’s top performer Thursday, up 16.7%.

Why it matters: Toyota is the earnings season's counterweight: hybrids and a weak yen beat the chip panic, and a raised forecast from a company that had guided down is a genuine surprise.

Tokyo’s split session: Nikkei -1.79% to 61,077.90 for a fourth straight fall as chips crater (Ibiden -16.3%, Advantest -14.7%, Kioxia -13.1%), while the Topix rises 1.16% and Toyota, Nintendo and Sony climb
