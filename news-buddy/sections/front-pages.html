The blame game has begun over Anthropic’s turbulent debut — shut-out funds, an overshot open, and $109 billion gone on day two, the Journal reports

The Wall Street Journal

The Journal’s Saturday lead, from its own reporting: Anthropic fell as much as 15% Friday — to five cents above the $83 IPO price — before closing down 8.5% at $88.91, shedding about $109 billion. Investors blame allocation (Citadel favored, BlackRock got half its $10 billion ask), bankers who may have overshot the $111.50 open, and a provision letting employees sell about 300 million shares immediately; the greenshoe was exercised Friday, and another 300 million shares unlock Dec. 15. The Journal frames the stakes beyond ANT: a $4 trillion Nasdaq wipeout this week endangers OpenAI’s planned debut.

Why it matters: Allocation is the debut's second story: who got shares decided who is angry. The Dec. 15 unlock of another 300M shares is the next supply date to circle.

Nikkei Asia

Nikkei Asia’s market file, via Jiji and Reuters closing reports: the Nikkei 225 closed at 56,436.65, down 3,007.56 points (5.06%), below 57,000 for the first time since April 13, with more than 90% of Prime Market stocks lower; TOPIX fell 3.88%. Why it matters: Tokyo's fifth-biggest point fall on an imported trigger, with a holiday Monday ahead, means Asia's next signal comes from futures, not the cash market.
