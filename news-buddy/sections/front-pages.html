The router cascade, in the Journal’s telling: a technician’s installation triggered the failures that grounded U.S. aviation for five hours

The Wall Street Journal

From the Journal’s related file (Drew FitzGerald): the communications-system upgrade ’caused unprecedented turmoil’ when a router installation cascaded through the FAA’s network. Monday’s development, via the FAA’s own Sunday FAQ and USA Today’s follow: investigators have narrowed the flood to two sites — a radio communications site in El Dorado, Arkansas, where L3Harris was deploying the new router, and a radar site in Amarillo, Texas — with both routers now in a Florida test lab and the broadcast function disabled on all new routers as a precaution. Whether Amarillo failed on its own or echoed El Dorado is still open. Engineering carries the full account.

Why it matters: The ground stop becomes a systems story with two named sites and routers in a Florida lab -- the Journal's mechanism account is the one Congress will cite.

Japan’s economy stalls: Q3 GDP contracts at a 0.1% annualized rate — the first contraction in four quarters, against expectations of modest growth

The Cabinet Office’s preliminary print, in Nikkei Asia’s Monday coverage: real GDP slipped an annualized 0.1% in July-September — effectively flat quarter-on-quarter before rounding — as natural disasters and inflation damped consumer spending and net trade weighed. The stall lands on a market already repricing the AI trade and gives Tokyo’s week a second, home-grown problem: Finance Minister Katayama’s response is the domestic follow to watch.

Why it matters: Japan's stall is domestic, not imported: final demand ex-inventories at -2.1% annualized is the number under the flat headline, and it lands the same morning as the chip rout.
