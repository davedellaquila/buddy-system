The JPMorgan alums whose big bet on AI stocks is being put to the test — Latigo Peak’s $18.6B concentrated book, and Thursday’s four sell signals did not include Nvidia, Broadcom or Dell

The Wall Street Journal

The Journal, Nov. 6: Phil Scott and Jim Atwood left JPMorgan Chase last month to form Latigo Peak Partners after the bank said their clients’ portfolios were too concentrated in highflying AI stocks including Nvidia, Broadcom and Dell. Thursday’s selloff wiped a record $2.5 trillion from the Nasdaq; Nvidia fell 9.3%, Broadcom 10% and Dell 15%. The pair are sticking with the strategy behind a combined $18.6 billion in assets; their portfolio was worth $927 million as of April 30, per a statement reviewed by the Journal. Four stocks met their sell criteria Thursday — Nvidia, Broadcom and Dell were not among them. Paywalled; via the Journal’s own coverage lines.

Why it matters: A concentration parable with receipts: two managers left a bank over exactly this book, and Thursday stress-tested it in public. The four sell signals that did NOT include Nvidia, Broadcom or Dell are the discipline detail worth keeping.

’Katherine Clark Could Be The House Majority Leader. Who Is She?’ — the Times profiles the No. 2 House Democrat, set to be the first woman majority leader

The New York Times

The Times, via KFF’s roundup: Representative Katherine M. Clark of Massachusetts, the No. 2 House Democrat, is set to become majority leader in January after her party won control of the chamber. The New York Times

’GOP’s Losses Will Raise Pressure On Conservative Supreme Court Justices To Retire’ — the Post on Alito, 76, and Thomas, 78, and the lame-duck confirmation window that closes Jan. 3

The Washington Post

The Post, via KFF: the GOP’s sweeping midterm losses Tuesday set up an unsettling possibility for the party — if it doesn’t act now to confirm a new Supreme Court justice, it may not have another chance for years. That is likely to ramp up pressure on the court’s two oldest conservatives, Justices Samuel A. Alito Jr., 76, and Clarence Thomas, 78, to retire during the lame-duck period, while Republicans still have Senate votes to confirm a nominee; the new Democratic-majority Senate is seated Jan. 3. The conservative 6-3 supermajority has delivered historic opinions on abortion, affirmative action and religious rights (Jouvenal, Nov. 5). Via the roundup; full text paywalled.

Why it matters: The retirement-pressure story is the wave's longest shadow: Alito at 76 and Thomas at 78, a lame-duck Senate, and a Jan. 3 deadline. If either retires, the 6-3 supermajority gets a generation younger; if not, it may age into Democratic appointments.

British healthcare worker with Ebola evacuated from DR Congo to the Royal Free — the first UK national infected in the current outbreak; public risk ’remains low’

BBC News

The BBC’s domestic front, corroborated by Reuters and AP: a British healthcare worker who tested positive for Ebola in the Democratic Republic of Congo was flown to RAF Northolt and taken to the specialist infectious diseases unit at the Royal Free Hospital in north London — the first confirmed case of a UK national contracting the virus in the current outbreak, and the third person to test positive and return to Europe. The evacuation Thursday was arranged by the British and Congolese governments and international health partners; the U.K. government said the risk to the public remains low. The wider outbreak has reached 10,699 cases including 5,202 deaths, per CDC figures cited by CIDRAP. Card links Reuters’ version; the BBC’s own URL was not returned.

Why it matters: An Ebola evacuation is a logistics story as much as a health one: RAF Northolt to the Royal Free's specialist unit, third returnee to Europe, outbreak at 10,699 cases. 'Risk remains low' is the official line, printed as such.

Hong Kong activist Joshua Wong sentenced to 10 years for foreign collusion — Nikkei Asia’s account (Kenji Kawase): a 13-year starting point, reduced for the September guilty plea

Nikkei Asia

Nikkei Asia’s report, via a mirror of the piece: Hong Kong’s High Court sentenced Joshua Wong, 30, to 10 years on Thursday for conspiring to collude with foreign forces under the Beijing-imposed national security law. Nikkei Asia reported the court set a starting point of 13 years before reducing the term for Wong’s September guilty plea. Wong conspired with exiled activist Nathan Law and others in 2020 to seek sanctions or blockades against Hong Kong and China; he had been due for release in January after a 4-year-8-month subversion sentence, and has been in custody since November 2020. Friday’s follow-up — ten governments, including the U.S. and Britain, jointly condemned the sentencing (Reuters) — is in the wires. Nikkei Asia’s own page is paywalled to the desk; card links a mirror.

Why it matters: Nikkei's Kawase gives the sentence its legal skeleton -- a 13-year starting point, a plea reduction, a January release that will now never happen. Ten governments condemned it Friday; the wire follow-up is noted on the card.
