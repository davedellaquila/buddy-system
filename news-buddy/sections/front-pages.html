CFOs and businesses grapple with midterm uncertainty; Apple beats expectations — the Journal’s CFO Journal lead on Election morning

The Wall Street Journal

The Journal’s CFO Journal, Nov. 3: ’The midterms are here, which may bring some relief to markets and finance chiefs this week, or not, depending on the results.’ Its earnings read: Apple’s growth machine is wavering, but price increases carried it just ahead of expectations in the September quarter — revenue +12%, gross margin 47.9% — with CFO Kevan Parekh saying higher memory and storage costs hit as expected and were partly offset; the pressure is on John Ternus to keep the momentum going, and Apple’s high-margin services business continues to slow. Palantir’s revenue surge gets the second slot (see below).

Why it matters: The business paper's Election morning frame is CFO uncertainty, not a horse race -- plus its verdict that price increases, not volume, carried Apple's quarter.

Palantir profit soars as U.S. commercial revenue overtakes government business for the first time — revenue +96% to $2.31B, profit $1.13B

The Wall Street Journal

The Journal, Nov. 2-3: Palantir reported Q3 revenue up 96% to $2.31 billion (vs. $2.18 billion expected), as businesses turn away from AI labs to control the technology themselves. U.S. commercial revenue surged 148% to $984 million, passing U.S. government revenue ($953 million, +96%) for the first time. Profit was $1.13 billion, or 44 cents a share (adjusted 44 cents vs. 41 expected). Full-year guidance rose to $8.53-$8.534 billion, with Q4 expected at $2.649-$2.653 billion. The stock still dropped more than 7% premarket: total contract value closed was $2.9 billion across 212 deals, down from $3.4 billion and 220 deals in Q2 — the sustainability question, not the quarter, is the debate.

Why it matters: Commercial passing government for the first time is Palantir's structural story; the stock's -7% premarket on slower contract value is the valuation story. Both are in the Journal's numbers.
