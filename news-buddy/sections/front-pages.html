The U.S. loses its measles elimination status after 26 years — 5,400+ cases, 10 deaths — and Kennedy says the U.S. will ’work to regain’ it while still recommending MMR

The New York Times

From the print front’s health lead, via the e-edition listing and the Early Edition: measles is now continually spreading in the United States after elimination in 2000, with more than 5,400 cases and the first measles deaths since the early 1990s. HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. said the U.S. will work to regain the status and added, ’I continue to recommend MMR vaccination as the most effective way to prevent measles’ — a sentence that would once have been boilerplate and is now front-page news in itself.

Why it matters: Elimination status lost after 26 years is a measurable public-health reversal -- 5,400 cases, deaths back -- and the HHS secretary's MMR sentence is news precisely because it can no longer be assumed.

Peltola’s Alaska win doesn’t end DOJ scrutiny: a grand jury, dozens of subpoenas, and the spoiler-candidate case run past her swearing-in — the Post’s national-security file

The Washington Post

From the Post’s Tuesday-night file, carried on Wednesday’s front conversation: Democrat Mary Peltola was declared the winner in Alaska — the last Senate race called, giving Democrats a 56-44 majority, the biggest flip since 1958 — but Justice Department officials say a criminal grand jury investigation continues into whether Peltola staffers or others recruited a spoiler candidate named Dan J. Sullivan to confuse voters against incumbent Dan S. Sullivan, which Peltola denies. Prosecutors have issued dozens of subpoenas and target letters; a parallel civil appeal over delaying certification has a briefing schedule running to Jan. 11, past her expected swearing-in.

Why it matters: A called race that is not over: a grand jury and a certification appeal running past the swearing-in. Alaska's spoiler-candidate case is now the midterms' longest legal tail.
