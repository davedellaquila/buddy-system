Bomb threats cause diverted flights and emergency landings across Europe — the Journal’s aviation lead: more than a dozen flights Thursday, an Aer Lingus diversion to a French military base, a Finnair search

The Wall Street Journal

The Journal: a string of bomb threats roiled passenger flights across Europe in a suspected hoax campaign that grounded aircraft, led to diversions and prompted the scrambling of jet fighters — a second day of disruption after Tuesday’s spate involving at least seven flights. Two Thursday diversions were triggered by an email to Rome’s primary airport purporting to be from a fringe ultranationalist Russian group: an Aer Lingus Dublin-Rome flight diverted to a military base in northern France, and a named Finnair flight landed and was searched; no devices were found. Why it matters: The aviation mechanics matter: a named flight in the air triggers mandatory diversion and search, which is why hoaxes scale so cheaply. The Rome email's fringe-group signature is a clue or a plant -- authorities aren't saying.

Pope Leo visits a Latin America that’s losing its religion — the Journal in Montevideo: the no-religion share up from 4% in 1995 to 19% in 2024, Argentina and Peru next

The Wall Street Journal

The Journal, from Montevideo: Pope Leo XIV kicked off his first Latin American tour in Uruguay, the region’s most secular country, three weeks after lung-cancer surgery, with the Vatican describing him in ’pretty good health’ in other coverage. The challenge is regional: Latinobarometro polling puts Latin Americans with no religion at 19% in 2024, up from 4% in 1995, across a region of some 670 million. In his first address at Independence Square, Leo argued church-state separation does not mean faith should be ’confined to the private sphere.’ He heads to Argentina Sunday, then Peru, where the Chicago-born pope served as a missionary priest and bishop for two decades and holds citizenship.

Why it matters: A pope touring a continent leaving the church: 4% to 19% no-religion in a generation is the Journal's chart, and Uruguay-first is a deliberate hardest-room-first itinerary. Peru, where Leo holds citizenship, is the homecoming leg.
