What is at stake at the EU-UK summit in Brussels? — the Guardian’s explainer fronts Friday’s reset summit

The Guardian

The Guardian’s summit-eve explainer, fronting its Friday coverage: three linked deals on the table in Brussels — a food and drink (SPS) agreement removing most Brexit checks with the UK aligning to EU rules (government estimate: up to GBP 5.1 billion a year, not expected to bite until 2028), a link between the UK and EU emissions trading systems (up to GBP 3.8 billion, shielding about GBP 6.5 billion of exports from the EU carbon border tax), and a youth scheme for 18-to-31-year-olds to live and work on either side for up to three years, possibly not before 2030. The EU links all three: none takes effect without the others, and the price of access is alignment — including dropping the planned foie gras import ban. Burnham said he could still walk away; by Friday, Downing Street said a reset deal was agreed, with timelines for all three tracks.

Why it matters: Alignment is the price and 2028/2030 are the dates: the reset's economics (GBP 5.1B food, GBP 3.8B carbon) are real but slow, which is precisely why Brussels links all three deals -- leverage against backsliding.
