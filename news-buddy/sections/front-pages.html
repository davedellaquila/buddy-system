The Front Pages

Sunday's fronts: the Journal leads the ground stop's third day and its Huang espionage investigation; the Times (Saturday page, via listing) splashes the stranded thousands and reports Russia's demand for a NATO rollback to 1997; the Post fronts the Electoral College compact, the Rubio memo behind Huang's fall, and Vance's 2028 viability; the BBC and the Guardian's Observer both lead the Donbas-for-NATO float; Nikkei Asia, via flagged relays, looks to the Shenzhen trilateral and Tokyo's best week since June. Ground Stop Ripples Into Third Day of Scrapped Flights

The Wall Street Journal

Travel disruption stretched into a third day Sunday as the effects of a more-than-four-hour ground stop reverberated: 9,000-plus flights canceled Friday, 6,600 Saturday, another 1,300 by Sunday morning. The FAA traced the issue to a newly installed router at an Arkansas site overloading the network.

Why it matters: The Journal's Sunday version of its own lead, with the cancellation ledger (9,000 / 6,600 / 1,300) that turns Friday's chaos into an accountability count.

Behind the Nvidia Affair Sits a Vast Chinese Spying Machine

The Wall Street Journal

The Journal's lead investigation: behind Jensen Huang's fall sits a vast Chinese spying operation targeting Western tech and AI — the network U.S. officials say ensnared the now-former Nvidia CEO at the center of the AI boom.

Why it matters: The espionage frame, from the paper that broke the Huang story: how a suspected asset reached the center of the AI buildout is the question Congress asks before Thanksgiving.

In Jensen Huang's Ouster, Tech Billionaires See a Mistake — and a Lesson

The Wall Street Journal

Musk, Luckey, Kalanick and Gebbia defend Huang after his Thursday ouster. Musk said he would sign a reinstatement petition; Epic's Tim Sweeney called it 'Apple firing Steve Jobs level of idiocy.' Huang's stake is roughly $140 billion, about 3.6% of Nvidia.

Why it matters: The founder-control precedent, still fronting Sunday: billionaires petitioning for a fallen CEO is now a governance constituency boards must price.

Thousands Remain Stranded After Nearly 5-Hour Stoppage

The New York Times

The Times' splash: U.S. air traffic stayed snarled after the ground stop ended Friday night, and it could take days to recover. Officials blamed an aging air-traffic control system. (Saturday front page, via the Intellicurious listing; Sunday's page had not been captured when the desk swept.)

Why it matters: The Times' splash, captured from Saturday's front page via listing — the recovery-curve story ('days to recover') is the traveler's practical read.

Trump Kept Out of Sight as F.A.A. Issued Flight Ground Stop

The New York Times (via Political Wire)

With flights grounded nearly five hours Friday, Americans heard from DHS Secretary Mullin and Transportation Secretary Duffy — not President Trump, who posted on other topics. The halt grounded or delayed roughly 10,000 flights, the first major crisis since the midterms.

Why it matters: Presence as news: who briefed the country during a five-hour halt is the Times' accountability angle, and it names the midterm context plainly.

Russia Demands NATO Pullback to 1997 Positions, the Times Reports

The New York Times (via Aju Press)

A Russian document dated Nov. 10, circulated in the Abu Dhabi talks and obtained by the Times, revives the December 2021 demands: NATO withdraws to its 1997 positions and halts enlargement and military activity in Eastern Europe, the South Caucasus and Central Asia. Two sources confirmed the document's authenticity in the relays; treat the detail as single-sourced until corroborated.

Why it matters: The maximalist demand, documented: a dated Russian non-paper reviving 1997 rollback is the Abu Dhabi talks' real text. Single-sourced via relays — flagged, and the confirmation watch is the follow-up.

Movement to Sidestep the Electoral College Gains Ground After Democratic State Wins

The Washington Post (via relay)

The Post fronts the popular-vote compact's new momentum: 222 electoral votes committed, with five newly Democratic-controlled states able to carry it past 270 for 2028 — if legislatures act and courts allow.

Why it matters: The Post fronts the compact story that also makes Top Stories — filed here because the front page is the record of what Washington's paper chose to lead with.

Rubio 'Set in Motion' Jensen Huang's Fall, the Post Reports

The Washington Post (via relay)

The Post reported Nov. 13 that Secretary of State Marco Rubio recast a CIA report 'in his own more assertive terms' in a memo warning Trump about Huang's relationship with Alice Huang, a suspected Chinese asset. The CIA is reportedly 'not yet convinced' she knowingly passed information; the memo's leak set off the chain that ended in Huang's resignation.

Why it matters: The Huang file's Washington layer: a recast intelligence memo as the trigger. The CIA's reported skepticism is the sentence to hold onto — attribution, not verdict.

JD Vance Still Likely to Run for President

The Washington Post (via Political Wire)

'Vice President JD Vance isn't out of the 2028 presidential race — at least not yet,' the Post reports. Detractors wrote his obituary after the Republicans' midterm drubbing, but several close allies say he is still more likely to run than not.

Why it matters: 2028 positioning, on the record via allies: Vance's viability after a midterm drubbing is the Republican field's first sorting question.

US Has Floated Ukraine Ceding Donbas for NATO Membership, Zelensky Says

BBC News (via relay)

The BBC's lead: U.S. negotiators raised the Donbas-for-NATO trade directly, Zelensky said Saturday — the first time it had been raised. A U.S. official told the BBC 'many ideas continue to be discussed.'

Why it matters: The BBC leads with the same trade CNN carries in Top Stories — the transatlantic echo confirms this is the weekend's diplomacy story, not a single outlet's kite.

Three Men Sentenced to Death in Jordan Over Fatal Church Mass Shooting

BBC News (via relay)

Three men were sentenced to death in Jordan over last month's attack on a funeral at a Greek Orthodox church in the Karak area, which killed nine people and injured 34.

Why it matters: A death sentence is a finality most wire briefs bury: nine dead at a funeral, three condemned. The BBC's second slot is a reminder of the region's sectarian fault lines.

Ukraine Offered Stark Choice: Donbas for NATO Membership

The Guardian / The Observer

The Observer — the Guardian's Sunday sister paper — leads with the same stark trade: surrender the rest of the eastern Donbas for NATO membership. Ukraine has repeatedly rejected giving up territory; Zelensky would put an initiative carrying guarantees to a referendum.

Why it matters: The Observer is the Guardian's Sunday voice; its lead matches the BBC's, which is itself the datum — the Donbas-for-NATO float led two London fronts.

Democrat Wins Alaska Governor's Seat for First Time in Nearly 30 Years

The Guardian (via relay)

Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins' victory means Democrats will hold 30 U.S. governorships after the midterm blowout. The former state legislator defeated three Republicans, including the Trump-backed candidate — the first Democrat elected Alaska governor since 1998, called by AP ten days after polls closed with more than 50% of first-choice votes.

Why it matters: The Guardian's second slot is Saturday's Alaska call — Democrats at 30 governorships is the midterm map's durable number.

Trump, Xi and Putin Trilateral Summit Expected at APEC Shenzhen

Nikkei Asia (via relay)

Trump said Nov. 8 he expects to meet Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen Nov. 18–19, with denuclearization and the Iran war named as topics, per Nikkei Asia reporting relayed via Reuters copy. Confirmation status conflicts across relays — the White House reportedly announced a Nov. 19 meeting on Nov. 13; Beijing and Moscow have not all formally confirmed.

Why it matters: A three-leader format in Shenzhen would eclipse the closed Vance channel diplomacy of recent weeks. Confirmation is genuinely conflicting across relays — the desk prints the conflict, not a verdict.

Nikkei Surges 6.46% in Best Week Since June as Chip Stocks Lead Rebound

Nikkei Asia (via relay)

The Nikkei closed Friday at 61,142.35, up 0.53% on the day and 6.46% on the week — its strongest since the week ended June 15; the Topix gained 6.3% on the week, with Advantest leading Friday. Ahead: Japan Q3 GDP Monday, Nvidia earnings Nov. 17, Anthropic's Nasdaq listing Nov. 19.

Why it matters: Tokyo's tape as the AI trade's other mirror: +6.46% on the week into a week containing both Nvidia earnings and Anthropic's listing.
