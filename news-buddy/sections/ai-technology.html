Reuters stacks the Anthropic IPO against the record books: up to $64.4 billion raised at $78, a valuation up to $1.4 trillion — second only to SpaceX’s $75 billion June offering in U.S. IPO history

Reuters

Reuters’ Nov. 12 comparison piece is the roadshow’s day-three document: 825 million shares at as much as $78, plus the $26 billion cornerstone placement (Google, Nvidia, AMD named in the wire’s summary), against SpaceX’s record $75 billion raise in June. The desk’s running figures hold: pricing expected Nov. 18, first trading Nov. 19 as ANT, about 20% allocated to retail — the Australian retail offer closes Nov. 17. What changed overnight is in Top Stories: the filing’s own research-threshold determination, which is the prospectus story underneath the price story.

Why it matters: Scale context for Nov. 18 pricing: only SpaceX's $75 billion raise is bigger. The comparison charts are the roadshow's quiet argument that $1.4 trillion is appetite, not froth -- Dave can judge it against the filing's own numbers.

Nebius beats, raises, and falls anyway: Q3 revenue $981 million (+571%, vs $907.5 million expected), adjusted EBITDA $466 million — but 2026 guided to the low end, capex lifted to $27 billion, shares down about 10%

Reuters

Thursday before the open, via Reuters: the second neocloud exam of the week, graded the opposite way from CoreWeave’s. Revenue topped LSEG consensus by 8%, AI Cloud revenue rose 643% to $973.8 million (99% of the group), adjusted net income turned positive at $90.9 million, and the GAAP loss narrowed to $37.5 million (-$0.13). Then the guidance: full-year 2026 revenue at the low end of $3.0-3.4 billion (consensus $3.37 billion), the $7 billion ARR milestone pushed from December to February, 2026 capex raised to about $27 billion, and a first 2027 outlook of $12-13 billion revenue against $40-45 billion of capex. After a 15% Wednesday ride on CoreWeave’s coattails, the stock gave back about 10%. Demand proven, again; the market is now pricing the spending, not the growth.

Why it matters: The neocloud pair is now a controlled experiment: CoreWeave beat and soared, Nebius beat and fell on guidance and capex. The market's question moved from 'is demand real' to 'what does growth cost' in 24 hours.

The succession market opens before the board has spoken: prediction markets price Ian Buck at 35%, CFO Colette Kress at 30%, Arm’s Rene Haas at 8% — Polymarket puts Huang gone by year-end at 77%

The Wall Street Journal (live file)

From the Journal’s live file: with no succession plan by the founder’s own design, the betting markets are doing the board’s shortlisting in public — Buck (hyperscale/HPC engineering), Kress (a decade-plus as CFO), Haas (an outsider who spent seven years inside). Analysts float Lisa Su, Eric Schmidt and Jonathan Ross; Baird, downgrading the stock, favors an outside hire and says the odds of a transition into 2027 are rising. All of it prices a decision the company has not announced, five days before a Nov. 17 earnings call that now has no certain host. Flagged as what it is: market odds, not reporting.

Why it matters: No succession plan means a betting market is the only published shortlist -- Buck's engineering depth versus Kress's continuity is the real choice, whatever the odds. Treat the percentages as mood, not information.

OpenAI posts its first $10 billion quarter: revenue above $10 billion for the first time, per Wednesday’s market reports — the demand datum underneath the whole AI rally

Barchart via TradingView

Via Barchart’s closing report: chipmakers and AI-infrastructure names rallied Wednesday in part on OpenAI’s first $10 billion revenue quarter — the top-line evidence the buildout’s customers can pay for the buildout. Set it beside the week’s other two demand prints (CoreWeave’s $132.5 billion backlog, Nebius’s +571%) and the Anthropic prospectus ($16.96 billion in Q3) and the AI trade’s bull case is currently a revenue argument, not a promise. Flagged thin: a closing-report relay of the figure; OpenAI’s own disclosure is the document to check.

Why it matters: A $10 billion quarter from the buildout's biggest customer is the demand evidence under CoreWeave's backlog and Anthropic's revenue alike. Private-company figure, relayed -- but if it holds, the AI trade's bull case is arithmetic.
