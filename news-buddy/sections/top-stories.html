Nvidia passes the exam, loudly: $115.8 billion revenue (consensus $108.7-109 billion), data center $107.6 billion, EPS $2.68 — and a $130 billion Q4 guide, with zero China data-center compute assumed

Pandromeda / Nvidia 8-K

Tuesday after the close, fiscal Q3 ended Oct. 25: revenue $115.844 billion, up 20% sequentially and 103% year over year, beating the company’s own $108 billion guide by $7.8 billion; data-center revenue $107.6 billion, up 110%; GAAP EPS $2.70, non-GAAP $2.68 against $2.47 consensus; gross margin 74.8-74.9%. Q4 guidance is $130.0 billion plus or minus 3% — well above the roughly $125 billion consensus — though the quarter carries an extra week and margin is guided down to 71-72% as the Vera Rubin ramp raises costs. Interim CEO Colette Kress raised the fiscal 2028 growth outlook to roughly 75% from about 70%. Two honest caveats travel with the beat: spread over 14 weeks, the guide implies weekly revenue up only about 4%, and the outlook assumes no data-center compute revenue from China after $2.4 billion of licensed Hopper sales were halted in Q3. Shares rose about 2-3% after hours. The leadership story runs beside the numbers in The Front Pages: the board will not reinstate Huang, and the Journal reports an SEC probe into disclosures.

Why it matters: The exam is graded: a $7.8B beat on the company's own guide and a $130B guide against $125B expected. The two caveats -- the extra week and the margin trough -- are printed with it, because Thursday's Anthropic pricing will trade on exactly those slopes.

Xi breaks protocol to greet Trump on the Shenzhen tarmac — a handshake that became a hug — as Putin, who got no airport greeting, waits for Thursday’s first-ever trilateral

Associated Press via Classical WMHT

The Associated Press via WMHT, Shenzhen, Wednesday: President Trump landed in China seeking to reinforce U.S. technology leadership, and Xi Jinping did something he has not done for a visiting U.S. president — he met the plane himself, reciprocating Trump’s Joint Base Andrews welcome in September. Putin, who arrived earlier Wednesday, was met by Cai Qi, the fifth-ranking party official; Xi called him an ’old friend’ in two hours of talks and described the Russia relationship as ’as stable as a mountain,’ but the tarmac honour went to Trump alone. At the APEC CEO sessions Xi pledged to stand against trade protectionism and Putin called Western powers ’former hegemons’ whose tools of dominance ’have largely run their course.’ Thursday brings a bilateral Trump-Xi on trade, then the first face-to-face meeting of the three leaders — nuclear limits, Ukraine and the Iran war on the table, with a senior U.S. official having already said no agreement is expected. A distressing run of U.S. tech outages left the administration projecting calm it does not entirely feel; that file is in Engineering.

Why it matters: Protocol is the message: Xi does not meet planes, and he met this one, while Putin got a party official. Thursday's trilateral starts from that choreography -- and from the intelligence read, on the NYT front, that Putin intends to fight through winter.

Iran strikes Kuwait and Bahrain after the U.S. hits Qeshm Island — Houthis claim five drones at Saudi pipeline pumping stations as the Gulf war widens again

Wall Street Journal / Reuters via Just Security

Via Just Security’s Early Edition, Wednesday: Iran early today launched strikes against Kuwait and Bahrain, saying it targeted Kuwait’s Ali Al Salem Air Base, which hosts U.S. forces, after the United States struck Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz (Gambrell for the Journal; Alashray and Abdallah for Reuters). Yemen’s Houthis said they launched five drones toward two pumping stations on Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline near Riyadh. Separately, Trump’s Board of Peace accused both Israel and Hamas of failing to meet their ceasefire commitments, in its second report to the U.N. Security Council. The exchange matters to Thursday’s room in Shenzhen: the Iran war is one of the three files the trilateral is supposed to touch, and it escalated the night before the leaders sit down.

Why it matters: The Gulf war widened the night before Shenzhen: a U.S.-hosting base in Kuwait targeted, Saudi pipeline stations droned. The trilateral's Iran file is no longer theoretical agenda -- it is last night's strikes.
