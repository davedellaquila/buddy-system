Election Day: polls open nationwide with Democrats favored in both chambers — 91% for the House, about 69% for the Senate — and 58 million ballots already cast

Frothier

Frothier’s Election Day tracker, updated Nov. 3 at 3:42 a.m. PT: its 20,000-simulation model projects Democrats taking the Senate 52-48 (69% chance) and the House 238-197 (91% chance), on a generic ballot of Democrats 51.3% to Republicans 42.9% across 183 polls. President Trump’s approval averages 38.4% approve to 59.3% disapprove; the final Ipsos poll (Nov. 2) had him at 36%. Republicans currently hold the Senate 53-47 and a narrow House majority; Democrats need a net four Senate seats and three House seats. First polls close at 7:30 p.m. ET; California closes at 11 p.m. ET. Forecasts are not results — the Senate turns on a handful of state races, not the national numbers.

Why it matters: Today decides Congress for Trump's final two years. The model numbers (House 91%, Senate ~69%), the 58M early ballots, and a 38.4% approval average are the frame; the Senate still turns on a handful of state races, not the national lead.

Supreme Court rejects Trump’s bid to set aside 20,000 California ballots — no noted dissent, hours before Election Day

USA Today

USA Today, Nov. 2: the Supreme Court declined the Justice Department’s emergency request to force California to segregate mail ballots from about 20,000 registered voters the administration alleges may not be citizens, with none of the justices publicly dissenting. District Judge David Carter had called it unprecedented for the federal government, on an election’s eve, to coerce a state to abandon its own procedures; the 9th Circuit (2-1) said forcing use of the federal list could bar valid votes and jeopardize Los Angeles County’s election. A UCLA Voting Rights Project analysis found about 42% of the names on the federal list did not match anyone in California’s voter file. The underlying lawsuit continues; today’s ballots are counted under California’s existing procedures.

Why it matters: Directly California: 20,000 voters' ballots count today under state procedures, with no segregation. The UCLA finding that ~42% of the federal list matched nobody in the voter file is why two lower courts and now the Supreme Court said no.

Apple beats in Ternus’s first report — $114.48B revenue, $2.10 EPS, record iPhone sales — but a softer holiday margin guide sends the stock down about 3%

MacRumors

MacRumors’ takeaways from the Nov. 2 call, John Ternus’s first as CEO (he succeeded Tim Cook Sept. 1): fiscal Q4 revenue $114.48 billion (+12%) beat the $113.4 billion consensus, EPS $2.10 beat $2.00, and gross margin of 47.9% edged the 47.5% estimate — with a 5-cent EPS lift from tariff refunds. iPhone revenue was $57.58 billion (vs. $56.5 billion expected), Mac $10.79 billion (vs. $9.61 billion) and Services a record $31.58 billion; iPad ($6.23 billion vs. $6.83 billion) and Greater China ($17.54 billion vs. $18.75 billion) missed. The after-hours slide came from the December-quarter margin guide of 45-46%, below the 46.3% consensus, as memory costs rise; Ternus told Reuters Apple cannot meet iPhone Duo demand, with no balance date given.

Why it matters: Your upvoted Apple beat, results edition: the beat was real (iPhone, Mac, Services records) and so is the problem -- memory costs pushing holiday margins to 45-46%. Ternus's Duo supply line ('cannot meet demand') is the holiday quarter's open question.
