House shelves the Hegseth impeachment, 211-204: Massie’s eight articles go to the Judiciary Committee — no floor vote, no debate, and the effort expires with this Congress on Jan. 3

NPR / House Clerk

Tuesday evening’s only recorded vote on the question, Roll Call 317: Rep. Darrell Issa’s motion to refer carried 211-204 shortly after 6 p.m. Voting yes were 210 Republicans and independent Kevin Kiley; voting no were 203 Democrats and Massie himself — the Kentucky Republican who wrote the 34-page privileged resolution and forced it to the floor Monday. Eighteen members did not vote, 11 of them Democrats; NPR’s read is that attendance decided it — enough Democrats were in Washington to block referral, but not enough were on the floor. The articles (waging the Iran war past 90 days without authorization, the Venezuela/Yemen/Caribbean operations, civilian-casualty law, the Kelly retaliation charge) now sit with the Republican-led Judiciary Committee, which is not expected to advance them. Reason’s Volokh blog and the wires agree on the sequel: Democrats take both chambers Jan. 3 with roughly 260 House seats and can revive the case then; Senate conviction would still need 67 votes. Trump welcomed the outcome, calling Hegseth a ’great American patriot.’ Hegseth, at a Tuesday Pentagon briefing, called the charges political. Dave’s CPI-calendar and war-ledger threads both run through this vote.

Why it matters: The impeachment is shelved, not settled: an attendance game decided it (11 absent Democrats, a 7-vote margin), and the incoming majority can refloat the same articles on Jan. 3. Hegseth governs on; the war's congressional ledger stays open.

CPI comes in hot: October +0.4% headline, core +0.3% for a third straight month — both above consensus, the 2-year jumps 19bp to 4.75%, and a December hike is priced near 90%

BLS / CME FedWatch

The print the calendar pointed at, arriving hotter than hoped: headline CPI rose 0.4% in October (consensus +0.3%, September +0.6%), core rose 0.3% (consensus +0.2%) for a third consecutive month, and — as the Journal flagged Tuesday — BLS published no 12-month rates, because October 2025 was never collected during last fall’s shutdown. Gov. Waller said Tuesday he backs a quarter-point December hike, with a half-point possible if inflation runs hot; CME FedWatch moved to about 84.7% on a hike to 4.00-4.25% plus 15.3% on a larger move — roughly 90% for a hike of some size in the morning briefs, with Goldman now floating a further 25bp in March. Tuesday’s tape: S&P 500 7,520.93 (-0.38%), Dow 52,231.89 (-0.30%, -157.86), Nasdaq 25,065.88 (-0.74%, -187.26), Nvidia -5.47% on the day, AAPL $348.51 (-0.62%). The FOMC meets Dec. 8-9 — a day before November CPI lands — so October’s print is the last one the committee sees. PPI follows Friday. Dave upvoted the CPI-calendar card in October; this is the second print in that sequence.

Why it matters: Dave upvoted the CPI-calendar thread; this print is why it mattered. A hot core with no annual rate means the Fed hikes in December on monthly data alone -- and AAPL, rates and the AI trade all reprice off the same 8:30 a.m. number.

Russia and Ukraine resume U.S.-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi — first trilateral meeting in nine months, no breakthrough Tuesday, discussions continue today

Reuters / PBS via 10 Things

Via the 10 Things global brief (sourcing PBS, Reuters and the Post): Russian and Ukrainian delegations returned to U.S.-mediated negotiations in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday — their first direct three-way meeting since February — and agreed to continue Wednesday. The Post reported Russia’s delegation expanded to include senior diplomatic and military figures, while Ukraine sent senior security and intelligence officials. Humanitarian measures, security guarantees and de-escalation steps are the immediate agenda; the Tuesday session ran nearly four hours without a breakthrough, per AP. The talks run in parallel with the wider European-security frame the Post reported Tuesday — and with an Iran file (below, in The Front Pages) that is crowding the same diplomatic week.

Why it matters: The first trilateral table in nine months is movement even without a breakthrough: expanded Russian delegation, security officials on the Ukrainian side, and a Wednesday continuation. Watch whether humanitarian measures -- the easiest first step -- actually get signed.
