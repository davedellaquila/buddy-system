Product Design

Scale at both ends: SOM-scale institution-building in Sydney, where Moreau Kusunoki and Genton's lattice-exoskeleton Powerhouse Parramatta opens tomorrow as Australia's largest cultural project since the Opera House; 4Space's Falwa in Abu Dhabi, translating one famous horse's skeleton into plaster and tack-stitched chairs; and the small end winning the votes -- Dado's single-joint, tool-free flat-pack takes Dezeen readers' furniture design of the year, while 36 Danish cabinetmakers show at Ordrupgaard through Dec. 13.

Powerhouse Parramatta revealed ahead of tomorrow’s opening — Moreau Kusunoki and Genton’s lattice-wrapped museum, Australia’s largest cultural project since the Opera House

Dezeen

Dezeen, Nov. 6: Powerhouse Parramatta, by Paris-based Moreau Kusunoki with Australian firm Genton, opens tomorrow, Saturday Nov. 7, on the Parramatta River in western Sydney — described as Australia’s largest cultural project since the Sydney Opera House. The 30,000-square-metre museum is split into two blocky forms joined at the upper levels and wrapped in a steel lattice exoskeleton that is both facade and structure, creating column-free spaces. Five international exhibitions open with it, including Task Eternal on humanity’s journey into space, designed by Open Architecture, and The Mall, on shopping-centre culture. A three-week opening festival runs 179 events showing 3,000-plus objects, 1,600-plus from the Powerhouse collection. The building is part of a $1.3 billion renewal of Australia’s largest museum group; the heritage Powerhouse Ultimo renewal runs to late 2028.

Why it matters: A museum whose structure is its facade: the lattice exoskeleton makes column-free halls and gives western Sydney its Opera House-scale claim. It opens tomorrow -- expect the photography to be everywhere this weekend.

4Space draws on Arabian horses for the Falwa restaurant in Abu Dhabi — skeletal curves in dusty-white plaster, tack-stitched chairs, inside the Royal Equestrian Arts academy

Dezeen

Dezeen, Nov. 6: Dubai studio 4Space has completed Falwa, a contemporary Emirati restaurant inside the Abu Dhabi Royal Equestrian Arts (ADREA) academy, led by Michelin-starred Emirati chef Faisal Alharmoodi. Named for the Arabic term for a young female mare in her first year, the interior translates the Arabian horse’s skeletal structure, muscular tension and fluid movement into stretched, curved walls in a dusty-white mineral plaster inspired by Rabdan, the late Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan’s horse. Custom dining chairs carry leather straps, visible stitching and metal hardware referencing tack, and lighting throughout is Stefan Diez’s Vibia Plusminus system. 4Space says the project expresses ’how a deep cultural relationship can be expressed as a living, modern architectural language.’ It opened in 2026 — the Year of the Horse.

Why it matters: Biomimicry with a specific horse: Falwa's curves come from Rabdan's skeleton, not a mood board, and the tack-stitched chairs carry the argument at hand height. Cultural reference as construction detail, not theme.

Dado flat-pack furniture — held together by a single traditional joint, no tools — wins Dezeen readers’ furniture design of the year

Dezeen

Dezeen, Nov. 6: Berlin studio Allen Kaufmann Architekten has adapted a traditional woodworking joint to create Dado, a flat-pack furniture range that assembles completely without tools, with nothing but that single joint holding each piece together. Dezeen readers voted Dado furniture design of the year in this year’s Dezeen Awards public vote. It now goes before the Awards jury — including Norman Foster, Michael Anastassiades and Jaime Hayon — for the overall design project of the year title, against projects from Tom Fereday, Yabu Pushelberg, String Furniture and others. The counterpoint to the weekend’s big institutional openings in Sydney and Doha: the reader-chosen winner is small, demountable, tool-free joinery rather than a landmark building.

Why it matters: One joint, no tools, reader-voted design of the year: Dado is the anti-landmark -- flat-pack as joinery craft. Dave builds things; this is the design story most likely to end up as a workshop reference.

Cabinetmakers’ Autumn Exhibition: 36 Danish designers at Museum Ordrupgaard through Dec. 13 — the craft pole of a design week owned by mega-projects

Dezeen

Dezeen, Nov. 6: the Cabinetmakers’ Autumn Exhibition brings 36 designers to Museum Ordrupgaard in Denmark, running through Dec. 13 — the annual showcase of the Danish cabinetmaking tradition, where the object is the argument: joinery, timber and finish at furniture scale. It lands the same morning as Sydney’s $1.3 billion museum opening and the Doha and Dubai design weeks, and reads as the deliberate opposite pole: no exoskeleton, no masterplan, just 36 makers’ current work in one museum. Also on Dezeen’s Nov. 6 file: Collective Architecture’s Paisley Central Library, shortlisted for the RIAS Doolan Award (winner announced Nov. 26). Details beyond the listing were light in this morning’s coverage, but the exhibition’s dates and venue are the venue’s own.

Why it matters: 36 makers, one museum, no masterplan: the Cabinetmakers' Exhibition is the craft baseline against which Doha, Dubai and Sydney's billions get measured. Runs to Dec. 13 if anyone's Denmark-bound.
