London Tropical: OEB Architects channels 1980s interiors and tropical modernism for a Southwark terrace extension wrapped around its garden

Dezeen

Dezeen, Nov. 4: glossy ceramic tiles, a glass-block wall and a leafy garden evoke tropical modernism and 1980s interiors in OEB Architects’ rear extension to a three-storey Victorian terrace in Southwark — an open-plan kitchen and dining space designed to feel ’surrounded’ by its garden. Project architect Gabriel George told Dezeen the material and spatial ideas grew from conversations with the clients through precedents: mid-century West Coast case study houses, 80s interiors, tropical modernism. OEB was founded in 2018 by Tessa Baird and David Eland, with offices in London and Brighton.

Why it matters: 1980s gloss and glass block against a Victorian terrace, composed around a garden: the extension as mood, sourced from case-study houses by way of client conversation.

SOM unveils its Cunningham Pier transformation: a 34-storey trefoil tower, 280 apartments, a 200-room hotel and 12 villas with yacht berths over Corio Bay

Dezeen

Dezeen, Nov. 4: SOM has won the competition to transform Geelong’s historic Cunningham Pier — built in 1855 to give railways access to docking ships — into a 10,000-square-metre waterfront destination for Gurner Group and Costa Property Group. A 34-storey tower at the pier’s end, trefoil in plan like three combined cylinders tapering top and bottom, will surpass the 21-storey Miramar as Geelong’s tallest. Along the pier, rounded buildings with layered terraces reference the movement of water; the program adds public space, ~280 apartments, a 200-room hotel, shops, restaurants and 12 waterfront villas with individual yacht berths.

Why it matters: A pier from 1855 becomes a trefoil tower and yacht-berth villas: waterfront luxury at infrastructure scale, and Geelong's new tallest building as a byproduct.

’Unrefined’ materials define Jeju: &Daughters builds a Vancouver Korean restaurant around a reinterpreted chogajip — basalt, cork, thatch, raw concrete

Dezeen

Dezeen, Nov. 4: Canadian studio &Daughters designed Jeju, a 44-seat Korean restaurant in Vancouver, starting from the menu — modern dishes by chef Dylan Kim, traditional dishes by his mother, chef Myungseok ’Julie’ Suh, and dishes they created together. At the heart sits a reinterpretation of the chogajip, the traditional thatched house of Jeju Island. ’The palette is quite simple — basalt, cork, raw concrete masonry units, polished concrete, wood and thatch,’ principal Emma Sims told Dezeen. Tactility is the concept: direct, unrefined materials carrying both generations of the cooking.

Why it matters: The menu as brief -- two generations of chefs, one thatched-house idea, six unrefined materials. Restaurant design where the concept is literally the family recipe.

Alter returns to Tokyo with an AI-aided tea ceremony with no host, cedar stools made from every part of the log, and 75 young studios re-examining product design

Dezeen

Dezeen, Nov. 4: the second edition of Alter, the young designers’ exhibition, has opened at Nihonbashi Mitsu Hall during Designart Tokyo (Oct. 30-Nov. 8), bringing together 75 international creators, mostly in their 20s and 30s, united by a desire to ’re-examine the value of product design’ in an industry they say is growing rigid. Tariki Research Institute’s ’tea ceremony with no host’ inverts the traditional ceremony with AI; Pan Projects’ Bole, with Nara forestry partners, makes stools from Japanese cedar using every part of the log. A glowing lung-like lamp rounds out the highlights — the product-design story and the AI story sharing one hall.

Why it matters: A host-less AI tea ceremony inside a young-designers' show is the most Dave story in today's design file: craft ritual, inverted by a model, in Tokyo.
