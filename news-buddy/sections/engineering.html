Deadline day for WordPress CVE-2026-119977: CISA’s federal remediation date is today, Wordfence has blocked 240,000+ attempts, and the first try came 9 minutes after its rule went live

Moatline

Today, Nov. 6, is CISA’s federal remediation deadline under BOD 26-04 for CVE-2026-119977, CVSS 8.1, a time-of-check/time-of-use race (CWE-367) in WordPress Core 4.7 through 7.1.3: during the seconds a full core update unpacks into the predictable, web-accessible wp-content/upgrade staging folder containing the installer, an unauthenticated attacker can execute code. WordPress 7.1.4 (Oct. 28) fixes it plus 22 flaws in total, with backports to 4.7. CISA added it to the KEV catalog Nov. 2-3 on evidence of active exploitation, with forensic triage required, not patch-only. Wordfence saw its first attempt 9 minutes after its rule went live and had blocked more than 240,000 attempts by Nov. 5, peaking above 74,000 on Oct. 30. No confirmed site-takeover details have been published by CISA or Wordfence as of Nov. 5. Beyond patching: deny web access to wp-content/upgrade/. Researcher credit: Umut Bayram, Picus Security.

Why it matters: Deadline day is the story: 240,000+ blocked attempts, a first try in 9 minutes, and a BOD that demands forensic triage rather than a patch checkbox. If you run WordPress anywhere, wp-content/upgrade/ access is today's check.

Two fresh exploited flaws: OpenSSH’s ’sshole’ (CVE-2026-123158) hit honeypots within 9 hours, and 996 Zyxel GS1900 switches were compromised through a zero-day since August

Help Net Security

Help Net Security, Nov. 6: OpenSSH CVE-2026-123158, CVSS 8.1, disclosed and fixed Nov. 5 in OpenSSH 10.6p2, lets a remote attacker log into passwordless (’*’) accounts on musl-libc systems without PAM — notably Alpine Linux — by sending a password longer than 256 bytes, due to musl crypt() misinterpretation; glibc systems (Debian, Ubuntu, Fedora) and UsePAM yes are not affected. Previdian’s sensors saw a first attempt under 9 hours after publication — 8 attempts from a single Japan IP, with confirmed root access on an Alpine honeypot. Alpine shipped fixes for 3.16 through edge on Nov. 5; CISA has not added it to the KEV yet. Separately, Binarly disclosed Nov. 5 that 996 Zyxel GS1900 switches across 48 countries were compromised since about Aug. 17 via a previously unknown zero-day, CVE-2026-107441, CVSS 8.8, an out-of-bounds write in the web-management CGI allowing unauthenticated OS command execution — working even on switches patched for the earlier CVE-2026-7273. Zyxel’s Patch 3 firmware was released Oct. 22, advisory Nov. 3, covering 10 GS1900 models on firmware 2.90 Patch 2 and earlier.

Why it matters: Two clocks: OpenSSH's sshole was exploited in under 9 hours on Alpine/musl only -- check your base images, not your glibc fleet -- and 996 Zyxel switches were owned for months through a zero-day that survived earlier patching. Patch-level is not the same as safe.

Cursor can now build and host live apps: prompt to Postgres, auth and a shareable domain, gated by security checks and an explicit publish step

Frontmatter

Cursor announced Nov. 5 ’Build Live Apps’: a cloud agent scaffolds a web app from a prompt or an existing repo, provisions a Postgres database if needed, configures auth behind Cursor SSO, provisions hosting, and returns a shareable link at a user-picked domain. Publishing is gated by deterministic and agentic security checks; nothing goes live until the checks pass and the user explicitly publishes, and team admins can review, approve and unpublish. Available on all paid plans with cloud agents enabled, deploying from Cursor Origin and GitHub, with GitLab coming soon. Also Nov. 5: Microsoft Research described its Proof-Driven Development (PDD) Kit, turning plain-language intent into a formal Verus specification and proving agent-written code for every input, trialed on the login logic of a Rust ’Pet Store’ app. Both items reach the desk via changelog relays without independent hands-on testing — flagged.

Why it matters: Cursor crosses from editor to platform: prompt-to-hosted-app with Postgres and SSO auth, gated by checks and an explicit publish. The gating design is the responsible part; the desk has changelog relays only, flagged.
