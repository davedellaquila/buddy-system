Sao Paulo Friday: six rookies sit in for FP1, Camara makes his full Haas debut, and Norris warns McLaren’s upgrades ’could even make it harder’

Formula 1

Formula1.com, Nov. 6: round 20 at Interlagos runs Nov. 6-8, with FP1 Friday 7:30-8:30 a.m. PT and FP2 at 11:00 a.m. PT, on Apple TV in the U.S. Six drivers sit out FP1 under the 2026 rookie rule: Leonardo Fornaroli (Piastri’s McLaren), Dino Beganovic (Leclerc’s Ferrari), Luke Browning (Sainz’s Williams), Freddie Slater (Hulkenberg’s Audi), Gabriele Mini (Gasly’s Alpine) and Colton Herta (Perez’s Cadillac — Herta needs 100 km for his final Super Licence point). Rafael Camara, 21, instead makes his full F1 debut for Haas alongside Ollie Bearman all weekend. McLaren brings a new front wing, rear wing and floor; Lando Norris, 6th in the standings and the outgoing champion after Antonelli took the title in Mexico, called the MCL40 ’horrible, difficult to drive the whole season’ and warned the upgrades ’could even make it harder.’ The forecast: cloudy Friday practice, thundery showers Saturday, high 27C. Antonelli arrives champion on 380; Mercedes 630, Ferrari 504 in the constructors’.

Why it matters: Friday at Interlagos is three stories: the rookie-rule FP1 carousel (Herta's licence point is the sleeper), Camara's full debut, and Norris publicly doubting his own upgrades. Saturday's thunderstorms are the weekend's real variable.

Homestead tonight: the Trucks title is decided at 7:30 p.m. ET on FS1 — Honeycutt leads Riggs by 20, clinches outright with 57 points, and wins every tiebreaker from a top-3 finish

NASCAR.com

NASCAR.com: Championship Weekend returns to Homestead-Miami Speedway for the first time since 2019. Friday Nov. 6: Craftsman Truck qualifying at 2:30 p.m. ET on FS2, then the 134-lap, 201-mile championship race at 7:30 p.m. ET on FS1 and FOX One, with stages at 30, 60 and 134. Kaden Honeycutt leads Layne Riggs by 20 points; Honeycutt clinches outright with 57 points and wins every tiebreaker if he finishes top-3, while Riggs clinches outright with 75 points — if Riggs wins, he loses only if Honeycutt finishes 2nd or 3rd. Ty Majeski was fastest in Thursday practice; Honeycutt was 5th, Riggs 6th. Saturday Nov. 7, 5:00 p.m. ET on The CW: the O’Reilly Series — Justin Allgaier leads Jesse Love by 22, clinches with 54 points, five drivers within 42. Sunday Nov. 8, 3:00 p.m. ET on NBC: Cup — Kyle Larson leads Christopher Bell by 67 and Denny Hamlin by 70, needs just 9 points, and Racing Insights puts him at 99.4%.

Why it matters: Tonight decides the first of three titles: Honeycutt's math is friendly (57 points, top-3 tiebreakers), Riggs needs the win and help. Majeski's practice pace is the wrinkle. 7:30 p.m. ET on FS1 -- 4:30 Dave's time.

MotoGP Qatar FP1: Marquez fastest on match point, 1:52.278 — and Martin, passed fit on a still-broken wrist, goes P5; 18 riders, no Yamaha, 34C air and 42C track

MotoGP.com

MotoGP.com and Crash.net, Nov. 6: at Lusail on Friday, championship leader Marc Marquez (Ducati Lenovo) topped FP1 in 1:52.278, 0.110s ahead of Marco Bezzecchi (Aprilia), with Fabio Di Giannantonio 3rd and Pedro Acosta 4th despite a premature end to his session. Jorge Martin — passed fit Friday morning after breaking his right wrist at Phillip Island and withdrawing mid-weekend at Sepang — was an encouraging 5th, +0.734s, logging 16 laps; he admitted ’the bone is still broken.’ Marquez arrives on 399 points, 44 ahead of Martin and 67 ahead of Bezzecchi, and clinches his eighth premier-class title this weekend if he outscores Martin by 30 points and Bezzecchi by 8 — though Martin scoring 8 points alone would postpone it regardless. The grid is only 18 riders: Yamaha’s factory team and Pramac are withdrawn, Alex Marquez is out after shoulder surgery, and Joan Mir is absent, replaced by Iker Lecuona and Jake Dixon — Dixon crashed on lap one. The timed Practice session follows at 19:00 local, the Sprint Saturday, a 22-lap GP Sunday.

Why it matters: Match point, session one, job done: Marquez fastest, and Martin P5 on a broken wrist is the braver headline. The 8-point postponement clause means Saturday's Sprint carries the title math, not just points.

WEC closes at Monza: Toyota’s eighth manufacturers’ title is banked, but the drivers’ crown is a Toyota-versus-Toyota duel — #8 leads #7 by 17 with 26 available, and ’no team orders’

FIA WEC

FIA WEC, Nov. 6: the World Endurance Championship closes Nov. 6-8 with the 6 Hours of Monza, its first visit since 2023. Friday practice runs 10:30-12:00 and 14:30-16:00 CET, qualifying Saturday from 14:20 CET, the race Sunday at 11:00 CET. Toyota has already secured its eighth Hypercar Manufacturers’ title (212 points to BMW 154, Ferrari 130, Cadillac 118) with a one-two at Barcelona, but the Drivers’ title is an in-house duel: the #8 crew — Sebastien Buemi, Brendon Hartley, Ryo Hirakawa, 114 points — leads the #7 crew — Kamui Kobayashi, Mike Conway, Nyck de Vries, 97 points — by 17, with 26 points available (25 for the win, 1 for pole) and, per Kobayashi, ’no team orders.’ Conway retires after this race. Ferrari, 24 points behind BMW for 2nd in Manufacturers’, chases a home win. Fourteen manufacturers and 11 Italian drivers are on the grid; both LMGT3 titles are also open.

Why it matters: An in-house title fight with 'no team orders' and a retiring Conway: Monza's finale has the cleanest storyline in endurance racing this year. Toyota's manufacturers' crown is done; the drivers' is 17 points and 26 available.
