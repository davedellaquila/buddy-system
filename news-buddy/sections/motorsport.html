Motorsport

Finale weekend everywhere: F1 at a rain-threatened Interlagos (Antonelli champion at 380; Mercedes' constructors' clinch live at 630-504, with Reuters and the previews disagreeing on the exact math; Rafael Camara debuts for Haas at home after Ocon's abrupt exit; Brazil fields two drivers for the first time since 2016). Sao Paulo GP: Antonelli, champion at 380, leads the field into a wet Interlagos — Mercedes’ constructors’ clinch is live (630 to Ferrari’s 504), and Brazil gets two home drivers for the first time since 2016

Reuters

Reuters’ Nov. 4 statistics file for round 20 at Interlagos (Nov. 6-8, no Sprint; race Sunday 2 p.m. local, noon ET, 71 laps): Kimi Antonelli clinched the drivers’ title in Mexico City — the youngest champion in F1 history, the first Italian winner in 73 years, 9 wins and 380 points. The fight behind him is for second: Verstappen 261, Hamilton 259, Russell 250, Leclerc 245. Mercedes can close out the constructors’ title Sunday with its 126-point lead over Ferrari (630-504) — note the clinch math differs by source: Reuters says Mercedes must extend the lead by four points, while several previews put it at outscoring Ferrari by three; 129 points remain after Brazil either way. The weather is the weekend’s wildcard: Weather.com’s Interlagos forecast has Friday showers (74F, 40% rain), Saturday thunderstorms (82F, 80%, 0.63 in), and a wet Sunday (63F, 80%, 0.27 in, SSE winds 16 mph). With Gabriel Bortoleto (Audi) joined by a debuting countryman (next card), Brazil fields two drivers in the same Grand Prix for the first time since 2016.

Why it matters: Three storylines in one card: a champion defending pride, a constructors' title on a knife's edge (and two sources disagreeing on the clinch math -- both printed), and a rain forecast that could scramble all of it.

Haas ends the mystery: Ocon will not race for the team again in 2026, and 21-year-old Rafael Camara — F2 leader, Ferrari academy, signed for 2027 — debuts at his home race

Formula 1

Formula1.com, Nov. 4: Haas confirmed Esteban Ocon will not race for the team again in 2026, ending the unnamed-second-seat question this paper carried Monday. In his place at Interlagos: Rafael Camara, 21, a Ferrari Driver Academy member since 2021, the current Formula 2 leader, already signed full-time for Haas for 2027, who tested the 2025 car at Portimao in August. He partners Ollie Bearman in the VF-26; reserve Ryo Hirakawa, who deputised in Mexico and scored a point on debut, is unavailable with a WEC finale clash. Camara: ’There is a lot to learn... Making my Formula 1 debut in Brazil is a special moment.’ One gap the announcement leaves open: Haas named Sao Paulo only — no word on who drives Las Vegas, Qatar or Abu Dhabi.

Why it matters: The Mexico mystery resolves into a debut: Camara gets the fairy tale (home race, F2 leader, 2027 seat already his), and Ocon's Haas chapter closes without a farewell race. The un-named final three seats are the loose end.

Homestead decides everything: Larson needs 9 points to close the Cup title Sunday (3 p.m. ET, NBC), Allgaier’s five-driver O’Reilly fight runs Saturday, Honeycutt leads the Trucks into Friday night

NASCAR.com

NASCAR.com, Nov. 4: the finale returns to Homestead-Miami for the first time since 2019, with all three national series deciding titles Nov. 6-8. Cup — the Straight Talk Wireless 400, Sunday 3 p.m. ET on NBC/HBO Max, 267 laps, the last race of the 10-race Chase: defending champion Kyle Larson leads Christopher Bell by 67 points and Denny Hamlin by 70, needs just 9 points to clinch regardless (finish 28th or better, or collect 8 stage points, per the clinch scenarios), with three drivers mathematically eligible; Carson Hocevar has been suspended by Spire Motorsports for his Martinsville actions and is replaced by Justin Haley. O’Reilly Series — Saturday 5 p.m. ET on The CW, 200 laps: five Chevrolet drivers remain, Justin Allgaier (2,436) ahead of Jesse Love (-22), Corey Day (-23), Carson Kvapil (-31) and Sheldon Creed (-42); Allgaier clinches with 54 points but loses every tiebreaker. Trucks — Friday 7:30 p.m. ET on FS1: Kaden Honeycutt (2,403) leads Layne Riggs by 20.

Why it matters: Finale math, all three series, with Larson's 9-point clinch bar as the headline: Sunday is a coronation unless it isn't, which is exactly what a finale should be. Allgaier's tiebreaker problem is Saturday's drama.

MotoGP’s match point under the Lusail lights: Marquez (399) must outscore Martin by 30 and Bezzecchi by 8 this weekend to take his eighth MotoGP title — and Yamaha has withdrawn from Qatar entirely

Road Racing World

Road Racing World’s MotoGP preview (series release), Nov. 5: round 20 at Lusail, Nov. 6-8, night race, with three rounds and 111 points left (74 after Qatar). Marc Marquez (Ducati Lenovo) arrives on 399 points after winning Malaysia by 4.1 seconds — his seventh win of 2026 and 80th in MotoGP — leading Jorge Martin (Aprilia) by 44 and Marco Bezzecchi by 67, having been 102 points behind the lead 161 days ago. The match-point math: Marquez must outscore Martin by 30 points and Bezzecchi by 8 across the weekend to clinch his eighth MotoGP (tenth world) title; Crash.net’s permutations add he must finish at least fifth in the Saturday Sprint to keep a Sunday clinch alive. Marquez is a double Lusail winner (2014, 2025), and five of his seven titles were clinched at the first opportunity. The field note: Yamaha has withdrawn from the Qatar GP entirely — neither Monster Energy Yamaha nor Prima Pramac Yamaha will be present, per the previews, citing corporate travel and security concerns — and Alex Marquez is ruled out for shoulder surgery.

Why it matters: Match point with real permutations: Marquez needs a 30-point swing on Martin in one weekend, which means the Sprint matters as much as the GP. Yamaha's absence is the paddock story nobody scheduled.
