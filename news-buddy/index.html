Binance drained of $6.3 billion in about 20 minutes — the largest crypto theft ever; investigators blame North Korea’s TraderTraitor

CCN, Oct. 30: the Oct. 29 attack struck Binance wallets across 10 blockchains; TRM Labs and Chainalysis attribute it with high confidence to TraderTraitor, the North Korean unit the FBI named for 2025’s $1.5 billion Bybit theft. About $3 billion has been frozen on-chain (Tether, Circle and others froze roughly $2.7 billion of it), Binance pledges to cover all losses from its SAFU fund and company funds, and withdrawals are paused with a restart expected within about 48 hours. Binance’s preliminary investigation points to a compromised DevOps engineer’s laptop — and no evidence that wallet private keys leaked.

The fast-cash advances draining America’s small businesses: MCA costs can reach 2,000%, and funders stand behind over half of 2026’s small-business bankruptcies

The Journal’s lead investigation, featured in its Oct. 30 10-Point: merchant cash advances promise cash in 24 hours with past credit troubles no problem — but because advances are not legally loans, funders evade lending rules and pull money straight from company bank accounts daily or weekly. Engineering

Anatomy of the drain: 10 blockchains, about 20 minutes, an EIP-7702 delegation that turned a Binance hot wallet into the attacker’s sweeper

TRM Labs’ primary report, Oct. 29-30: approximately $6.36 billion left Binance wallets starting 20:05 UTC on Oct. 29 across Ethereum, Bitcoin, Tron, the XRP Ledger, Solana, Arbitrum, Avalanche, Optimism, Base and Polygon — most of it in about 20 minutes. On Ethereum the attacker used an EIP-7702 delegation to convert a hot wallet into a sweeper it controlled, with 50 pre-signed authorizations prepared in advance; staging began at least 36 hours earlier, seeded by small Tornado Cash withdrawals. TRM attributes the operation to North Korea’s TraderTraitor ’with high confidence.’ Tether, Circle, Ripple and World Liberty Financial froze about $2.67 billion of stablecoins; Arbitrum’s Security Council froze more.

TRM Labs

The way in was a laptop: Binance’s preliminary read points to a compromised DevOps engineer’s machine — no private keys leaked, SAFU covers lossescrypto news site

CoinLab, Oct. 29-30, on Binance’s preliminary investigation: the entry point may have been a compromised DevOps engineer’s laptop, giving the attacker access to a service container from which unauthorized withdrawals were submitted. Binance says it has found no evidence wallet private keys leaked. The exchange puts the total impact at $6.3 billion with about $3 billion frozen, and says it will cover all losses with SAFU and company funds. The engineering lesson is the week’s starkest: the perimeter held; an engineer’s endpoint did not.

CoinLab

Fragnesia’s fuse burns down: the kernel half of the PHP2Shell chain is due TOMORROW, Oct. 31 — a PHP bump is not a kernel patchsecurity analysis blog

RWP Ventures’ KEV analysis, Oct. 29: CISA added the Linux kernel flaw Fragnesia (CVE-2026-46300, CVSS 7.8) to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Oct. 28 with a federal deadline of Oct. 31 and forensic triage required. CISA’s own description: it chains with the two PHP2Shell CVEs ’to implant PHP webshells and run commands as root’ — Dream’s chain rooted 24-25 firewalls in under 15 minutes. PHP2Shell’s own federal deadline passed yesterday (fixed builds 8.5.12 / 8.4.27 / 8.3.36 / 8.2.35; PHP 8.1 and older must come offline). But php -v does not patch uname -r: the kernel closes only with a fixed running kernel — distro package plus reboot, or the appliance vendor’s image. Palo Alto’s remaining PAN-OS hotfixes land Oct. 29-30.
