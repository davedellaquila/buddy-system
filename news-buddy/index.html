COP31 ends without a deal: the final draft never mentions fossil fuels, the EU refuses it, and the Antalya summit collapses in double overtime

Seoul Economic Daily, reporting Reuters from Antalya: COP31 closed Sunday without adopting a final agreement after the EU and climate-vulnerable countries refused a text that made no direct reference to fossil fuels. The draft released at 3 a.m. Sunday by co-president Murat Kurum omitted the words fossil, oil, coal and phase out across 10 pages, referenced the Dubai deal instead, acknowledged warming will exceed 1.5C, and set a carbon-capture goal of 1 billion tonnes. Saudi Arabia and other producers pushed to keep fossil fuels out; the talks ran two days past their Friday deadline before Kurum conceded the decisions had not been adopted.

Systems & security

Engineering

Java4Shell, itemized: a 62-byte overwrite in OpenJDK’s Base64 intrinsics, CVSS 9.2, pre-auth RCE in Keycloak, Jira, Confluence, Jenkins — patch builds listed

Metir’s engineering teardown of CVE-2026-114938: a short malformed input makes the AVX-512 VBMI Base64 decoder write 62 bytes past a 50-byte buffer before Java rejects it. Affected: OpenJDK 17.0.6+ (lines 17, 21, 25, 27) on x86-64 with AVX-512 VBMI — Intel Ice Lake onward, AMD Zen 4 onward; ARM is not affected. Oracle’s Nov. 17 fixes: 17.0.21.1, 21.0.13.1, 25.0.5.1, 27.0.1.1. Qualys demonstrated pre-auth RCE in Keycloak, Jira, Confluence, Artifactory, Jenkins and PingFederate. Stopgap: -XX:-UseBASE64Intrinsics. The durable fix is an inventory — every bundled JVM keeps its own copy of the decoder.

Metir

Java4Shell as a boundary failure: the flaw lives in the hand-written assembly Java substitutes for speed, where its memory-safety promise never applied

DEV Community’s deep dive makes the design point: Base64 carries auth headers, tokens and uploads, so attacker-controlled input reaching the decoder is ordinary traffic, not an edge case. The bug computes a negative output length from misplaced padding, and the runtime’s safety net arrives after the overwrite. Cloudflare shipped six managed WAF rules (log by default); each target app needed its own exploit chain, and no in-the-wild exploitation was confirmed as of Nov. 17. Found by Calif’s Quang Luong in about an hour with Codex, exploit in two more, under $200 in tokens.

DEV Community

AI found the bug in an hour; patching took the week: DNI tells the House the Microsoft, FAA, Chase, United, NSA and Apple outages were patching, not attacks

PAN’s relay of the BBC/CyberScoop file: the Director of National Intelligence told a House committee the week’s outages came from maintenance and technical changes, not attacks, and the White House said the incidents were separate — caused by patching vulnerabilities and upgrading outdated systems. Experts see the link to the surge in AI-found bugs: discovery is now measured in hours and dollars, remediation in change windows. That asymmetry — not any single CVE — is the week’s most consequential engineering hypothesis, and it is testable in every org’s own incident log.
