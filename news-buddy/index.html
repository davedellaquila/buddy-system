Engineering

OpenSSH ’sshole’ (CVE-2026-123158): the federal deadline is Thursday, Nov. 12 — update to 10.6p2 or kill password auth, then hunt for asterisk-account logins

Tuesday’s action list, from the daily KEV briefing: the musl password bypass — an oversized password makes musl’s hashing return an asterisk that OpenSSH treats as a match against asterisk-hash (password-disabled) accounts, up to root where such accounts carry passwordless admin rights — carries a CISA federal remediation deadline of Nov. 12, two days out. Exploitation attempts began Nov. 5, the day a proof of concept appeared. The prescribed sequence: update to OpenSSH 10.6p2 (or the distro backport) or set PasswordAuthentication no; then audit for unexpected password logins to asterisk accounts in /etc/shadow, unauthorized SSH keys, and persistence (agent files, startup services). Affected scope stays narrow — musl builds (Alpine, OpenWrt, Buildroot) without PAM — which is exactly why the check is cheap.

CVETodo (CISA KEV)

SonicWall (CVE-2026-19868), the hunt half: patching by Nov. 20 is the floor — the remediation is incomplete without a VPN-login review and credential resets

The vulnerability briefing’s remediation detail, carried into Tuesday: the SAML-enforcement bypass is exploitable only where SAML guards SSL-VPN logins, and a password from an alternate method can succeed alone — so the post-patch work is forensic: review SSL-VPN logins lacking a matching identity-provider sign-in, and reset credentials on accounts that show them. Fixes stand at Gen 7 7.3.4-7013 / Gen 8 8.2.3-8015, no workaround, KEV deadline Nov. 20, catalog at 1,769 entries. A completed patch, the briefing notes, is not proof earlier compromise did not occur.
