Top Stories

Krakow mourning: a gunman with a licensed AR-15 kills six in the Main Market Square — 15 wounded, two officers shot — and police find three more bodies at his home; Poland declares national mourning

Reuters via the wire relays, Nov. 7-8: at about 3 p.m. Saturday a 24-year-old Polish citizen drove into Krakow’s Main Market Square and opened fire at random. Six people were killed — four women and two men, a British citizen among them — and 15 were injured; two police officers were shot, one three times and in critical condition. Police shot and detained the gunman, who acted alone; later counts put total injured at 16 including the wounded suspect. Investigators then found three more bodies at his home in Ostrowiec Swietokrzyski, some 200 km away — reported to be his parents and sister. Interior Minister Marcin Kierwinski said everything points to a far-right ideology. Mayor Lukasz Gibala declared city mourning Sunday through Wednesday; President Nawrocki declared national mourning Nov. 8-9. It is Poland’s deadliest shooting since 1989.

Reuters

Israel’s president hands the mandate to Gadi Eisenkot: the former military chief gets 28 days to build a government Netanyahu’s bloc could not

President Isaac Herzog announced Saturday evening that he will formally task Yashar leader Gadi Eisenkot with forming a government, at a ceremony today at 5 p.m. at the President’s Residence. The recommendation count ran 67 for Eisenkot against 46 for Benjamin Netanyahu, with United Torah Judaism’s 7 backing neither. The arithmetic is the story: Netanyahu’s bloc holds 53 seats, the Eisenkot alliance 48, and the Arab-led Joint List (11) plus Ra’am (8) supply the 19 that make 67 — yet Eisenkot has ruled out Arab parties as formal coalition partners. Avigdor Liberman is the central obstacle. Eisenkot wants a national emergency government or a minority government, fast; Netanyahu stays on as caretaker. The Oct. 27 election produced no majority; the clock now runs 28 days, plus a possible 14-day extension.

President of Israel / CNN

The Gulf widens overnight: Houthi missiles intercepted over Riyadh, debris damages an Aramco building, the U.S. disables a ship out of Iran and stands up a 20-member task force

Sunday’s live reporting: the Saudi-led coalition intercepted two Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh and one at the Eastern Province, plus two cruise missiles; falling debris damaged an Aramco administrative building in Riyadh’s King Abdullah Financial District — minor damage, no casualties. Late Saturday the U.S. disabled a St. Kitts-flagged cargo ship in the Gulf of Oman after it left an Iranian port (AP), and Washington announced a 20-member task force to disrupt land transit routes into Iran (AFP). The IRGC said Saturday it has lifted its self-imposed 2,000 km missile-range limit. Iran reported two, then three, tankers catching fire in the Strait of Hormuz after hitting mines; the UAE National Guard rescued 22 crew from a burning cargo ship in the Gulf of Oman.

The week’s quietly alarming servicing story: an out-of-bounds read in Windows 11’s Paint lets an unauthenticated attacker run code over the network — no sign-in, no click — and the patch travels only through the Microsoft Store, because Paint is now a Store app outside the cumulative-update track.
