Microsoft’s longest outage in years is finally over: services restored Saturday, cause traced to a network capacity upgrade — not cyber, not AI

Microsoft posted Saturday that it had restored Microsoft 365 for the remaining affected customers and regions, closing incidents EX1550543 (Exchange, from 11:17 a.m. ET Thursday) and MO1550821 (final notices 4:15 and 4:17 a.m. GMT Saturday). Outlook, Teams, Copilot, SharePoint, OneDrive and Defender were hit in 70-plus countries for well over 24 hours. The mechanism: backend servers became unavailable after an earlier network capacity upgrade was exposed by a subsequent network change, and restarted servers kept failing under shifted load until a patch to an authentication and routing component landed. The email backlog is processed; a full post-incident report is promised, not yet published.

Engineering

CISA adds the UnrealIRCd flaw to the KEV: server-level command injection, exploitation under way, federal deadline Monday Nov. 16

CVE-2026-114713 hit the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog Friday: a labeled-response flaw in UnrealIRCd 6.0.0 before 6.2.7.1 lets an attacker inject server-to-server commands with server or IRC-operator privileges. Maintainer Bram Matthys shipped 6.2.7.1; exploitation began Nov. 7, a day after the hotfix. Federal agencies must remediate by Nov. 16 under BOD 26-04. Sources disagree on severity — The Hacker News lists CVSS 8.6, another brief 9.8; patch first, argue later.

The Hacker News

Apache patches a critical HTTP/2 race: CVE-2026-119618 (CVSS 9.8) in mod_http2, fixed in 2.4.71 — found by Anthropic’s OSS scanner and Google Cloud

A race in assign_headers() can corrupt memory and run code with no login, in Apache HTTP Server 2.4.55 through 2.4.70 with mod_http2 loaded and h2 or h2c in Protocols. Version 2.4.71, released Nov. 11, carries three security fixes. The reporters are a sign of the times: Anthropic’s automated OSS Scanner (Oct. 26) and Anthony Weems and Simon Scannell of Google Cloud Vulnerability Research (Nov. 3). Red Hat says public research showed a reliable exploit; no confirmed in-the-wild exploitation yet, and CISA has not listed it.

Reef Sentinel

N-able N-central zero-days exploited within hours of the proof of concept: at least 21 servers reportedly compromised, no fix yetthin sourcing

Researcher Sean Bales published proofs of concept Nov. 12 for flaws in N-central 2026.4 HF2 — the hotfix N-able shipped Nov. 10. The 4EverAble2Shell chain pairs an Apache Axis flaw (CVE-2026-90436, CVSS 9.8) with an N-central hole where the Legacy API is enabled. Probing began about 08:30 UTC Nov. 12 and full exploitation by 09:16, roughly five hours after publication. Researchers count at least 21 compromised servers across seven countries, reportedly running an XMRig miner — a detail N-able has not confirmed. The flaws are not yet in the KEV.
