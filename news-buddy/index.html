House shelves the Hegseth impeachment, 211-204: Massie’s eight articles go to the Judiciary Committee — no floor vote, no debate, and the effort expires with this Congress on Jan. 3

Tuesday evening’s only recorded vote on the question, Roll Call 317: Rep. Darrell Issa’s motion to refer carried 211-204 shortly after 6 p.m. Voting yes were 210 Republicans and independent Kevin Kiley; voting no were 203 Democrats and Massie himself — the Kentucky Republican who wrote the 34-page privileged resolution and forced it to the floor Monday. Eighteen members did not vote, 11 of them Democrats; NPR’s read is that attendance decided it — enough Democrats were in Washington to block referral, but not enough were on the floor. The articles (waging the Iran war past 90 days without authorization, the Venezuela/Yemen/Caribbean operations, civilian-casualty law, the Kelly retaliation charge) now sit with the Republican-led Judiciary Committee, which is not expected to advance them. Reason’s Volokh blog and the wires agree on the sequel: Democrats take both chambers Jan. 3 with roughly 260 House seats and can revive the case then; Senate conviction would still need 67 votes. Trump welcomed the outcome, calling Hegseth a ’great American patriot.’ Hegseth, at a Tuesday Pentagon briefing, called the charges political. Dave’s CPI-calendar and war-ledger threads both run through this vote.

Engineering

Patch Tuesday sets the record: 1,235 flaws by BleepingComputer’s count (tallies run 1,093-1,342), 66 critical — and one exploited Outlook zero-day, CVE-2026-124194, leaking NTLM hashes at CVSS 6.5

The count disagreement is part of the story: BleepingComputer counts 1,235 Patch-Tuesday flaws (excluding 108 fixed earlier in November); Tenable and SecurityWeek count 1,093-1,094; Senserva’s MSRC-based tally reaches 1,342. All agree on the shape: 66-68 critical, 48 of them RCE in BleepingComputer’s count, and exactly one flaw exploited in the wild — an Outlook spoofing bug that discloses a user’s NTLMv2 hash when a malicious file is opened, KEV-listed with a Nov. 24 federal deadline. The desk’s read: patch Outlook and Microsoft 365 Apps first, then Exchange and domain controllers; the thousand-plus remainder is volume, much of it AI-found, and the exploited one is the queue-jumper regardless of its moderate score.

BleepingComputer

OpenSSH ’sshole’ — the federal deadline is TOMORROW, Thursday Nov. 12: patch to 10.6p2 or disable password auth today, then hunt asterisk-account logins

The final call, carried daily since the Nov. 9 KEV listing: CVE-2026-123158 — the musl/OpenSSH password bypass on no-PAM builds (Alpine, OpenWrt, Buildroot) where a 256-1,024-character password can match asterisk-hash accounts — shares its Nov. 12 deadline with three other Nov. 9 additions. Exploitation attempts began Nov. 5, the day the proof of concept appeared. The sequence stands: update or set PasswordAuthentication no, then audit /etc/shadow asterisk accounts for unexpected password logins, unauthorized keys and persistence. After tomorrow, an unpatched musl SSH endpoint is a known-exploited finding, not a backlog item.
