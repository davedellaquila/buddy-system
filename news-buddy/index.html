Paraguay impeaches its president: Senate removes Santiago Pena 44-1 over the Ueno Bank collapse, he is under house arrest, and Vice President Pedro Alliana is sworn in through 2028

Reuters, Nov. 6-7: Paraguay’s Senate voted Friday to remove President Santiago Pena from office over his connection to the collapse of digital lender ueno, which froze access to millions of accounts. Pena had offered his resignation Thursday; lawmakers refused it and impeached him instead — the lower house had voted 78-0. He was placed under house arrest and is due to be questioned Saturday over breach of trust, influence peddling and misappropriation, per the prosecutor. Reporting put the affected depositors at roughly three million — about 60% of the country’s adults — and Pena had been a business partner in the group that owned the lender until March 2025. The central bank chief resigned Tuesday; eight ueno executives faced arrest warrants. Vice President Pedro Alliana, 52, becomes president and serves out the term through August 2028.

AI Briefing

The labs agree on a jailbreak ruler: Frontier Model Forum v1.0 rates cyber jailbreaks on four 0-3 scales into five bands, Negligible to Critical — comment open to Dec. 21

The Frontier Model Forum — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Microsoft and Amazon — released Version 1.0 of its Jailbreak Rating Framework for Frontier Cyber Threats on Nov. 5: a reproducible jailbreak against a specified model version is scored 0-3 on capability gain, capability scope, ease of deployment and ease of discovery (tiers intended as logarithmic), combining into five risk bands from Negligible to Critical; a capability-gain score of 0 ends the rating at Negligible. The baseline is the most capable widely available models as of October 2026, with ExploitBench and ExploitGym named as initial screens (GPT-6 Cyber scored 48.5% on ExploitGym). The forum calls v1.0 preliminary, built by the companies it evaluates with AI Security Institute input, and takes public comment through Dec. 21, 2026.

SonicWall’s SAML bypass is exploited in the wild: CVE-2026-19868 (CVSS 7.5) lets alternate credentials skip SAML and its MFA on Gen 7/8 SSL VPN — CISA KEV Nov. 6, federal deadline Nov. 20, no workaround

SecurityWeek, Nov. 7: SonicWall patched CVE-2026-19868, an authentication-enforcement flaw in the SonicOS SSLVPN service that lets a user with valid credentials for an alternate authentication method bypass SAML authentication controls — and the SAML-enforced MFA behind them. It is one of seven flaws in advisory SNWLID-2026-0014 (Nov. 6); affected: Gen 7 TZ/NSa/NSsp/NSv on SonicOS 7.3.3-7015 and earlier, and Gen 8 on 8.2.2-8015 and earlier; fixes are 7.3.4-7013 and 8.2.3-8015. SonicWall’s PSIRT investigated a report of possible exploitation; CISA added it to the KEV catalog the same day with a Nov. 20 federal remediation date under BOD 26-04. SonicWall says there is no workaround. Reported by Ryan Harris of The Electronic Office. Score notes differ by source (7.1 in the advisory text, 7.5 in the CVE record) — printed as published.

SecurityWeek

WordPress CVE-2026-119977, the morning after deadline day: the first update is itself the exposure — block wp-content/upgrade/ BEFORE updating, Picus warns, and exploit code is now on GitHub, unverified

Saturday status on yesterday’s Engineering lead: CISA’s Nov. 6 federal remediation date for the WordPress update-race flaw (CVSS 8.1, TOCTOU in Core 4.7-7.1.3) has passed with the catalog entry unchanged. Researcher Umut Bayram of Picus Security, who found the flaw, warns the first update from a vulnerable release is unpacked by the OLD updater, which still uses the predictable web-accessible staging folder — so a vulnerable site is exposed one last time while it installs the fix, WP-CLI included. Block web access to wp-content/upgrade/ before updating, or update by hand outside the web root. The Hacker News reports exploit code in public GitHub repositories; whether it works is unestablished. Wordfence has blocked 240,000+ attempts; still no public report of a confirmed break-in. Fixed releases exist for every branch back to 4.7 (7.1.4).
