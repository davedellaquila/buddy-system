Russian figures, unverified

Ukraine’s largest drone barrage of the war, Moscow says: 1,336 craft claimed downed overnight — and the Kerch Bridge hit, road traffic restricted to midday

Russia’s Defense Ministry said Monday its air defenses intercepted 1,336 Ukrainian fixed-wing drones overnight across 16-17 regions, occupied Crimea and the Black and Azov seas — above the previous claimed record of 1,254 on Oct. 29, and, if the count holds, the largest single-night barrage of the war. Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin said roughly 600 were aimed at the capital region, 191 downed on approach. Crimea’s Russian-installed head Sergey Aksyonov confirmed on Telegram that the Kerch Bridge — the 19-kilometre road-and-rail link to Krasnodar — was struck; road traffic was halted from about 2:22 a.m. Moscow time and restricted to midday, rail kept running, and the Kremlin called the damage minor. A monitoring channel reported a hit on an arch over the navigable span; Kyiv had not commented by Monday morning, and none of Moscow’s figures could be independently verified — which is why this card carries the flag. Ukraine’s air force said Russia launched 158 drones the other way the same night. The barrage lands three days before the Shenzhen trilateral, from which Washington has pointedly excluded the Ukraine file.

NPR / Reuters

A tale of two rockets: SpaceX catches a Starship from orbit for the first time — ULA’s Vulcan fails ten minutes in and loses all 40 Amazon Leo satellites

Saturday, Starbase, Texas: Flight 15’s upper stage, Ship 42, deployed 26 Starlink satellites, spent about 22 hours in orbit across 14 revolutions, re-entered at some 27,000 km/h — and settled between the launch tower’s chopstick arms, the first time any spacecraft returning from orbit has been caught by its tower. Musk said he inspected the heat shield and engines and the ship could fly again after a few weeks of refurbishment. The double catch did not happen: the Super Heavy booster diverted on a guidance anomaly and was lost over the Gulf. Sunday, Cape Canaveral: ULA’s Vulcan, on the first of Amazon’s 38 booked Vulcan missions, suffered an anomaly about ten minutes after its 17:40 UTC liftoff and failed to reach orbit, destroying 40 Leo broadband satellites — reported as ULA’s first total mission failure since the Boeing-Lockheed venture formed in 2006, with the fleet expected to ground pending investigation. SPCX rose about 1% overnight. Full engineering treatment below.

Engineering

Deadline day: CISA’s KEV remediation date for the UnrealIRCd command-injection flaw (CVE-2026-114713, CVSS 8.6) is today, Nov. 16 — patch to 6.2.7.1 or disconnect

The flaw the desk has tracked since the weekend reaches its federal deadline today: in UnrealIRCd 6.0.0 through 6.2.7.0, the IRCv3 labeled-response module echoes the label tag without neutralizing delimiters, so any user on a network of two or more linked servers can inject server-to-server commands that execute with full server authority — network-wide GLINE bans, KILLs, routing changes. The maintainers found it in an internal audit, called it the most severe issue in the daemon in 15 years, and shipped 6.2.7.1 with an emergency hot-patch on Nov. 7; CISA added it to the KEV on Nov. 13 under BOD 26-04 with today’s date. Exploitation is confirmed, not theoretical. Any linked IRC endpoint still on 6.2.7.0 or earlier is, as of this morning, past a federal deadline and inside an active campaign window.

CISA / TechWhoKnows

The KEV’s second deadline today: Check Point Gaia OS privilege escalation (CVE-2026-123039, CVSS 8.8) — a DynamicCLI socket flaw chained with wiper attacks on perimeter appliances

Also added Nov. 13, also due today: an incorrect permission assignment on an internal UNIX domain socket in the DynamicCLI daemon of Check Point’s Gaia OS — the system under its Quantum gateways, management servers and scalable platforms — lets an authenticated local user execute commands as root. Incident responders report the flaw being chained with separate initial-access vectors to deploy destructive wiper malware inside perimeter appliances, and folded into automated post-exploitation toolkits by both financially motivated and state-sponsored groups. Check Point shipped hotfixes for all supported releases late Friday via SmartUpdate. The pattern is the week’s engineering lesson in one pair of CVEs: the perimeter device and the chat daemon, both past deadline on the same Monday, both exploited before the deadline arrived.

CISA / TechWhoKnows

How one router stopped a country: El Dorado and Amarillo identified, 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds, both routers now in a Florida test lab

USA Today’s Sunday follow on Friday’s ground stop, from the FAA’s own FAQ: the flood — roughly 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds — is traced to two potential sources, the El Dorado, Arkansas radio communications site where L3Harris was replacing copper with fiber and deploying a new router, and an Amarillo, Texas radar site. When L3Harris blocked both locations, the network stabilized; the stop ran about 3:30 to 8:15 p.m. ET, with 9,159 cancellations counted for the day. Open questions, stated as such: whether Amarillo malfunctioned independently or echoed El Dorado, and whether the root cause was a faulty router or an installation/configuration error. The mitigations are already systemic — the broadcast function is disabled on all new routers — and auditors note the FTI network underneath has been flagged as an outage risk for over 15 years. The system was 69-70% converted; the failure lived exactly at the seam between old and new.
