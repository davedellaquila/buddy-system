Nvidia Begins Selling AI-Optimized Personal Computers

Nvidia has started selling its first line of personal computers built for on-device AI, from $2,599, made on its RTX Spark chip by ASUS, Dell, HP, Lenovo, Microsoft and MSI. Product Design

RIBA’s Stirling Prize goes to social housing, furniture goes deliberately low-res, and Apple’s HomeView nods to the iMac G4.

“Welcoming and inclusive” London housing wins RIBA Stirling Prize 2026

Mary Duggan Architects with Ruff Architects won the UK’s top architecture prize for Lion Green Road — 157 high-density homes in south London stitched together with shared green spaces on a steep former car-park site. Only the fourth housing scheme to win in 30 years: faceted star-shaped plans give every home dual aspect and unique views, making the case that density and delight aren’t trade-offs.

Dezeen

Jonas Hejduk’s blurry furnishings are deliberately low resolution

French designer Jonas Hejduk is selling rugs, benches, ceramics and wallpapers rendered as deliberately pixelated “poor images” — Persian rugs reduced to their own blurry thumbnails, log-texture benches like unloaded 1990s video-game graphics, each piece named with filename formats and file-size metadata woven into the surface. Craft-as-commentary on digital clarity and AI polish, and deliberately hard for social-media algorithms to photograph.

Dezeen

Apple consolidates smart-home capabilities in HomeView dashboard

Apple’s new HomeView is a 7-inch smart-home dashboard — a tabletop version with integrated speaker and recycled-aluminium base that deliberately evokes the 2002 iMac G4’s rounded base and movable screen, plus a wall-mount variant. It centralizes Home controls with face recognition for six household members, FaceTime, an intercom and a new conversational “Siri AI.” The hardware callback to the iMac G4 alone is catnip for Apple design-history nerds.

Engineering

A paper mill becomes a horsepower palace, inference becomes the chip industry’s defining problem, and neurons on silicon.

Retired Michigan Paper Mill Becomes Home to Horsepower

The 1887 Plainwell paper mill — dormant for 25+ years, bought by the city for $1 — just opened as the Classic Auto Mill: 255,000 square feet of exposed brick and tall ceilings devoted to classic-car storage, restoration and sales, the second such operation by the Grigaliunas family. Industrial-heritage preservation meeting car culture at genuine scale.

Hagerty Media

Inside the Inference Hardware Revolution Of 2026

With training receding to the background, inference has become the defining hardware problem of 2026 — reasoning models running chain-of-thought up to 20x over, plus agents running inference around the clock, are forcing chipmakers to pivot hard. Jensen Huang called it the “inflection point of inference.” A solid IEEE Spectrum survey of the shift every hardware engineer is living through.

IEEE Spectrum

NSRAM: The Artificial Neuron on a Silicon Chip

Neuromorphic engineering’s bid to close the gap between GPU simulation — data-center GPUs burning up to 1,000 watts each just moving data — and the human brain, roughly a million times more energy-efficient at comparable tasks. By building circuits that act like biological neurons and synapses instead of simulating them, NSRAM-style approaches could radically change where AI runs.

Amador County

The wind shutoff is the county’s whole story: PG&E says winds eased Friday, but offered no restoration timeline. Otherwise a quiet week.

When will power be restored? PG&E meteorologists expect Sierra foothills wind to ease

Friday morning, 85,511 PG&E customers were still in the dark across the foothills — including 4,557 in Amador County — in what the utility calls its largest emergency shutoff in eight years. PG&E’s meteorologists expect winds to ease across much of Northern California on Friday, but the forecast gave no restoration timeline: warnings remain in effect through Saturday night, and crews must issue a weather all-clear, then inspect every line, pole and tower before re-energizing.
