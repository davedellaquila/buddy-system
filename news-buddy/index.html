Democrats seize control of Congress in a historic blue wave — Senate flips in Texas, Kansas, Iowa, Ohio, Maine, North Carolina and South Carolina; the House called at 1:28 a.m. ET

CNN’s takeaways, Nov. 4: a historic Democratic wave swept the party into power in Congress and statehouses as voters rebuked President Trump and rejected candidates he had propped up. Democrats won a Senate race in Kansas for the first time since the 1930s and a South Carolina seat lost when Strom Thurmond switched parties in the 1960s; the GOP gains of the Trump era among Latinos and rural voters were wiped out in one night, and the redistricting gambit fell flat. AP’s count early Wednesday: House Democrats 222 called with 41 leading; the incoming Senate stands 54-44 with Alaska and Mississippi still uncalled. The new Congress takes office Jan. 3, 2027.

Today is GitHub’s first SSH brownout: legacy ssh-rsa (SHA-1) and diffie-hellman-group-exchange-sha256 go dark briefly — permanent removal Jan. 13, 2027

GitHub’s changelog, action day: the Nov. 4 brownout temporarily removes the ssh-rsa signature type (RSA with SHA-1, including SHA-1 certificates) and the diffie-hellman-group-exchange-sha256 key exchange, ahead of a second brownout Dec. 9 and permanent removal Jan. 13, 2027 (GitHub Enterprise Server 3.25). Since Oct. 14, newly uploaded RSA keys must be at least 3,072 bits, and the post-quantum mlkem768x25519-sha256 exchange is supported on github.com and GHES Cloud with Data Residency (except the U.S. region). Most modern clients already sign with SHA-2 RSA and need no new key; HTTPS remotes are untouched. Today’s failures are the free audit: whatever breaks is what Jan. 13 would have broken permanently.

GitHub Changelog

F5’s Nov. 4 threat bulletin: attackers chained PHP2Shell with the Fragnesia kernel flaw to compromise 25 Palo Alto PAN-OS systems and tunnel into a U.S. government-affiliated network

F5 Labs’ Weekly Threat Bulletin, Nov. 4: threat actors compromised 25 Palo Alto Networks PAN-OS systems — 24 perimeter firewalls and one internal management server — by chaining the critical PHP2Shell RCE flaws (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, CVSS 9.2, in PHP’s session-upload handling) with the Fragnesia Linux kernel privilege-escalation flaw (CVE-2026-46300) to reach root. The custom exploit was tailored to the uniform PHP 8.0.30 build of the PAN-OS appweb management server; from there the operators set a persistent reverse SSH tunnel into a U.S. government-affiliated environment and harvested directory-service credentials. Mitigation: apply PAN-OS remediations, update standalone PHP to 8.2.35, 8.3.36, 8.4.27 or 8.5.12, or disable session.upload_progress.enabled to close the upload-progress path. The PHP2Shell saga this paper has tracked since Oct. 23 now has a named victim class.

WordPress Core’s upgrade race (CVE-2026-119977) joins CISA’s KEV catalog: 18,500-plus exploit requests in 21 hours, no confirmed compromises, federal due date Nov. 6

XHack’s Nov. 4 guide: CVE-2026-119977 is a time-of-check/time-of-use race in how WordPress Core unpacks its own complete-package updates — while an update extracts, an unauthenticated visitor may reach a staged copy of the installer and run code. Wordfence (the CNA) scores it CVSS 8.1 High with high attack complexity; WordPress’s own advisory says ’low’ with no score; CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog Nov. 3 marked ’active,’ due Nov. 6, with the forensic-triage flag — preserve logs before cleaning. Fixed in 7.1.4 (Oct. 28) with backports to every branch from 4.7; 4.6 and earlier get nothing. The patch window is the exposed window: Patchstack advises blocking web access to wp-content/upgrade/ before updating. Wordfence saw 18,500+ requests at the staged paths in 21 hours and reports no successful compromise yet.

Decision Desk HQ’s results, checked Nov. 4 at 6:06 a.m. ET: Xavier Becerra (D) won the California governorship with 4,960,174 votes (64.04%) to Steve Hilton’s (R) 2,784,695 (35.96%), called at 11:13 p.m. ET on Election Night with 66% reporting statewide.
