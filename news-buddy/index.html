Hong Kong court sentences Joshua Wong to 10 years on a national security charge — the face of the pro-democracy protests, detained since 2020, now held until late 2036

Reuters and Human Rights Watch, Nov. 5: Hong Kong’s High Court sentenced Joshua Wong, 30, to 10 years in jail for conspiring to collude with foreign forces under the Beijing-imposed National Security Law — his second NSL conviction, making him the only person charged twice under it. He was accused of conspiring with self-exiled activist Nathan Law and others to request that foreign governments impose sanctions or blockades against the Hong Kong and China governments, and pleaded guilty in September. Judge William Tam called the offense of ’grave nature,’ starting at 13 years before the guilty-plea reduction. Wong has been in custody since November 2020 and was already serving 4 years 8 months in the ’Hong Kong 47’ subversion case, due to end in January 2027; with two months of the new sentence concurrent, he faces imprisonment until late 2036, at age 40.

Bank of England raises rates to 4% — the first hike since 2023, 8-1, with Bailey warning more may come unless the Iran conflict ends and inflation forecast near 5% by mid-2027

Reuters, Nov. 5 (Milliken, Bruce and Abdulla, London): the Bank of England raised Bank Rate 25 basis points to 4% from 3.75% — its first increase in over three years — and warned at least one more is likely if the U.S.-Iran conflict continues, a path the Fed and ECB are already on. The Monetary Policy Committee voted 8-1; the dissent was Catherine Mann, voting for a larger hike to 4.25%. Governor Andrew Bailey: ’Today’s increase shouldn’t be seen as the first in an inevitable long sequence of hikes. But unless the conflict in the Middle East is resolved, neither does it seem likely to be the last increase.’ The Bank’s central forecast now sees UK inflation around 5% in the middle of next year, more than double the 2% target — a blow for Prime Minister Andy Burnham — with growth revised up to 1.6% this year and 1.3% next.

AI & Technology

Anthropic uses Claude to show bacterial and phage proteins form vaults — 15 billion predicted proteins searched, a first prokaryotic vault structure, and the lab’s own speed claims labeled as its own

Anthropic reported Nov. 4 that prokaryotic and bacteriophage relatives of the major vault protein can assemble into vault particles when produced in E. coli — the first 3D structure of a vault particle from prokaryotic life, in an unreviewed bioRxiv preprint. Using Claude Science, the team searched approximately 15 billion predicted proteins and found major vault protein homologs in 29 bacterial phyla and four archaeal phyla, in under 1% of sequenced species. Of 44 proteins selected for cell-free expression, 15 of 24 candidates produced soluble protein, 7 of those formed vault-like particles under electron microscopy, and cryo-EM confirmed vault shape in 4. The detailed phage-derived particle holds 96 copies of one protein and measures about 54 by 45 nanometres at 9.5 megadaltons, versus 78 copies in mammalian vaults. Anthropic’s own workflow claims — purification cut from days to about 90 minutes, a first model in 34 hours — are the lab’s, not independently benchmarked, and coverage notes Claude suggestions also delayed one experiment by two weeks. Native-host formation is untested.

Lagoons will weave through a new Egyptian city by SOM — Alam Al Roum: 7.2 km of Mediterranean coast, 22 km of seawater lagoons, phase one in 2030

Dezeen, Nov. 5: SOM has unveiled Alam Al Roum, a new city for developer Qatari Diar stretching 7.2 kilometres along Egypt’s Mediterranean coast near Marsa Matrouh, across a 20.6-square-kilometre masterplan. Swimmable lagoons sit alongside 22 kilometres of seawater-connected lagoons weaving through the built environment to support stormwater management, with buildings oriented for coastal breezes and sunlight; the development is elevated for changing coastal conditions and protected by riparian buffers, stormwater wetlands, stabilised dunes and living shorelines. The program: beachfront and lagoon-side neighbourhoods, town centres, hospitality and resorts, golf, an education district and a business hub, with a grand boulevard running from an international marina to an inland mobility hub and a waterfront downtown with a cultural pier, lighthouse, amphitheatre, yacht club, mosque and markets. SOM principal Thomas Hussey: ’Alam Al Roum presents a rare opportunity to imagine a new city from the ground up, one that responds to the realities of climate change while offering an exceptional quality of life.’ Phase one — four square kilometres with a town-centre marina — is expected to commence in 2030.

WordPress’s upgrade-race flaw is due tomorrow: CISA’s KEV listing for CVE-2026-119977 sets a Nov. 6 federal deadline, a public proof of concept landed Nov. 4, and Wordfence counts 12,000-18,500 attack requests

CISA added CVE-2026-119977 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Nov. 3, citing active exploitation, with a federal remediation due date of Nov. 6 under Binding Operational Directive BOD 26-04 — which requires forensic triage, not patch-only remediation; ransomware use is listed as unknown. The flaw is a time-of-check/time-of-use race (CWE-367, CVSS 3.1 8.1 High) in how WordPress Core unpacks full-package upgrades: during the upgrade window, an unauthenticated visitor may reach a staged copy of the installer at predictable web-accessible paths and run code. It affects WordPress Core 4.7 through 7.1.3; the fix landed in 7.1.4 (Oct. 28) with backports to every affected branch. A working public proof of concept was published on GitHub Nov. 4, and exploit counts differ by source and window — Wordfence blocked 12,289 attacks in the 24 hours before Nov. 4 in one account, and recorded 18,500-plus requests at the staged paths in the first 21 hours in another. One rating discrepancy worth knowing: WordPress’s own advisory calls the issue ’low’ and lists no CVE, while Wordfence, the CNA, scores it 8.1. The patch window is the exposed window: block web access to wp-content/upgrade/ first, and preserve logs.

CISA

The PHP2Shell chain, in full: F5’s bulletin plus the researchers’ account — 25 PAN-OS systems rooted, ~40 post-exploitation scripts, a reverse tunnel into a government-affiliated network in under 90 minutes

F5 Labs’ Nov. 4 bulletin, deepened by Dream Research’s fuller account (discovered Oct. 25, updated Oct. 29): attackers compromised 25 Palo Alto Networks PAN-OS systems — 24 perimeter firewalls and one internal management server — by chaining the PHP2Shell RCE flaws (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, CVSS 9.2, in PHP’s session-upload handling, with a custom exploit tailored to the uniform PHP 8.0.30 build of the PAN-OS appweb server) with the Fragnesia kernel flaw (CVE-2026-46300) to reach root. Dream describes roughly 40 custom post-exploitation scripts and exploitation across the U.S., Japan, Taiwan and South Korea; Palo Alto/Unit 42 independently verified the tooling (tracked as CL-UNK-1280, exploit maturity ’ATTACKED’). From the firewalls, the operators set a persistent reverse SSH tunnel into a U.S. government-affiliated environment — in under 90 minutes, per Dream — compromised the internal management server and attacked Active Directory. Singapore’s CSA notes the PHP flaws also affect selected Cisco, Citrix, F5 and Siemens products. Fixed PHP: 8.2.35, 8.3.36, 8.4.27, 8.5.12, or disable session.upload_progress.enabled. Attribution to a suspected Chinese actor is the researchers’ assessment, not a settled finding.

F5 Labs

GitHub’s first SSH brownout passed quietly: no fallout reports by Thursday morning, systems operational — the legacy ssh-rsa and DH-exchange removal returns Dec. 9, permanently on Jan. 13status relay

The day after: GitHub’s Nov. 4 brownout — a controlled, temporary removal of RSA signatures using SHA-1 (ssh-rsa, including SHA-1 certificates) and the diffie-hellman-group-exchange-sha256 key exchange — has produced no reported fallout in this morning’s check: no breakage counts, no incident report tied to the brownout, and a status aggregator at 5:15 a.m. Nov. 5 showed GitHub ’All Systems Operational.’ That is a relay’s read, not GitHub’s own post-mortem, hence the thin flag. The schedule ahead: a second brownout Dec. 9, 2026, and permanent removal Jan. 13, 2027. No key replacement is required for modern clients — RSA keys signing with SHA-2 (rsa-sha2-256/512) keep working with the same key, HTTPS remotes are untouched, and since Oct. 14 newly uploaded RSA keys must be at least 3,072 bits, with the post-quantum mlkem768x25519-sha256 exchange enabled outside the U.S. region initially. The risk pocket remains old SSH clients embedded in CI servers, Jenkins agents, Java SSH libraries and appliances, where failures surface as ’Permission denied (publickey)’ or ’no matching key exchange method found.’
