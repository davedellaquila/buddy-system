Roads

  • Million Dollar Highway (US 550), Colorado — Ouray → Silverton → Durango over Red Mountain Pass (11,018 ft). Shelf road, no guardrails in stretches; one of the great American drives. Best window: late spring through fall; winter is serious mountain driving. ~950 miles from Fiddletown — a Colorado-loop trip, not a weekend. Status: idea. Source: https://www.facebook.com/reel/4265069620303960/ (This World Traveled, Sep 23, 2026 — flagged by Dave Oct 30). Also named in the "8 scenic roads" reel, Aug 19, 2026: https://www.facebook.com/reel/1537903230931156/ (relayed Nov 5, 2026).

  • † US Route 89 — "The National Park Highway" — Flagstaff, AZ to the Canadian border in Montana. Backbone for 10 national parks: Grand Canyon and Petrified Forest (AZ); Zion, Bryce Canyon, Capitol Reef, Canyonlands, and Arches (UT); Grand Teton and Yellowstone (WY); Glacier (MT). A NatGeo Traveler Top 10 drivers' drive. Suggested use: early fall or 2027, or drive it in stretches rather than end-to-end. Distance from Fiddletown: not yet noted. Status: idea. Source: https://www.facebook.com/reel/1043514015336263/ (Luke Kelly — sent by Dave Nov 4, 2026). Second source (filed Nov 11, 2026): The Traveling Dans, "1 highway, 10 national parks" (posted Nov 4, 2026) — https://facebook.com/reel/874889135687370/ — bills US 89 as America's most scenic road trip; same 10 parks (Glacier, Yellowstone, Grand Teton, Arches, Canyonlands, Capitol Reef, Bryce Canyon, Zion, Grand Canyon, Petrified Forest) plus also-named: Cedar Breaks National Monument, Kodachrome Basin, Grand Staircase–Escalante National Monument, Montezuma Castle National Monument, Walnut Canyon National Monument, Wupatki National Monument, Sunset Crater National Monument. Creator's guidance: best window August to early October, driven NORTH to SOUTH (warmer parks later in the fall), at least 2 weeks for parks plus drive time. GPX format for Dave's inRoute — playbook

Learned Nov 19, 2026 — decoded by Lenwood Gazette Buddy from Dave's own inRoute export (creator "inRoute-v9.0.9"): Dave imported a draft GPX, set the intermediate points as vias in the app, and re-exported. Transferred to Road Trip Buddy at Dave's direction, Nov 19, 2026. Use this format for every GPX built for Dave from now on.

The native inRoute format

  1. Namespace: the <gpx> element carries the Garmin TripExtensions namespace: xmlns:trp="http://www.garmin.com/xmlschemas/TripExtensions/v1"
  2. The route is an <rte> of <rtept> points, each carrying <extensions>: - True endpoints (start/finish): <trp:ViaPoint/> - Shaping points — what Dave sets as "vias" in the app. They steer the route along chosen roads but are NOT announced during navigation: <trp:ShapingPoint/>, and the point also carries <time>1969-12-31T23:59:59Z</time> (null-time marker).
  3. An <rte> alone is enough for import — inRoute adds it as the route (≤150 points auto-adds; that is also Dave's standing cap per file). inRoute's own exports additionally include a <wpt> copy of every map pin and a <trk> of the drawn route, but neither is needed in files we author.
  4. NOT the format: <type>via</type> — that's the Locus/Cruiser convention and inRoute ignores it.

Route-building lesson (Dave, Nov 19, 2026)

  • POI-only waypoints remain right for point-to-point road routes — inRoute draws a good route between distant POIs (Dave's standing rule since Nov 7, 2026).
  • But when the specific streets ARE the route (a neighborhood crawl, a scenic loop), sparse pins fail — inRoute shortcuts and zigzags between them. Then: thread the intended streets with a shaping point at every turn so each leg is a single-street run, built from real street geometry (OpenStreetMap junction data works well).
  • Total points per file still ≤150 (Dave's cap, Nov 15, 2026).

Commit points (addendum, Nov 19, 2026 — Dave caught this from a screenshot of an imported route)

  • A shaping point placed exactly AT a junction, on the road you're already traveling, does NOT force a turn — the router rolls through it and can reach the next point by staying on the through road. (In the Willow Glen route, the Glen Brook Ave → Glen Echo Ave turn was skipped exactly this way: the point sat on Glen Brook at the junction, and Glen Echo's far end was reachable via Glen Brook + Willow St.)
  • Fix: place each turn's shaping point ~30 m PAST the junction, ON the street being turned onto — a "commit point" — computed from the street's real geometry (OpenStreetMap way geometry works).
  • Sanity check for every turn: if the next point is reachable without making the turn, the turn is unpinned — move its commit point onto the target street.
  • Short legs (addendum 2, Nov 19, 2026): on a very short connector street (~80 m, e.g. Glen Dell Dr between Camino Ramon and Camino Pablo), the drawn route can still kink at the corner even with commit points. Fix: add a mid-block shaping point on the short street itself, and push the next street's commit point further past the corner (~55 m) so it sits unambiguously on the new street.
  • Off-roadway points (addendum 3, Nov 19, 2026 — from Dave's screenshot review): a shaping point computed from one map's geometry can land off the roadway in the router's map data — it draws as a jog off the street (happened with a point 25 m up Camino Pablo computed from OpenStreetMap geometry). If a point draws off-street, drop it and let the next on-street point carry the turn.
  • Long runs (addendum 3): long single-street runs (~700 m) get mid-run shaping points every ~250 m so the router can't shortcut via parallel streets.

B. The MVUM's own framing

  • Edition: 2024. The MVUM states its designations were made by the responsible official under 36 CFR 212.51, are effective as of the date on the front cover, and remain in effect until superseded by the next year's MVUM. Users are told it is their responsibility to acquire the current map.
  • What the map designates: National Forest System roads and trails open to motorized travel, by vehicle class and season. Vehicle use off designated routes (cross-country travel) is prohibited — motorized travel on the Eldorado has been limited to designated routes since 2008.
  • Designated-use classes printed on the Georgetown MVUM:
  • Roads Open to All Vehicles — Seasonal / Yearlong
  • Roads Open to Highway Legal Vehicles Only — Seasonal / Yearlong
  • Trails Open to All Vehicles; Trails Open to Wheeled Vehicles Only < 50"; Trails Open to Motorcycles Only (seasonal/yearlong designations)
  • The map also notes Class 1, 2 and 3 e-bikes are allowed on roads designated for all motor vehicles and on trails open to all motor vehicles.
  • Seasonal designation, as printed on the map: "All routes with a seasonal designation are open from 04/01 to 12/31." The Georgetown sheets additionally print a Seasonal Designation table naming two route groups:
  • 04/01 – 12/31: 11E53, 11NY19, 12N56, 12N59, 12N59B, 12N60C, 12N60P, 13N58, 13N60
  • 05/02 – 11/09: 11E36, 11E37, 11E38, 11N81, 11N89, 11N92, 11N92A, 12N81B, 12N81E, 12NY01 — the Rock Creek system routes covered by the Critical Deer Winter Range closure (see Source 4; the FS Road and Trail Status page records the Nov 10–May 1 prohibition on the southern Rock Creek roads/trails, with exceptions for Darling Ridge Road 12N80, Mosquito Road 12N60, Rock Creek Road 12N70, Slate Mountain Mine Road 12N83, Slate Mountain Road 12N76 and Lower Rock Creek Trail).
  • The Rock Creek system also has its own wet-weather closure plan and does not fall under the forest-wide seasonal dirt closure (FS Road and Trail Status page).
  • Forest-wide seasonal closure (separate from the map's designations): all dirt (native-surface) roads and trails on the Eldorado are closed to motor vehicle use January 1 through at least March 31 each year; the closure may be extended for wet conditions (FS Road and Trail Status page).
  • How routes are numbered: Eldorado route numbers follow the form township + route number — e.g., 12N70 is road 70 in Township 12 North. On the Georgetown sheets the townships run 10N–14N, with a cluster of 17N labels at the northern sheet edge (see Section C4). Letter suffixes are branches/spurs or segments of the same road. "N" numbers are roads; "E" numbers are trails. A Rock Creek-area peculiarity: several routes carry a "Y" inside the number (11NY19, 12NY01, and 12NY21/12NY26 in other FS/USGS documents for this same area) — these are reproduced as printed. Some major through-roads also carry older short "Forest Road" numbers or "Primary Route" numbers in FS recreation documents — Eleven Pines Road is Forest Road 2 (= 14N08) and Wentworth Springs Road is Primary Forest Route 1 (and county road ELD-63).
  • Total designated route miles in the district: not stated on the MVUM. The map gives no district mileage total, and no total is claimed here. (Its printed tables do give exact lengths for the shortest segments — see Section C.)
  • Vehicle-suitability marker used by the forest: per the Eldorado NF OHV page, a vertical route marker is placed on roughly graded roads recommended for high-clearance vehicles, 4WD and pick-ups. The MVUM itself does not print a surface type per road, and no Georgetown-district FS document found pairs road names with surfacing/maintenance levels the way the Panther EA does for Amador — so surface and maintenance level are "not established" for most roads in Section D.

C4. Inventory completeness and limits — read this before using the census

  • 379 unique route numbers were extracted in total: 346 road (N) labels and 33 trail (E) labels, of which 160 printed segment rows (157 distinct route numbers) also have printed lengths/classes (C1).
  • Sheet-edge caution: a cluster of labels belongs to adjoining districts/forests that the Georgetown sheets draw for context — notably the 17N family (17N02 and segments; the 17N12 cluster around Wentworth Springs' northern end) at the Tahoe NF / Pacific RD edge, and the 10N40 family (10N40C, 10N40M, 10N40N) at the Placerville RD edge. They are extracted from this district's sheets and are counted above, but a route-by-route check against the adjoining district's MVUM is needed before treating the 346 as a certified Georgetown-only count. Read the total as "roughly 345 distinct designated road routes shown on the Georgetown sheets."
  • The MVUM map face is a graphic: road names are letter-spaced along curves and mostly do not survive text extraction, and a few route labels printed at awkward angles or split by the text layer (e.g., "12N 70"-style splits) may likewise be missed or merged. Two labels the FS status page establishes for this district — 12N76 (Slate Mountain Road) and 12N83 (Slate Mountain Mine Road; segment 12N83D is tabulated in C1) — did not survive extraction as base numbers, which proves the label pass is incomplete at the margins.
  • Suspect labels (reproduced as printed; verify against the paper map before relying on them): 13N31AN (tabulated; the "AN" suffix is unusual), 11NY19 and 12NY01 (mid-number "Y" — an attested Rock Creek-area pattern, see Section B, but still worth a paper check), 17N12QA / 17N12QB, and 12N68 (tabulated at 52 ft — plausible for a connector segment, but the shortest row on the sheet).
  • The census therefore = C1 (hard data) + C2 (label-only). It is a starter census: enough to plan drives and to check off routes, but the authoritative check for any single road's class/season is the MVUM map graphic itself (or the ranger district).

B. The MVUM's own framing

  • Edition: 2024. As on the Amador sheets, the designations were made by the responsible official under 36 CFR 212.51, are effective as of the date on the front cover, and remain in effect until superseded by the next year's MVUM. Users are told it is their responsibility to acquire the current map.
  • What the map designates: National Forest System roads and trails open to motorized travel, by vehicle class and season. Vehicle use off designated routes (cross-country travel) is prohibited — motorized travel on the Eldorado has been limited to designated routes since 2008.
  • Designated-use classes printed on the Pacific MVUM (the road classes match the Amador sheet; the Pacific tables add one more):
  • Roads Open to All Vehicles — Seasonal / Yearlong
  • Roads Open to Highway Legal Vehicles Only — Seasonal / Yearlong
  • Other Public Roads — the class the Pacific tables print for the ELD-numbered corridor segments (Section C1); these are designated routes carried under "ELD" identifiers rather than township route numbers.
  • Trails Open to All Vehicles; Trails Open to Wheeled Vehicles 50" or less; Trails Open to Motorcycles Only (seasonal designations)
  • Seasonal designation, as printed on the map: "All routes with a seasonal designation are open from 04/01 to 12/31." (No other date range appears in the Pacific sheets' extracted text.)
  • Forest-wide seasonal closure (separate from the map's designations): all dirt (native-surface) roads and trails on the Eldorado are closed to motor vehicle use January 1 through at least March 31 each year; the closure may be extended for wet conditions (FS Visitor Guide / Road and Trail Status page).
  • How routes are numbered: as on the Amador sheets, Eldorado route numbers follow the form township + route number — e.g., 13N17 is road 17 in Township 13 North. On the Pacific sheets the townships run 10N–17N for roads and 12E–17E for trails. Letter suffixes are branches/spurs or segments of the same road (Loon Lake Campground Road is 13N17, with segments 13N17A, 13N17BA–BF on the map). "N" numbers are roads; "E" numbers are trails.
  • Two numbering quirks unique to these sheets (flagged, not explained away):
  • "Y" identifiers. The Pacific tables print a family of route numbers with a Y between the township and the route number — 11NY32, 12NY04, 12NY15D, 17NY12H and more; 24 distinct Y-forms in all (Sections C1/C2). They are tabulated exactly like other routes. Meanwhile the map face also carries the corresponding labels without the Y (e.g. "11N32" appears as a map-face label while the table prints "11NY32"). This census keeps the two spellings separate, exactly as printed, and does not merge them — whether they denote the same physical routes is not established by any source consulted.
  • "ELD" identifiers. The principal Crystal Basin corridors are carried in the tables as ELD-63-A…F (Wentworth Springs corridor segments) and ELD-1593, ELD-2130, ELD-2214, all classed "Other Public Roads". The FS status page (Source 2) uses the same scheme: ELD-63 = Wentworth Springs Road, ELD-147 = Ice House Road.
  • Older short "Forest Road" numbers also circulate in FS recreation documents for this district: Ice House Road is Forest Road 3 ("Primary Forest Route 3"); Wrights Lake Road is Forest Road 4; the Wrights Lake Tie is Forest Road 32 in campground directions; Eleven Pines Road is Forest Road 2 (= 14N08).
  • Total designated route miles in the district: not stated on the MVUM. The map gives no district mileage total, and no total is claimed here. (Its printed tables do give exact lengths for the shortest segments — see Section C.)
  • Vehicle-suitability marker used by the forest: per the Eldorado NF OHV page (Source 7), a vertical route marker is placed on roughly graded roads recommended for high-clearance vehicles, 4WD and pick-ups. The MVUM itself does not print a surface type per road, and — unlike the Amador district, where the Panther EA supplied a name/surface table — no FS document found for the Pacific district pairs road names with surfacing or maintenance levels, so surfaces below are "not established" except where a source states them outright.

C4. Inventory completeness and limits — read this before using the census

  • 301 unique route numbers were extracted in total: 287 road (N and ELD) labels and 14 trail (E) labels, of which 230 route numbers also have printed lengths/classes (C1). Headline census numbers: 287 designated road routes and 14 designated trails shown on the Pacific sheets, 238 of their short segments tabulated with lengths and classes.
  • Suspect labels (flagged individually):
  • ELD-1, ELD-14, ELD-16 — extracted at sheet-edge/vicinity positions and almost certainly fragments of longer ELD designations (or of adjoining-area labels) rather than complete route numbers. They are included in the 287 count for transparency; the defensible road total is 284–287.
  • 13N22CB (map face) vs 13N22CB1 (tabulated, 498 ft) — the face label may be the same label truncated by extraction, or a parent route of CB1. Counted as extracted (both), flagged here.
  • The "Y" spellings (Section B): table spellings like 11NY32 and map-face spellings like 11N32 are counted as extracted — separately. If every Y-form is the same route as its no-Y counterpart, the unique road total would drop by up to ~24. No source consulted resolves this; the on-the-ground check is the printed MVUM graphic itself.
  • Known routes whose numbers do not extract at all: the plain tokens "11N26" (Wrights Lake Road — only its segments 11N26A/B extract), "ELD-147" (Ice House Road's status-page designation), "11N60" (Jaybird Canyon) and the trail "16E21" (Barrett Lake) appear in FS documents but not in the MVUM text layer; they are recorded in Sections D/C3 from those documents and are not in the 301 count.
  • The MVUM map face is a graphic: road names are letter-spaced along curves and mostly do not survive text extraction, and a few route labels printed at awkward angles may likewise be missed or merged. A handful of labels near the sheet edges belong to adjoining districts (Georgetown, Placerville, Lake Tahoe Basin Management Unit) that the map draws for context.
  • The census therefore = C1 (hard data) + C2 (label-only), cross-checked by two independent extraction methods. It is a starter census: enough to plan drives and to check off routes, but the authoritative check for any single road's class/season is the MVUM map graphic itself (or the ranger district).

B. Framing (as printed on the Placerville sheets)

  • The MVUM is the legal designation instrument under 36 CFR 212.51: motor vehicle use is allowed only on the routes the map designates, for the vehicle classes and seasons shown; the sheet's prohibition text cites 36 CFR 261.54 (state law, traffic signs, and orders also apply).
  • Seasonal routes are printed as open 04/01–12/31 ("…es with a seasonal designation are open from 04/01 to…" on page 1's Travel & Recreation column; the year-end date matches the identical text on the Amador sheets). Separately, the forest-wide dirt-road closure runs Jan 1 through at least Mar 31 and can be extended for wet conditions (FS Road and Trail Status page).
  • Numbering follows the township grid, as on the Amador sheets: the first two digits are the township tier (08N–12N and 17N on these sheets), N = road, E = trail, and trailing letters mark segments/spurs. Y-routes (e.g. 11NY32) are a further sub-family printed on these sheets.
  • Designated-use classes (the MVUM's own vocabulary): Roads Open to Highway Legal Vehicles Only, Roads Open to All Vehicles, each Yearlong or Seasonal; Trails Open to Wheeled Vehicles Only, < 50″ or Less in Width; and Other Public Roads (the two ELD county segments in the table).
  • Sheet layout: Page 1 = front matter, legend, map index, and the Elkins Flat / Gold Note OHV inset map with its own short-routes table — the inset straddles the Placerville/Amador boundary. Page 2 = the main district map (Highway 50 corridor; Mokelumne Wilderness at the lower right) with the main short-routes table block at top-center, printed in four sub-columns.
  • The MVUM prints no district mileage total for Placerville; none is claimed here.

B. The MVUM's own framing

  • Edition: 2024. The MVUM states its designations were made by the responsible official under 36 CFR 212.51, are effective as of the date on the front cover, and remain in effect until superseded by the next year's MVUM. Users are told it is their responsibility to acquire the current map.
  • What the map designates: National Forest System roads and trails open to motorized travel, by vehicle class and season. Vehicle use off designated routes (cross-country travel) is prohibited — motorized travel on the Eldorado has been limited to designated routes since 2008.
  • Designated-use classes printed on the Amador MVUM:
  • Roads Open to All Vehicles — Seasonal / Yearlong
  • Roads Open to Highway Legal Vehicles Only — Seasonal / Yearlong
  • Trails Open to All Vehicles; Trails Open to Wheeled Vehicles 50" or less; Trails Open to Motorcycles Only (seasonal designations)
  • The map also notes Class 1, 2 and 3 e-bikes are allowed on roads designated for all motor vehicles and on trails open to all motor vehicles.
  • Seasonal designation, as printed on the map: "All routes with a seasonal designation are open from 04/01 to 12/31."
  • Forest-wide seasonal closure (separate from the map's designations): all dirt (native-surface) roads and trails on the Eldorado are closed to motor vehicle use January 1 through at least March 31 each year; the closure may be extended for wet conditions (FS Visitor Guide / Gold Note ROG / Road and Trail Status page).
  • How routes are numbered: Eldorado route numbers follow the form township + route number — e.g., 9N17 is road 17 in Township 9 North. On the Amador sheets the townships run 07N–11N. Letter suffixes are branches/spurs or segments of the same road (e.g., North-South Road is 10N83, with segments lettered 10N83B, C, J, M, N, R, S and W). "N" numbers are roads; "E" numbers are trails (the printed tables attach "Roads Open to…" classes to N-numbers and "Trails Open to…" classes to E-numbers). Some major through-roads also carry older short "Forest Road" numbers in FS recreation documents — Ellis Road is cited as Forest Road 92 (= 8N25) and Panther Creek Road as Forest Road 91 (= 8N05).
  • Total designated route miles in the district: not stated on the MVUM. The map gives no district mileage total, and no total is claimed here. (Its printed tables do give exact lengths for the shortest segments — see Section C.)
  • Vehicle-suitability marker used by the forest: per the Eldorado NF OHV page, a vertical route marker is placed on roughly graded roads recommended for high-clearance vehicles, 4WD and pick-ups. The MVUM itself does not print a surface type per road; the best surface evidence in FS documents is the maintenance-level/surfacing table in Source 3 (Section E).

C4. Inventory completeness and limits — read this before using the census

  • 288 unique route numbers were extracted in total: 267 road (N) labels and 21 trail (E) labels, of which 107 segments also have printed lengths/classes (C1).
  • The MVUM map face is a graphic: road names are letter-spaced along curves and mostly do not survive text extraction, and a few route labels printed at awkward angles may likewise be missed or merged. Treat the count as "at least roughly 265 distinct designated road routes shown on the Amador sheets," not as a certified count. A handful of labels near the sheet edges belong to adjoining districts/forests (Georgetown, Placerville, Pacific, Stanislaus, Humboldt-Toiyabe) that the map draws for context.
  • The census therefore = C1 (hard data) + C2 (label-only). It is a starter census: enough to plan drives and to check off routes, but the authoritative check for any single road's class/season is the MVUM map graphic itself (or the ranger district).

Why Palm Sunday

In Thompson's home district of Telemark, Norway, skiers honor him with a memorial run every Palm Sunday. Palm Sunday 2027 is Sunday, March 21, 2027 (Easter Sunday is March 28, 2027 — date and weekday verified). Late March is still real winter at the passes — close to the conditions Thompson crossed in for twenty winters (1856–1876), carrying the mail ~90 miles from Placerville to Genoa on ten-foot oak skis.

The concept

Retrace the mail run by road: start in Placerville, where Thompson reported to the post office five mornings running to prove he meant it; cross the Sierra; end in Genoa, Nevada — his statue at Mormon Station, his courthouse, his grave. Along the way, participants carry the mail: commemorative envelopes/postcards picked up in Placerville, carried over the top in a mail sack, and mailed from the Genoa post office at the end.

Route — outbound (Placerville → Genoa, via US-50 / Luther Pass)

99.1 mi · OSRM 2.4 h pure driving (optimistic) · plan 2¾–3 h in March conditions, before stops

  1. Placerville — Main Street start; mail pickup at the start point (historic post office site).
  2. US-50 east — Echo Summit (7,382 ft) — Meyers.
  3. SR-89 south — Luther Pass (7,740 ft) — down into Hope Valley (Thompson traversed it on every crossing).
  4. Woodfords — Diamond Valley below, where Thompson homesteaded 160 acres in 1860.
  5. SR-88 east → US-395 north → Genoa, NV.

Route — return (Genoa → Placerville, via Hwy 88 west)

131.7 mi · OSRM 3.3 h · plan 3½–4 h — flagged honestly: the return is ~33 mi longer than the outbound.

  1. Genoa → US-395 south → SR-88 west — Woodfords, Hope Valley.
  2. Carson Pass (8,574 ft) summit stop — a Thompson monument stands along Hwy 88 in the pass country (per the story page, on one of his later routes).
  3. Kirkwood / Caples Lake country.
  4. Ham's Station (34950 Hwy 88, Pioneer) — story subject in its own right.
  5. Cook's Station (31950 Hwy 88, Pioneer) — story subject in its own right.
  6. Pioneer → Jackson → SR-49 north → Placerville.

Carson Pass placement — recommendation

The summit is only ~7 mi west of Hope Valley (Pickett's Junction). Two ways to include it: - Option A — outbound spur: from Hope Valley, up to Carson Pass and back before continuing to Genoa. Adds ~13–14 mi and ~30–40 min including the stop. Puts the summit before Genoa, matching the "east over the Sierra, Carson Pass stop, end in Genoa" sketch. - Option B — return-leg stop (RECOMMENDED): the Hwy 88 return runs through Carson Pass anyway — summit stop there, zero backtracking, and it strings the pass, Ham's, and Cook's into one westbound story sequence.

  • Full loop Placerville → Placerville: ~231 mi, ~5.7 h OSRM pure driving → 6½–7½ h realistic in late-March conditions, plus ~2½–3 h of stops (Genoa program, pass stop, stations, fuel) = a 9–10½ hour Placerville day.
  • Dave's own positioning: cabin → Placerville is 32 mi / ~1 h each way (OSRM 59 min), making his full day ~295 mi. Peel-off option: on the return, Dave (and any Fiddletown folks) can split at Jackson — ~25 min home to Fiddletown — instead of riding back up SR-49 to Placerville.

"Carry the mail" — mechanics and the Sunday problem

  • Commemorative envelopes/postcards, stamped and addressed (to participants themselves, or to friends), handed out in Placerville, carried in a mail sack in the lead car, dropped in Genoa. A printed cachet on each envelope ("Carried over the Sierra in the spirit of Snowshoe Thompson — Palm Sunday, March 21, 2027") is a design detail for later.
  • The catch: Palm Sunday is a Sunday, and the Genoa Post Office (2244 Foothill Rd, Genoa, NV 89411; (775) 782-0552) is closed Sundays — retail hours Mon–Fri 8:30–5:00, Sat 9:00–1:00. The lobby/collection box is accessible (24-hour lobby per USPS listings), so the mail CAN be dropped in Genoa on the day — but it will be collected Monday and postmarked Monday, March 22, not the 21st.
  • Options: (a) accept the box drop + Monday postmark (the carrying is the ceremony); (b) ask the Genoa postmaster in advance whether a special hand-cancel is possible for the event — small-town post offices sometimes accommodate; no promise; (c) mail a token subset Saturday from Genoa by a volunteer — weakest option, splits the ceremony. Dave's call.

Road Trip Buddy — Job Req

Role summary

Road Trip Buddy keeps Dave's register of great drives and the stops that make them worth taking. Every road-trip idea Dave stumbles on — a reel, a link, a tip from a friend — gets captured, checked just enough to be useful, and filed in one living register of roads and stops, each with its source, its best season, and its rough distance from home. When a trip firms up, the raw material for a route is already sitting there, organized and honest about what is actually known. The register is the product: two kinds of entries — great roads and worthy stops — linked into candidate loops, kept current on the buddy's homepage, and handed to Travel Buddy as a route skeleton when Dave picks a trip.

Reports to

Travel Buddy (parent buddy); Dave via Astro. Sibling in spirit to Sports Car Adventures — Dave's rally and vintage-driving world feeds this register, but this list is Dave's personal one.

Responsibilities

  • Keep the Road Trip Register: every entry carries its source link, why it earned a place, best season, rough distance from Fiddletown, and a status (idea → shortlisted → routed → done).
  • Triage every reel, link, or mention Dave flags as a road-trip idea: identify the road or place, verify the basics against a real source, and file the entry the same day.
  • Link roads and stops into candidate loops — a road anchored by its stops, stops that pair into a weekend — and flag the natural combinations.
  • Keep the homepage register current and visual; the homepage is the register's front door and must always match KNOWLEDGE.md.
  • When Dave firms up a trip, hand Travel Buddy a ready route skeleton: roads, stops, season check, rough mileage.
  • Keep KNOWLEDGE.md as the register's durable record and log every status change in DECISIONS.md.

Success criteria

  • Every idea Dave forwards is in the register within a day, with a working source link.
  • No entry without a season note and a distance estimate — Dave can judge feasibility at a glance.
  • Homepage register and KNOWLEDGE.md never disagree.
  • Zero invented details: anything unverified is marked unknown, never filled in.

  • No booking or buying anything — reservations and purchases stay with Travel Buddy and Dave.
  • No day-by-day itineraries until Dave actually picks a trip.
  • No Sports Car Adventures client work — this register is personal.
