### Roads
- **Million Dollar Highway (US 550), Colorado** — Ouray → Silverton → Durango over Red Mountain Pass (11,018 ft). Shelf road, no guardrails in stretches; one of the great American drives. Best window: late spring through fall; winter is serious mountain driving. ~950 miles from Fiddletown — a Colorado-loop trip, not a weekend. Status: idea. Source: https://www.facebook.com/reel/4265069620303960/ (This World Traveled, Sep 23, 2026 — flagged by Dave Oct 30). Also named in the "8 scenic roads" reel, Aug 19, 2026: https://www.facebook.com/reel/1537903230931156/ (relayed Nov 5, 2026).
- † **US Route 89 — "The National Park Highway"** — Flagstaff, AZ to the Canadian border in Montana. Backbone for 10 national parks: Grand Canyon and Petrified Forest (AZ); Zion, Bryce Canyon, Capitol Reef, Canyonlands, and Arches (UT); Grand Teton and Yellowstone (WY); Glacier (MT). A NatGeo Traveler Top 10 drivers' drive. Suggested use: early fall or 2027, or drive it in stretches rather than end-to-end. Distance from Fiddletown: not yet noted. Status: idea. Source: https://www.facebook.com/reel/1043514015336263/ (Luke Kelly — sent by Dave Nov 4, 2026). Second source (filed Nov 11, 2026): The Traveling Dans, "1 highway, 10 national parks" (posted Nov 4, 2026) — https://facebook.com/reel/874889135687370/ — bills US 89 as America's most scenic road trip; same 10 parks (Glacier, Yellowstone, Grand Teton, Arches, Canyonlands, Capitol Reef, Bryce Canyon, Zion, Grand Canyon, Petrified Forest) plus also-named: Cedar Breaks National Monument, Kodachrome Basin, Grand Staircase–Escalante National Monument, Montezuma Castle National Monument, Walnut Canyon National Monument, Wupatki National Monument, Sunset Crater National Monument. Creator's guidance: best window August to early October, driven NORTH to SOUTH (warmer parks later in the fall), at least 2 weeks for parks plus drive time. (Caption content as relayed by Astro — Facebook exposed the caption only, not the narration.)

### Stops
- **Ouray, Colorado — "Switzerland of America"** — south end of the Million Dollar Highway, San Juan Mountains. Box Canyon Falls, Ouray Hot Springs, Cascade Falls, Ouray Brewery, Switzerland of America lookout, Ouray Ice Park (winter ice climbing). Best window: late spring–fall (Ice Park is the winter exception). Status: idea. Source: same reel as above.
- **Black Canyon of the Gunnison National Park, Colorado** — ~1 hr from Ouray; natural loop partner. Status: idea.
- **Silverton & Durango, Colorado** — the far end of the Million Dollar Highway; loop partners with Ouray. Status: idea.
- **Telluride (via Ridgway) & Mesa Verde, Colorado** — Colorado-loop partners. Status: idea.

Status: idea. Source: https://www.facebook.com/reel/1636938344435534/ (Crystal Wanderlust, Sep 11, 2026 — sent by Dave Oct 29).
- † **Burney Falls, McArthur-Burney Falls Memorial State Park, near Burney, Shasta County, CA** — 129-foot spring-fed falls flowing ~100M gallons/day year-round; Theodore Roosevelt's "Eighth Wonder of the World"; ~1.3-mile loop trail to the pool. Season: year-round flow. Distance from Fiddletown: not yet noted. Status: idea. Source: https://www.facebook.com/reel/1761934814848980/ (Mike Herrera, family adventure — sent by Dave Nov 4, 2026).
- ‡ **Monument Valley, AZ/UT line** — filed as named in the reel; pairs with Moki Dugway and Valley of the Gods in the Utah cluster. Status: idea. Source: https://www.facebook.com/reel/2272816953491966/ (Anna & Nitin — relayed Nov 5, 2026).
- ‡ **Valley of the Gods, near Mexican Hat, Utah** — a miniature Monument Valley on a dirt loop, at the foot of the Moki Dugway. Status: idea. Source: same Anna & Nitin reel.
- ‡ **Lamar Valley, Yellowstone National Park, Wyoming** — Yellowstone's wildlife valley; pairs with Teton Road at the north end of US 89. Status: idea. Source: same Anna & Nitin reel.
- † **The Dutch Flat Hotel, Dutch Flat, CA** — historic Gold Country hotel, 1 mile off I-80 at exit 145, on the Tahoe run. Filed as a detour stop, not a destination: the pitch is the food (burger, loaded baked potato, beer) as a reason to pull off. Season/distance: not yet noted. Status: idea. Source: https://www.facebook.com/reel/3475187862659738/ (the hotel's own reel — sent by Dave Nov 4, 2026).
- 🚂 **Railtown 1897 State Historic Park, Jamestown, CA** (10501 Reservoir Rd; ~45 min south of the cabin via Hwy 49) — the Sierra Railway's original shops and roundhouse, one of the last working steam roundhouses in the US: guided roundhouse tours (included with admission; call (209) 984-3953 for the day's times), belt-driven machine shop, working turntable, Sierra No. 3 (1891) and No. 28 (1922), 200+ film/TV productions shot there. Winter hours Nov–Mar: park open 10 AM–3 PM; regular excursion trains run Apr–Oct weekends (Sat/Sun departures 11 AM–3 PM). Seasonal special: THE POLAR EXPRESS, Nov 27–Dec 20, Fri/Sat/Sun only, departures 4:30 / 6:00 / 7:30 PM, $85 (per a Nov 11, 2026 holiday-trains roundup). Admission $5 adults / $3 youth. Status: idea — reference for future outings, nothing booked. Source: railtown1897.org; research relayed by Astro Nov 12, 2026 (Dave asked for Jamestown railroad info in main chat).
- 🚂 **Yosemite Mountain Sugar Pine Railroad, Fish Camp, CA** (56001 CA-41; ~2.5 hrs from the cabin, 10 min from Yosemite's south entrance) — narrow-gauge logging railroad, Shay steam locomotives, open-air log cars. Logger Steam Train: 1-hour narrated 4-mile round trip, daily in season (Apr–Oct), departures up to 4×/day (9:30, 11:00, 1:00, 2:30). Seasonal special: HOLIDAY MAGIC TRAIN, select dates Nov 20–Dec 30, departures 4:00 / 5:30 / 7:00 PM, adults from $63.60, dogs welcome, hot cocoa included. Moonlight Special dinner train is summer only. Status: idea — reference for future outings, nothing booked. Source: ymsprr.com, (559) 683-7273; research relayed by Astro Nov 12, 2026.

# GPX format for Dave's inRoute — playbook

*Learned Nov 19, 2026 — decoded by Lenwood Gazette Buddy from Dave's own inRoute export (creator "inRoute-v9.0.9"): Dave imported a draft GPX, set the intermediate points as vias in the app, and re-exported. Transferred to Road Trip Buddy at Dave's direction, Nov 19, 2026. **Use this format for every GPX built for Dave from now on.***

## The native inRoute format

1. **Namespace:** the `<gpx>` element carries the Garmin TripExtensions namespace:
   `xmlns:trp="http://www.garmin.com/xmlschemas/TripExtensions/v1"`
2. **The route is an `<rte>` of `<rtept>` points**, each carrying `<extensions>`:
   - **True endpoints (start/finish):** `<trp:ViaPoint/>`
   - **Shaping points** — what Dave sets as "vias" in the app. They steer the route along chosen roads but are **NOT announced during navigation**: `<trp:ShapingPoint/>`, and the point also carries `<time>1969-12-31T23:59:59Z</time>` (null-time marker).
3. **An `<rte>` alone is enough for import** — inRoute adds it as the route (≤150 points auto-adds; that is also Dave's standing cap per file). inRoute's own exports additionally include a `<wpt>` copy of every map pin and a `<trk>` of the drawn route, but **neither is needed in files we author**.
4. **NOT the format:** `<type>via</type>` — that's the Locus/Cruiser convention and inRoute ignores it.

## Route-building lesson (Dave, Nov 19, 2026)

- **POI-only waypoints remain right for point-to-point road routes** — inRoute draws a good route between distant POIs (Dave's standing rule since Nov 7, 2026).
- **But when the specific streets ARE the route** (a neighborhood crawl, a scenic loop), sparse pins fail — inRoute shortcuts and zigzags between them. Then: **thread the intended streets with a shaping point at every turn** so each leg is a single-street run, built from real street geometry (OpenStreetMap junction data works well).
- Total points per file still ≤150 (Dave's cap, Nov 15, 2026).

### Commit points (addendum, Nov 19, 2026 — Dave caught this from a screenshot of an imported route)

- A shaping point placed exactly **AT a junction**, on the road you're already traveling, does **NOT** force a turn — the router rolls through it and can reach the next point by staying on the through road. (In the Willow Glen route, the Glen Brook Ave → Glen Echo Ave turn was skipped exactly this way: the point sat on Glen Brook at the junction, and Glen Echo's far end was reachable via Glen Brook + Willow St.)
- **Fix:** place each turn's shaping point **~30 m PAST the junction, ON the street being turned onto** — a "commit point" — computed from the street's real geometry (OpenStreetMap way geometry works).
- **Sanity check for every turn:** if the next point is reachable without making the turn, the turn is unpinned — move its commit point onto the target street.
- **Short legs (addendum 2, Nov 19, 2026):** on a very short connector street (~80 m, e.g. Glen Dell Dr between Camino Ramon and Camino Pablo), the drawn route can still kink at the corner even with commit points. Fix: add a **mid-block shaping point on the short street itself**, and push the **next street's commit point further past the corner (~55 m)** so it sits unambiguously on the new street.
- **Off-roadway points (addendum 3, Nov 19, 2026 — from Dave's screenshot review):** a shaping point computed from one map's geometry can land off the roadway in the router's map data — it draws as a jog off the street (happened with a point 25 m up Camino Pablo computed from OpenStreetMap geometry). **If a point draws off-street, drop it and let the next on-street point carry the turn.**
- **Long runs (addendum 3):** long single-street runs (~700 m) get **mid-run shaping points every ~250 m** so the router can't shortcut via parallel streets.

- **What the map designates:** National Forest System roads and trails open
  to motorized travel, by vehicle class and season. Vehicle use off
  designated routes (cross-country travel) is prohibited — motorized travel
  on the Eldorado has been limited to designated routes since 2008.
- **Designated-use classes printed on the Georgetown MVUM:**
  - Roads Open to All Vehicles — Seasonal / Yearlong
  - Roads Open to Highway Legal Vehicles Only — Seasonal / Yearlong
  - Trails Open to All Vehicles; Trails Open to Wheeled Vehicles Only
    < 50"; Trails Open to Motorcycles Only (seasonal/yearlong designations)
  - The map also notes Class 1, 2 and 3 e-bikes are allowed on roads
    designated for all motor vehicles and on trails open to all motor
    vehicles.
- **Seasonal designation, as printed on the map:** "All routes with a
  seasonal designation are open from 04/01 to 12/31." The Georgetown sheets
  additionally print a **Seasonal Designation table** naming two route
  groups:
  - **04/01 – 12/31:** 11E53, 11NY19, 12N56, 12N59, 12N59B, 12N60C, 12N60P,
    13N58, 13N60
  - **05/02 – 11/09:** 11E36, 11E37, 11E38, 11N81, 11N89, 11N92, 11N92A,
    12N81B, 12N81E, 12NY01 — the Rock Creek system routes covered by the
    Critical Deer Winter Range closure (see Source 4; the FS Road and Trail
    Status page records the Nov 10–May 1 prohibition on the southern Rock
    Creek roads/trails, with exceptions for Darling Ridge Road 12N80,
    Mosquito Road 12N60, Rock Creek Road 12N70, Slate Mountain Mine Road
    12N83, Slate Mountain Road 12N76 and Lower Rock Creek Trail).
  - The Rock Creek system also has its **own wet-weather closure plan** and
    does not fall under the forest-wide seasonal dirt closure (FS Road and
    Trail Status page).
- **Forest-wide seasonal closure (separate from the map's designations):**
  all dirt (native-surface) roads and trails on the Eldorado are closed to
  motor vehicle use **January 1 through at least March 31** each year; the
  closure may be extended for wet conditions (FS Road and Trail Status
  page).
- **How routes are numbered:** Eldorado route numbers follow the form
  *township + route number* — e.g., **12N70** is road 70 in Township 12
  North. On the Georgetown sheets the townships run 10N–14N, with a cluster
  of 17N labels at the northern sheet edge (see Section C4). Letter suffixes
  are branches/spurs or segments of the same road. **"N" numbers are roads;
  "E" numbers are trails.** A Rock Creek-area peculiarity: several routes
  carry a **"Y" inside the number** (11NY19, 12NY01, and 12NY21/12NY26 in
  other FS/USGS documents for this same area) — these are reproduced as
  printed. Some major through-roads also carry older short "Forest Road"
  numbers or "Primary Route" numbers in FS recreation documents — Eleven
  Pines Road is **Forest Road 2** (= 14N08) and Wentworth Springs Road is
  **Primary Forest Route 1** (and county road **ELD-63**).
- **Total designated route miles in the district:** **not stated on the
  MVUM.** The map gives no district mileage total, and no total is claimed
  here. (Its printed tables do give exact lengths for the shortest
  segments — see Section C.)
- **Vehicle-suitability marker used by the forest:** per the Eldorado NF
  OHV page, a vertical route marker is placed on roughly graded roads
  recommended for high-clearance vehicles, 4WD and pick-ups. The MVUM
  itself does not print a surface type per road, and no Georgetown-district
  FS document found pairs road names with surfacing/maintenance levels the
  way the Panther EA does for Amador — so surface and maintenance level are
  "not established" for most roads in Section D.

  Wentworth Springs' northern end) at the Tahoe NF / Pacific RD edge, and
  the **10N40 family** (10N40C, 10N40M, 10N40N) at the Placerville RD edge.
  They are extracted from this district's sheets and are counted above,
  but a route-by-route check against the adjoining district's MVUM is
  needed before treating the 346 as a certified Georgetown-only count.
  Read the total as "roughly 345 distinct designated road routes shown on
  the Georgetown sheets."
- The MVUM map face is a graphic: road names are letter-spaced along
  curves and mostly do **not** survive text extraction, and a few route
  labels printed at awkward angles or split by the text layer (e.g.,
  "12N 70"-style splits) may likewise be missed or merged. Two labels the
  FS status page establishes for this district — **12N76** (Slate Mountain
  Road) and **12N83** (Slate Mountain Mine Road; segment 12N83D *is*
  tabulated in C1) — did not survive extraction as base numbers, which
  proves the label pass is incomplete at the margins.
- **Suspect labels** (reproduced as printed; verify against the paper map
  before relying on them): **13N31AN** (tabulated; the "AN" suffix is
  unusual), **11NY19** and **12NY01** (mid-number "Y" — an attested Rock
  Creek-area pattern, see Section B, but still worth a paper check),
  **17N12QA / 17N12QB**, and **12N68** (tabulated at 52 ft — plausible for
  a connector segment, but the shortest row on the sheet).
- The census therefore = C1 (hard data) + C2 (label-only). It is a
  *starter* census: enough to plan drives and to check off routes, but the
  authoritative check for any single road's class/season is the MVUM map
  graphic itself (or the ranger district).

## B. The MVUM's own framing

- **Edition:** 2024. As on the Amador sheets, the designations were made by
  the responsible official under 36 CFR 212.51, are effective as of the date
  on the front cover, and remain in effect until superseded by the next
  year's MVUM. Users are told it is their responsibility to acquire the
  current map.
- **What the map designates:** National Forest System roads and trails open
  to motorized travel, by vehicle class and season. Vehicle use off
  designated routes (cross-country travel) is prohibited — motorized travel
  on the Eldorado has been limited to designated routes since 2008.
- **Designated-use classes printed on the Pacific MVUM** (the road classes
  match the Amador sheet; the Pacific tables add one more):
  - Roads Open to All Vehicles — Seasonal / Yearlong
  - Roads Open to Highway Legal Vehicles Only — Seasonal / Yearlong
  - **Other Public Roads** — the class the Pacific tables print for the
    ELD-numbered corridor segments (Section C1); these are designated routes
    carried under "ELD" identifiers rather than township route numbers.
  - Trails Open to All Vehicles; Trails Open to Wheeled Vehicles 50" or
    less; Trails Open to Motorcycles Only (seasonal designations)
- **Seasonal designation, as printed on the map:** "All routes with a
  seasonal designation are open from 04/01 to 12/31." (No other date range
  appears in the Pacific sheets' extracted text.)
- **Forest-wide seasonal closure (separate from the map's designations):**
  all dirt (native-surface) roads and trails on the Eldorado are closed to
  motor vehicle use **January 1 through at least March 31** each year; the
  closure may be extended for wet conditions (FS Visitor Guide / Road and
  Trail Status page).
- **How routes are numbered:** as on the Amador sheets, Eldorado route
  numbers follow the form *township + route number* — e.g., **13N17** is
  road 17 in Township 13 North. On the Pacific sheets the townships run
  10N–17N for roads and 12E–17E for trails. Letter suffixes are
  branches/spurs or segments of the same road (Loon Lake Campground Road is
  13N17, with segments 13N17A, 13N17BA–BF on the map). **"N" numbers are
  roads; "E" numbers are trails.**
- **Two numbering quirks unique to these sheets (flagged, not explained
  away):**
  - **"Y" identifiers.** The Pacific tables print a family of route numbers
    with a **Y between the township and the route number** — 11NY32,
    12NY04, 12NY15D, 17NY12H and more; **24 distinct Y-forms** in all
    (Sections C1/C2). They are
    tabulated exactly like other routes. Meanwhile the map face also carries
    the corresponding labels *without* the Y (e.g. "11N32" appears as a
    map-face label while the table prints "11NY32"). This census keeps the
    two spellings separate, exactly as printed, and does **not** merge them
    — whether they denote the same physical routes is not established by any
    source consulted.
  - **"ELD" identifiers.** The principal Crystal Basin corridors are carried
    in the tables as **ELD-63-A…F** (Wentworth Springs corridor segments)
    and **ELD-1593, ELD-2130, ELD-2214**, all classed "Other Public Roads".
    The FS status page (Source 2) uses the same scheme: ELD-63 = Wentworth
    Springs Road, ELD-147 = Ice House Road.
- **Older short "Forest Road" numbers** also circulate in FS recreation
  documents for this district: Ice House Road is **Forest Road 3**
  ("Primary Forest Route 3"); Wrights Lake Road is **Forest Road 4**; the
  Wrights Lake Tie is **Forest Road 32** in campground directions; Eleven
  Pines Road is **Forest Road 2** (= 14N08).
- **Total designated route miles in the district:** **not stated on the
  MVUM.** The map gives no district mileage total, and no total is claimed
  here. (Its printed tables do give exact lengths for the shortest
  segments — see Section C.)
- **Vehicle-suitability marker used by the forest:** per the Eldorado NF
  OHV page (Source 7), a vertical route marker is placed on roughly graded
  roads recommended for high-clearance vehicles, 4WD and pick-ups. The MVUM
  itself does not print a surface type per road, and — unlike the Amador
  district, where the Panther EA supplied a name/surface table — **no FS
  document found for the Pacific district pairs road names with surfacing
  or maintenance levels**, so surfaces below are "not established" except
  where a source states them outright.

### What the PDF actually contains

Where the Amador MVUM prints one short-route table per sheet (threshold
0.25 miles), the Pacific MVUM prints **four** short-route tables with
**three different thresholds**:

|---|---|---|---|

| Inset 1 Map | 1 | Routes Less Than 0.6 Miles (3,168 feet) | 51 |
| Inset 2 Map | 1 | Routes Less Than 0.25 Miles (1,320 feet) | 24 |
| Inset 3 Map | 1 | Routes Less Than 0.25 Miles (1,320 feet) | 15 |

The tables overlap heavily — the same segment is re-listed in every table
whose map covers it — so the census counts **unique segments** (route +
length + designated use), not raw rows. Longer designated roads appear
**only as labels on the map face**; there is no printed name/length table
for them on the MVUM.

Extraction method: both PDF sheets were downloaded and parsed **two
independent ways** — (1) `pdftotext -bbox` word coordinates, reconstructing
each table row by column position (route, length, designated-use phrase,
tolerating stray map-face words interleaved in the text layer), and (2) a
`pypdf` reading-order text parse matched against the same route/length/class
pattern. The two parses were reconciled row by row; every discrepancy found
along the way was traced to a specific text-layer artifact and resolved
(see C4), and the finished cross-check (`pypdf_check.log`) shows the two
methods' final segment sets are **identical — 238 segments, no differences
either way**. A second internal consistency check also passes: each table's
longest extracted segment sits just under its own printed threshold (main
2,634 ≤ 2,640 ft; Inset 1 3,115 ≤ 3,168 ft; Inset 2 1,304 ≤ 1,320 ft;
Inset 3 1,165 ≤ 1,320 ft).

### C4. Inventory completeness and limits — read this before using the census

- **301 unique route numbers** were extracted in total: **287 road (N and
  ELD) labels** and **14 trail (E) labels**, of which 230 route numbers
  also have printed lengths/classes (C1). Headline census numbers: **287
  designated road routes and 14 designated trails shown on the Pacific
  sheets, 238 of their short segments tabulated with lengths and classes.**
- **Suspect labels (flagged individually):**
  - **ELD-1, ELD-14, ELD-16** — extracted at sheet-edge/vicinity positions
    and almost certainly fragments of longer ELD designations (or of
    adjoining-area labels) rather than complete route numbers. They are
    included in the 287 count for transparency; the defensible road total
    is **284–287**.
  - **13N22CB (map face) vs 13N22CB1 (tabulated, 498 ft)** — the face label
    may be the same label truncated by extraction, or a parent route of
    CB1. Counted as extracted (both), flagged here.
  - **The "Y" spellings** (Section B): table spellings like 11NY32 and
    map-face spellings like 11N32 are counted as extracted — separately.
    If every Y-form is the same route as its no-Y counterpart, the unique
    road total would drop by up to ~24. No source consulted resolves this;
    the on-the-ground check is the printed MVUM graphic itself.
- **Known routes whose numbers do not extract at all:** the plain tokens
  "11N26" (Wrights Lake Road — only its segments 11N26A/B extract),
  "ELD-147" (Ice House Road's status-page designation), "11N60" (Jaybird
  Canyon) and the trail "16E21" (Barrett Lake) appear in FS documents but
  not in the MVUM text layer; they are recorded in Sections D/C3 from
  those documents and are **not** in the 301 count.
- The MVUM map face is a graphic: road names are letter-spaced along
  curves and mostly do **not** survive text extraction, and a few route
  labels printed at awkward angles may likewise be missed or merged. A
  handful of labels near the sheet edges belong to adjoining districts
  (Georgetown, Placerville, Lake Tahoe Basin Management Unit) that the map
  draws for context.
- The census therefore = C1 (hard data) + C2 (label-only), cross-checked
  by two independent extraction methods. It is a *starter* census: enough
  to plan drives and to check off routes, but the authoritative check for
  any single road's class/season is the MVUM map graphic itself (or the
  ranger district).

## B. Framing (as printed on the Placerville sheets)

- The MVUM is the legal designation instrument under **36 CFR 212.51**: motor vehicle use is allowed only on the routes the map designates, for the vehicle classes and seasons shown; the sheet's prohibition text cites **36 CFR 261.54** (state law, traffic signs, and orders also apply).
- **Seasonal routes are printed as open 04/01–12/31** ("…es with a seasonal designation are open from 04/01 to…" on page 1's Travel & Recreation column; the year-end date matches the identical text on the Amador sheets). Separately, the forest-wide **dirt-road closure runs Jan 1 through at least Mar 31** and can be extended for wet conditions (FS Road and Trail Status page).
- **Numbering follows the township grid**, as on the Amador sheets: the first two digits are the township tier (08N–12N and 17N on these sheets), **N = road, E = trail**, and trailing letters mark segments/spurs. Y-routes (e.g. 11NY32) are a further sub-family printed on these sheets.
- **Designated-use classes** (the MVUM's own vocabulary): *Roads Open to Highway Legal Vehicles Only*, *Roads Open to All Vehicles*, each *Yearlong* or *Seasonal*; *Trails Open to Wheeled Vehicles Only, < 50″ or Less in Width*; and *Other Public Roads* (the two ELD county segments in the table).
- Sheet layout: **Page 1** = front matter, legend, map index, and the **Elkins Flat / Gold Note OHV inset map** with its own short-routes table — the inset straddles the Placerville/Amador boundary. **Page 2** = the main district map (Highway 50 corridor; Mokelumne Wilderness at the lower right) with the main short-routes table block at top-center, printed in four sub-columns.
- The MVUM prints **no district mileage total** for Placerville; none is claimed here.

## B. The MVUM's own framing

- **Edition:** 2024. The MVUM states its designations were made by the
  responsible official under 36 CFR 212.51, are effective as of the date on
  the front cover, and remain in effect until superseded by the next year's
  MVUM. Users are told it is their responsibility to acquire the current map.
- **What the map designates:** National Forest System roads and trails open
  to motorized travel, by vehicle class and season. Vehicle use off
  designated routes (cross-country travel) is prohibited — motorized travel
  on the Eldorado has been limited to designated routes since 2008.
- **Designated-use classes printed on the Amador MVUM:**
  - Roads Open to All Vehicles — Seasonal / Yearlong
  - Roads Open to Highway Legal Vehicles Only — Seasonal / Yearlong
  - Trails Open to All Vehicles; Trails Open to Wheeled Vehicles 50" or
    less; Trails Open to Motorcycles Only (seasonal designations)
  - The map also notes Class 1, 2 and 3 e-bikes are allowed on roads
    designated for all motor vehicles and on trails open to all motor
    vehicles.
- **Seasonal designation, as printed on the map:** "All routes with a
  seasonal designation are open from 04/01 to 12/31."
- **Forest-wide seasonal closure (separate from the map's designations):**
  all dirt (native-surface) roads and trails on the Eldorado are closed to
  motor vehicle use **January 1 through at least March 31** each year; the
  closure may be extended for wet conditions (FS Visitor Guide / Gold Note
  ROG / Road and Trail Status page).
- **How routes are numbered:** Eldorado route numbers follow the form
  *township + route number* — e.g., **9N17** is road 17 in Township 9 North.
  On the Amador sheets the townships run 07N–11N. Letter suffixes are
  branches/spurs or segments of the same road (e.g., North-South Road is
  10N83, with segments lettered 10N83B, C, J, M, N, R, S and W). **"N"
  numbers are roads; "E" numbers are trails** (the printed tables attach
  "Roads Open to…" classes to N-numbers and "Trails Open to…" classes to
  E-numbers). Some major through-roads also carry older short "Forest Road"
  numbers in FS recreation documents — Ellis Road is cited as **Forest
  Road 92** (= 8N25) and Panther Creek Road as **Forest Road 91** (= 8N05).
- **Total designated route miles in the district:** **not stated on the
  MVUM.** The map gives no district mileage total, and no total is claimed
  here. (Its printed tables do give exact lengths for the shortest
  segments — see Section C.)
- **Vehicle-suitability marker used by the forest:** per the Eldorado NF
  OHV page, a vertical route marker is placed on roughly graded roads
  recommended for high-clearance vehicles, 4WD and pick-ups. The MVUM
  itself does not print a surface type per road; the best surface evidence
  in FS documents is the maintenance-level/surfacing table in Source 3
  (Section E).

# Snowshoe Thompson Commemorative Drive — Palm Sunday, March 21, 2027

**Status: DRAFT (Nov 15, 2026)** — route + day plan developed by Road Trip Buddy at Dave's request. Scale/audience decision is Dave's (being made in the main chat); the route is built to work for a small friends-and-neighbors run, a family day, or a Sports Car Adventures event.
Story: [The Snowshoe Thompson Story](https://davedellaquila.github.io/stories/snowshoe-thompson/)

## Why Palm Sunday
In Thompson's home district of Telemark, Norway, skiers honor him with a memorial run **every Palm Sunday**. Palm Sunday 2027 is **Sunday, March 21, 2027** (Easter Sunday is March 28, 2027 — date and weekday verified). Late March is still real winter at the passes — close to the conditions Thompson crossed in for twenty winters (1856–1876), carrying the mail ~90 miles from Placerville to Genoa on ten-foot oak skis.

## The concept
Retrace the mail run by road: start in **Placerville**, where Thompson reported to the post office five mornings running to prove he meant it; cross the Sierra; end in **Genoa, Nevada** — his statue at Mormon Station, his courthouse, his grave. Along the way, participants **carry the mail**: commemorative envelopes/postcards picked up in Placerville, carried over the top in a mail sack, and mailed from the Genoa post office at the end.

## Route — outbound (Placerville → Genoa, via US-50 / Luther Pass)
99.1 mi · OSRM 2.4 h pure driving (optimistic) · **plan 2¾–3 h in March conditions, before stops**

1. **Placerville** — Main Street start; mail pickup at the start point (historic post office site).
2. US-50 east — **Echo Summit (7,382 ft)** — Meyers.
3. SR-89 south — **Luther Pass (7,740 ft)** — down into **Hope Valley** (Thompson traversed it on every crossing).
4. **Woodfords** — Diamond Valley below, where Thompson homesteaded 160 acres in 1860.
5. SR-88 east → US-395 north → **Genoa, NV**.

## Route — return (Genoa → Placerville, via Hwy 88 west)
131.7 mi · OSRM 3.3 h · **plan 3½–4 h** — flagged honestly: the return is ~33 mi longer than the outbound.

1. Genoa → US-395 south → SR-88 west — Woodfords, Hope Valley.
2. **Carson Pass (8,574 ft)** summit stop — a Thompson monument stands along Hwy 88 in the pass country (per the story page, on one of his later routes).
3. Kirkwood / Caples Lake country.
4. **Ham's Station** (34950 Hwy 88, Pioneer) — story subject in its own right.
5. **Cook's Station** (31950 Hwy 88, Pioneer) — story subject in its own right.
6. Pioneer → Jackson → SR-49 north → **Placerville**.

### Carson Pass placement — recommendation
The summit is only ~7 mi west of Hope Valley (Pickett's Junction). Two ways to include it:
- **Option A — outbound spur:** from Hope Valley, up to Carson Pass and back before continuing to Genoa. Adds ~13–14 mi and ~30–40 min including the stop. Puts the summit before Genoa, matching the "east over the Sierra, Carson Pass stop, end in Genoa" sketch.
- **Option B — return-leg stop (RECOMMENDED):** the Hwy 88 return runs through Carson Pass anyway — summit stop there, zero backtracking, and it strings the pass, Ham's, and Cook's into one westbound story sequence.

## The honest day-size math

# Road Trip Buddy — Job Req

## Role summary

Road Trip Buddy keeps Dave's register of great drives and the stops that make them worth taking. Every road-trip idea Dave stumbles on — a reel, a link, a tip from a friend — gets captured, checked just enough to be useful, and filed in one living register of roads and stops, each with its source, its best season, and its rough distance from home.
