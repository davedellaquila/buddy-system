Bank sync pipeline

THE MOAThard to copycore business value

What this is

The Money Buddy pipeline: bank connections, transaction sync, categorization, and the statement-ready import flow. It turns raw financial chaos into the one screen Dave actually trusts.

Pipelines like this are earned — through edge cases, bank quirks, and months of reconciliation pain no clone can shortcut.

Why it sits here on the chart

Firmly in the moat: hard to copy because the value is in the accumulated handling of real-world mess — every bank's CSV dialect, every categorization rule learned from Dave's actual spending. High on the value axis: for Money Buddy, the pipeline is the product.

Large bubble: load-bearing asset number two.

Current risk \u0026 defensibility

Risk: medium-high. Financial data is the most sensitive category in the system, and the pipeline touches credentials-adjacent material (imported statements, transaction histories). A leak here is worse than a leak anywhere else.

Copying risk is low — but dependency risk is real: the pipeline leans on bank export formats and (eventually) aggregation APIs that can change without notice.

Guardian\u2019s recommendation

Keep it server-side and keep it boring. No financial data in client-side code, no credentials in the browser, imports validated before they touch the store. The pipeline's defensibility comes from its history with Dave's data — protect the data, and the moat maintains itself.

Next action: Dave's standing rule — no new financial connections until he picks the import method. When he does, the connection review goes through Guardian first.

Guardian\u2019s working assessment \u2014 judgment calls, not measured data.
