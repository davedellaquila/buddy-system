Client-side UI craft

THE HUSKeasy to copymoderate business value

What this is

The dashboard's look and feel — Jonny's Kin design language, the Atlas lenses, the attention tiles, the micro-interactions. This is the craft layer: the part Dave actually sees and touches every day.

It is also, inevitably, the part competitors see first. Everything visible can be imitated.

Why it sits here on the chart

Right of center: easy to copy. A competent team can reproduce the visual language in weeks — taste is hard, but pixels are cheap. Mid-chart vertically: the craft carries real but moderate business value, because Dave's daily habit lives here.

Medium bubble: it matters more than the shell, less than the services.

Current risk \u0026 defensibility

Risk: medium, and the wrong kind to wall off. UI craft is a speed moat, not a wall — the defense is shipping the next iteration before the copy ships the last one. Legal protection for UI (trade dress, design patents) is slow, expensive, and mostly unenforceable against fast followers.

The real exposure: a clone that looks right but has none of the services behind it could still confuse users. That is a brand problem, not a UI problem.

Guardian\u2019s recommendation

Keep out-running, not out-lawyering. The Kin system and the Atlas lenses are living work — Jonny iterates weekly. A copier is always one version behind by construction.

Next action: keep the design velocity up; pair it with the trademark filing (see Trademark / brand) so a pixel-clone can't also borrow the name.
