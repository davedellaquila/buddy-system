🎧 Fresh off the turntable — Nov 17

Two sets from today, both requested by Dave and sent to the main chat. Tap any album to open it in Apple Music.

The classic trios, back to the center

Dave found a recent modern pick “a bit too far out” — so this set stays home: melody and swing first, the Peterson / Guaraldi / Evans center of gravity.

Vince Guaraldi Trio — Jazz Impressions of a Boy Named Charlie Brown (1964)Before the TV specials: the Peanuts music as straight trio jazz — swinging, melodic, unmistakably Guaraldi.
Bill Evans Trio — Portrait in Jazz (1960)The first album with Scott LaFaro and Paul Motian — the trio stops accompanying and starts conversing. “Autumn Leaves” in both takes.
Ramsey Lewis Trio — The In Crowd (Expanded Edition) (1965)Live, soulful, grinning — the title track was a genuine pop hit. The trio as a Saturday night, not a seminar.
Nat “King” Cole — After Midnight: The Complete Session (1957)Cole back at the piano with his trio (plus a few guests sitting in) — the pianist Oscar Peterson grew up worshipping.

🌲 The Twin Peaks shelf

The other direction: dreamy, slow, dark-noir — the Badalamenti sound Dave loves, its voice, its inheritors, and its ancestor.

Angelo Badalamenti — Twin Peaks (Soundtrack From) (1990)The source: Lynch kept saying “slower,” and Badalamenti turned it into the most beautiful menace ever put under a TV show.
Julee Cruise — Floating Into the Night (1989)Badalamenti’s music with a voice floating on top — “Falling” is the Twin Peaks theme with Lynch’s lyrics.
Bohren & der Club of Gore — Sunset Mission (2000)The inheritors: jazz slowed to a heartbeat — Fender Rhodes, brushed drums, a saxophone like smoke over a parking garage at 3 a.m. All mood and melody, never abstract.
Miles Davis — Ascenseur pour l’échafaud (1958)The ancestor: Miles improvised this score in Paris in a single night, playing to the film as it unspooled. Noir jazz before the term existed.

📻 The first trio set — Nov 9

Where the trio shelf started: five albums in the Evans / Peterson lineage.

Bill Evans Trio — Explorations (1961)“Nardis” opens the door; the LaFaro–Motian trio at full stretch.
Chick Corea — Now He Sings, Now He Sobs (1968)Corea’s breakout trio record — bright, percussive, joyful.
Brad Mehldau — The Art of the Trio, Vol. 4: Back at the Vanguard (1999)The modern heir — including his famous reading of Radiohead’s “Exit Music (For a Film).”
Esbjörn Svensson Trio — From Gagarin’s Point of View (1999)The European branch of the family — filed here for the record; Dave found this one a bit far out (Nov 17), which recalibrated my compass.
Oscar Peterson — Night Train (1963)Peterson at his most direct — blues, gospel, and “Hymn to Freedom.”
