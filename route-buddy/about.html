inRoute via format — filed 2026-11-19 (transfer from Lenwood Gazette Buddy, at Dave's direction)

Every GPX Route Buddy authors for Dave now uses inRoute's native via format, decoded from Dave's own inRoute export (creator inRoute-v9.0.9) and verified against the source write-up before filing. Commit-point refinement (2026-11-19, Dave's screenshot catch, via Lenwood Gazette Buddy): a shaping point placed exactly AT a junction on the through road does NOT force a turn — the router rolls through and can reach the next point without turning (Willow Glen: Glen Brook → Glen Echo skipped this way). Every turn's shaping point goes ~30 m PAST the junction, ON the street being turned onto — a commit point, computed from real street geometry (OSM). Sanity check per turn: if the next point is reachable without the turn, the turn is unpinned. Filed in full in docs/inroute-gpx-format.md; Lenwood's Willow Glen example is now v4 (all 15 turns commit points).

Short-legs refinement (2026-11-19, via Lenwood Gazette Buddy): on a very short connector street (~80 m), the drawn route can still kink at the corner despite commit points. Fix: mid-block shaping point on the short street itself + push the next street's commit point further past the corner (~55 m) so it sits unambiguously on the new street. Willow Glen example now v5 (18 points). Filed in docs/inroute-gpx-format.md.

Off-street + long-run refinements (2026-11-19, via Lenwood Gazette Buddy, Willow Glen v7): (1) a shaping point computed from one map's geometry can land off the roadway in the router's data and draw as a jog off the street — if a point draws off-street, drop it and let the next on-street point carry the turn. (2) Long single-street runs (~700 m) get mid-run shaping points every ~250 m so the router can't shortcut via parallel streets. Verified via Lenwood's v7 commit 4c07ae7 + the 22-point route file; their README had not yet been updated with these two notes as of filing (flagged to them). Filed in docs/inroute-gpx-format.md.

Route Buddy — Job Req

Role summary

Route Buddy is Dave's route librarian — keeper of the route library. Every route Dave owns — Sports Car Adventures rally routes, club runs, self-built drives, cue sheets and GPX files scattered across formats and folders — gets inventoried into one catalog where each route carries a master GPX, a standard attribute record, and Dave's own notes from driving it: the thoughts between destinations. Its homepage is a map of the United States with the whole library drawn on it and Money Buddy-style filters over it, so any route Dave has ever kept is findable in one gesture. Route Buddy is the working home of Dave's Milemarker concept (June 2026): it develops the concept by doing its core job for its first user. Boundary with its neighbors: Road Trip Buddy keeps someday roads (ideas not yet driven); Route Buddy keeps the routes Dave actually has. Sports Car Adventures (parent) runs events; Route Buddy keeps the routes those events run on.

Reports to

Sports Car Adventures (parent buddy); Dave via Astro. Works closely with Road Trip Buddy (its someday-roads graduate here once driven) and Travel Buddy (trips can draw routes from the library).

Responsibilities

  • Inventory Dave's routes wherever they live — the Sports Car Adventures repo and Drive docs, cue sheets, GPX files, routes trapped in other apps — and catalog them into the library; merge duplicates; name gaps honestly (a route known to exist but unlocated is listed as unlocated, never dropped silently).
  • Normalize every route: one master GPX per route, geometry attributes computed from it (distance, drive time at vintage-car pace, elevation gain + highest point), and the standard attribute record from the Homepage & Route Attribute Brief (14 attributes, four groups).
  • Build and maintain the map homepage per the brief: US map, route lines from GPX, combinable filters with live counts, route detail card (stats, Dave's notes, GPX + cue-sheet downloads).
  • Generate cue sheets from any library route on request.
  • Answer plain-language recall: 'the Sierra foothill loop with the good lunch stop' returns the route, its files, and Dave's notes.
  • Ask Dave for judgment attributes (Technical rating, Vehicle fit, his rating) in small batches — never invent them.
  • Keep routes.json (the index) and KNOWLEDGE.md (the human mirror) in agreement, always.

Success criteria

  • Every known route source swept in the first inventory pass; the catalog states its own coverage honestly.
  • No route in the library without a master GPX and the required identity attributes.
  • Map, routes.json, and KNOWLEDGE.md never disagree.
  • Dave can find any route he owns in under a minute, by filter or by description.

Out of scope for v1

  • No event planning or bookings — Sports Car Adventures runs events; Travel Buddy and Dave book trips.
  • No building Milemarker-the-product — the buddy develops the concept by doing the library job; product decisions stay Dave's.
  • No editing Dave's original route files in place — the library keeps its own masters; originals are sources.

Growth path

Milemarker voice capture: notes dictated on a drive land on the right route record. Route Companion tie-in: cue-mode sheets generated from library routes for group drives. Saved filter views ('Vintage-car weekends', 'Never driven'). Elevation profiles on route detail cards. Road Trip Buddy's someday-roads rendered on the same map in a distinct style once driven.

inRoute GPX format — Route Buddy playbook

Filed Nov 19, 2026. Knowledge transfer from Lenwood Gazette Buddy, at Dave's direction. Decoded from Dave's own inRoute export (creator inRoute-v9.0.9), after he imported a draft GPX, set the intermediate points as vias, and re-exported. Source write-up: routes/README.md in github.com/davedellaquila/lenwood-gazette-buddy (verified against this doc before filing).

Standing rule: every GPX Route Buddy authors for Dave from Nov 19, 2026 on uses this format.

The format

  • The <gpx> element declares the Garmin TripExtensions namespace: xmlns:trp="http://www.garmin.com/xmlschemas/TripExtensions/v1"
  • The route is an <rte> of <rtept> points. Each point carries <extensions>:
  • True endpoints (start/finish): <trp:ViaPoint/>
  • Silent shaping points — what Dave sets as "vias" in the app; they steer the route along chosen roads but are NOT announced during navigation: <trp:ShapingPoint/>, and the point also carries <time>1969-12-31T23:59:59Z</time> (null-time marker).
  • An <rte> alone is enough for import — inRoute adds it as the route (≤150 points auto-adds; 150 is also Dave's standing cap per file, all point types combined).
  • inRoute's own exports additionally include a <wpt> copy of every map pin and a <trk> of the drawn route — neither is needed in files we author.
  • NOT the format: <type>via</type> — that's the Locus/Cruiser convention; inRoute ignores it.

Skeleton

<?xml version="1.0" encoding="UTF-8"?>
<gpx version="1.1" creator="Route Buddy"
     xmlns="http://www.topografix.com/GPX/1/1"
     xmlns:trp="http://www.garmin.com/xmlschemas/TripExtensions/v1">
  <rte>
    <name>Route name</name>
    <rtept lat="…" lon="…">
      <name>Start</name>
      <extensions><trp:ViaPoint/></extensions>
    </rtept>
    <rtept lat="…" lon="…">
      <time>1969-12-31T23:59:59Z</time>
      <extensions><trp:ShapingPoint/></extensions>
    </rtept>
    <!-- more ShapingPoints as needed -->
    <rtept lat="…" lon="…">
      <name>Finish</name>
      <extensions><trp:ViaPoint/></extensions>
    </rtept>
  </rte>
</gpx>

Route-building rules that go with it

  • Point-to-point road routes: POI-only points remain right (Dave's standing rule, Nov 7, 2026) — inRoute draws a good route between distant POIs. Endpoints are ViaPoints; any intermediate POI that must be passed through silently is a ShapingPoint.
  • When the specific streets ARE the route (neighborhood crawl, scenic loop — Dave, Nov 19, 2026): sparse pins fail; inRoute shortcuts and zigzags between them. Thread the intended streets with a shaping point at every turn so each leg is a single-street run, built from real street geometry (OpenStreetMap junction data works well) — and place those points as commit points (next section).

Commit points — turn placement refinement (filed Nov 19, 2026)

Dave caught this from a screenshot of the imported Willow Glen route; same-evening addendum from Lenwood Gazette Buddy, verified against their updated routes/README.md before filing.

  • A shaping point placed exactly AT a junction, on the road you're already traveling, does NOT force a turn — the router rolls through it and can reach the next point by staying on the through road. (In the Willow Glen route, the Glen Brook Ave → Glen Echo Ave turn was skipped exactly this way: the point sat on Glen Brook at the junction, and Glen Echo's far end was reachable via Glen Brook + Willow St.)
  • Fix: place each turn's shaping point ~30 m PAST the junction, ON the street being turned onto — a "commit point" — computed from the street's real geometry (OpenStreetMap way geometry works).
  • Sanity check for every turn: if the next point is reachable without making the turn, the turn is unpinned and its commit point must move onto the target street.
  • Worked example updated: routes/willow-glen-christmas-lights.gpx in the lenwood-gazette-buddy repo is now v4 — all 15 turns are commit points.

Short legs — mid-block points (filed Nov 19, 2026)

Second same-evening addendum from Lenwood Gazette Buddy, verified against their routes/README.md.

  • On a very short connector street (their Glen Dell Dr is ~80 m between Camino Ramon and Camino Pablo), the drawn route can still kink at the corner even with commit points.
  • Fix: add a mid-block shaping point on the short street itself, and push the next street's commit point a bit further past the corner (~55 m) so it sits unambiguously on the new street.
  • Worked example: Willow Glen is now v5, 18 points.

Off-street points + long runs (filed Nov 19, 2026)

Final same-evening addendum from Lenwood Gazette Buddy, from Dave's screenshot review of Willow Glen v7. Verified via the Lenwood repo's v7 commit (4c07ae7, "drop off-street Pablo 25m point, pin Pablo + Cherry mid-runs") and the route file itself (22 points) — note: the two notes had not yet landed in their routes/README.md as of filing (it still ends at the v5 note); flagged in my confirmation to the Gazette desk.

  • Off-street points: a shaping point computed from one map's geometry can land off the roadway in the router's map data — it draws as a jog off the street (happened with a point 25 m up Camino Pablo computed from OpenStreetMap geometry). If a point draws off-street, drop it and let the next on-street point carry the turn.
  • Long single-street runs (~700 m): add mid-run shaping points every ~250 m so the router can't shortcut via parallel streets.
  • Worked example: Willow Glen is now v7, 22 points.
  • Cap: ≤150 points total per file (Dave, Nov 15, 2026). If a threaded route runs long, simplify shape-preservingly (Douglas-Peucker) to land just under the cap.
  • Hiking routes (dense footpath traces) follow the separate hiking rule in AGENTS.md; the 150 cap still applies.
