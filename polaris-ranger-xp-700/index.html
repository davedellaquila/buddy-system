🎫 Job ticket #1 — the split CV boot

Dave, Nov 9, 2026: one of the Monkey's CV boots is split and needs replacement. A split boot is a split boot — left open, it throws its grease, eats grit, and takes the joint with it. Here's the plan, in order. Nothing gets ordered until steps 1–2 land.

Maintenance priority #1 · awaiting two facts

Replace the split CV boot

  1. Identify the corner — front/rear, left/right, inner or outer — and photograph it. Inspect the joint itself: grit, play, or clicking means the joint is in question too, and a boot over a ruined joint is false economy.
  2. Confirm the year from the VIN — boot kits split by year and corner (one front-axle listing covers 2006–07 only; rear boot kits list 2008–09 separately). The wrong year buys the wrong boot.
  3. Source the kit — boot, CV grease, both clamps; OEM from the Polaris parts fiche or a proven All Balls / EPI equivalent — for Dave's approval before ordering.
  4. Turn the wrench — Final Drive chapter of the factory service manual; lugs back on at 90 ft-lb. While that corner's apart: check the other three boots, brake pads (replace at 1 mm), and bearing play.
  5. Log it — date, hours, corner, part number, who did it. First entry in the service log.

🗓️ Regular maintenance — the proposal

Drafted from the factory Periodic Maintenance Chart in the 2008 owner's manual (P/N 9921268), pending the year check. One honest wrinkle: the factory's severe-use list includes prolonged low-speed, heavy-load operation — that's firewood work, word for word — so this draft runs oil at the severe-use end. It's a proposal. Dave tunes it; I track it.

Step 0 — baseline catch-up (history is unknown, so start clean)

Oil + filter · air filter · every gearcase fluid · coolant tested (replaced if age unknown) · plugs · belt measured and photographed · brake fluid flushed if age unknown · every grease fitting · all four CV boots · battery tested · pads measured · tires recorded. Logged with date and hours — the service log's first real page.
