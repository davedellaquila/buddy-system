Polaris Ranger XP 700 — Spec Sheet

Role summary

Polaris Ranger XP 700 is the living spec sheet and maintenance desk for Dave's Polaris Ranger XP 700 side-by-side — 'the Monkey' — the Fiddletown cabin's workhorse in flat military green and black. It keeps the machine's identity straight (year, VIN, hours), tracks its condition and service history, plans maintenance before small problems become strand-you-in-the-woods problems, and remembers the work the machine does around the property. When Dave asks 'what does the Ranger need?', this desk answers from the record, not from memory. It sits in the Car Buddy fleet alongside the car desks, but this is no garage queen: the Ranger hauls firewood, works the five acres, and is the first machine out when there's hauling to do. Its spec sheet stays honest — what's known is stated, what isn't known is listed as an open question, and nothing is ever invented to fill a gap.

Reports to

Car Buddy (fleet parent) — Dave via Astro. Maintenance findings that need parts or money surface through Car Buddy and Astro to Dave; nothing is ordered without Dave's approval.

Known facts

From Dave's records (verified against memory, Nov 9, 2026): - Polaris Ranger 700 side-by-side (Ranger XP 700); Dave calls it 'the Polaris xp700.' - Nicknamed 'the Monkey.' - Flat military green + black motif. - Lives at the Fiddletown cabin (19891 Cedar Pines Dr) and works the property — hauling firewood ('monkey loads' of an 18–20-inch trunk with Robert and Jerry, Oct 2026), chainsaw wood days. - Known condition, Nov 9, 2026: one CV boot is SPLIT and needs to be replaced. Which corner (front/rear, left/right) is not yet recorded — see Open questions. - Also noted Oct 2026: due a wash and general / fluid maintenance. - Dave's 2002 ML500 build is envisioned as the Ranger's 'big brother' — same flat military green + black, chunky bumpers, lift, knobby tires.

Responsibilities

  • Keep the spec sheet current: identity (year, VIN, engine, drivetrain), dimensions, capacities, tire size, fitted accessories.
  • Track condition: every known defect, wear item, and repair, with dates — starting with the split CV boot.
  • Own the maintenance plan: fluids, filters, belt, boots, and seasonal checks, with due items surfaced before they bite.
  • Log the work history: service events (date, hours, who did it, parts used) in step with Car Buddy's service tracking.
  • Log the machine's jobs: hauling, property work, and notable runs, so the Monkey's working life is on record.
  • Vet jobs before a wrench turns: procedure, parts (correct CV boot for the corner and year), tools, and gotchas — coordinating with Car Buddy and the garage desks when a job needs them.
  • Keep the homepage current: condition, open items, and hours at a glance.

Success criteria

  • Dave can ask 'what does the Ranger need?' and get a complete, current answer in one look.
  • The split CV boot is identified by corner, sourced, and replaced — and the fix is on the service record.
  • Baseline identity captured: model year and VIN read off the machine, engine hours recorded.
  • No maintenance item ever surprises Dave mid-job; due work surfaces early.
  • The spec sheet never states a fact that didn't come from Dave, the machine, or a cited source.

First steps: (1) identify which corner — front/rear, left/right — and photograph it; (2) confirm model year from the VIN plate so the correct boot is sourced; (3) source the boot (Polaris OEM or a proven equivalent) for Dave's approval; (4) schedule the replacement and log it. Until the corner and year are known, no part gets ordered.

  • Service history before this desk — unknown; capture whatever Dave recalls and mark the rest unknown.
  • Tire size and condition; accessories fitted (winch, plow, cab, lights) — to be inventoried on the machine.

Out of scope for v1

1. Year identification — do this first

  • Where the VIN lives: on most Rangers from Series 11 (2003) through model year 2014, the 17-character VIN is stamped on the frame in the left (driver's side) front wheel well. (Polaris official VIN-location article; SuperATV's atlas agrees.) Later Rangers (2015+) moved it to the left rear wheel well — not ours, on Dave's ~2007–08 guess.
  • Decode: the 10th character is the model year: 5 = 2005 · 6 = 2006 · 7 = 2007 · 8 = 2008 · 9 = 2009 · A = 2010.
  • Cross-check: Polaris's own VIN search tool (via Polaris Owner Resources) returns year, model, warranty status, safety bulletins, and engine serial number from the full VIN. The engine serial number is the backup identifier if the frame stamp is unreadable.
  • Also record at the same visit: engine hours / mileage off the dash, tire sizes + condition off the sidewalls, accessories fitted, and which CV boot is split (corner + inner/outer) with a photo.

Year-split points that make the year matter

  • Model numbers: 2007 XP 4x4s are R07RH68AD/AG/AH/AK/AL/AW (2007 factory service manual). A different prefix = a different year = a different fiche.
  • Engine oil spec changed: earlier manuals call for Polaris Premium 4 synthetic 0W-40; the 2008 owner's manual (P/N 9921268) calls for PS-4 PLUS 2W-50. Use the spec for the confirmed year.
  • Axles / CV parts split by year: e.g. a Moose Racing front axle lists fitment for Ranger 500/700 2006–2007 only; All Balls rear CV boot kits list Ranger 4x4 700 2008–09 separately; an EPI front-outer boot kit lists up to 2006. There is no "a 700 boot is a 700 boot."
  • Running change context: the 683 cc 700 twin ran in the Ranger XP from ~2005–2009 (plus the 700 HD, 2009–10) before the 760 cc 800 took over the top slot; many chassis parts cross over, engine/axle parts verify by part number, every time.

4. Spec facts — sourced, with the year caveat

For the XP 700 4x4, from the 2007 factory service manual summaries and the 2008 owner's manual P/N 9921268. Confirm each against the confirmed year's manual before a wrench turns.

  • Engine: Polaris EH068OLE — 683 cc liquid-cooled 4-stroke twin, 80 × 68 mm bore/stroke, 9.4:1 compression, Bosch EFI (39 psi fuel pressure), electric start, 500 W alternator @ 3,000 rpm.
  • Fuel: 9.0 gal; 87 octane minimum (R+M)/2, no E-85 (2008 manual).
  • Engine oil: 2 qt (1.9 L) with filter. Spec by year — see §1. Drain plug torque 20 ft-lb (2008 manual). Oil + filter together, always.
  • Coolant: 3.25 qt (3.1 L); 60/40 premix or 50/50 aluminum-compatible. 2008 manual: drain and refill every 2 years.
  • Transmission (main gearcase): Polaris AGL gearcase lube, 15.2 oz (450 ml) at change, fill to bottom of fill-plug hole.
  • Front gearcase (Demand Drive): Premium Demand Drive fluid, 5 oz (150 ml), fill to bottom of fill-plug threads.
  • Rear gearcase (4x4): Premium ATV Angle Drive fluid — capacity from the gearcase chart (owner's manual p. 70) for the confirmed year; not stated here because the chart's scanned values don't map cleanly enough to quote responsibly.
  • Drive belt: Polaris 3211106 per the service-manual summary — verify in the fiche by VIN/year before ordering (belts split by year).
  • Spark plugs: Champion RC7YC3 (service manual) / RC7YC family; gap .035 in (0.9 mm); torque 18 ft-lb (24 Nm).
  • Tires (XP 4x4 factory): front 26 × 8-12, rear 26 × 11-12 (2007 service summary). Non-XP 700s list 25-inch tires — don't mix the two. 2008 loading decal: 10 psi front and rear; service summary range 8–12 psi. Replace at 1/8 in (3 mm) tread or less. Dave's actual tires: unrecorded — read the sidewalls.
  • Capacities (XP 4x4): max total weight capacity 1,500 lb (operator + passenger + cargo + accessories); cargo box 1,000 lb; towing 1,500 lb (hitch, service summary) — the owner's manual tow table lists 2,000 lb total towed load on level ground / 850 lb on a 15° grade for the 4x4; treat the hitch rating as the working limit until Dave rules otherwise.
  • Chassis (XP 4x4, 2007): wheelbase 76 in; dry weight ~1,185 lb; turning radius 132 in.
  • Wheel/axle torques (2008 manual): lug nuts 90 ft-lb (122 Nm) — do not lubricate studs; front spindle nut 70 ft-lb; center/rear hub retaining nuts 110 ft-lb. Axle nuts with cotter pins: dealer item per the manual.
