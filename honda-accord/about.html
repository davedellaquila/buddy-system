2) The process, end to end

  1. Quote (5–10 min call): The shop asks for year, trim, VIN, and what's attached to the glass (see §3). They confirm the exact glass is available — same-day is common if it's in local stock, next-day otherwise.
  2. Insurance decision (before booking, if claiming): Verify coverage/deductible (see §4). Most shops, Safelite especially, will run the claim with the insurer directly — Sawyer just needs the policy number and claim go-ahead.
  3. Scheduling: Pick in-shop or mobile (home or Performance Technic). Mobile needs a reasonably flat, dry spot and room to open both front doors; rain can force a reschedule.
  4. Install (~60–90 min hands-on): Trim and wipers off, old glass cut out, frame cleaned and primed, new glass set in fresh urethane, trim/wipers back on, glass cleaned, leak check.
  5. ADAS calibration, if the car has it (~30–90 min extra): Camera recalibrated — static (targets, in-shop), dynamic (a guided drive), or both, per Honda's spec for the year/trim.
  6. Cure — safe drive-away time (at least 1 hour, plan on 2): The urethane needs a minimum of ~1 hour before the car is safe to drive (longer in cold weather; the tech states the exact time). Full bond strength takes 24–72 hours. Total appointment-to-drive-away: roughly 2–3 hours standard, 3–4 hours with calibration.
  7. First 24–48 hours: Leave the retention tape on ~24 hours; don't slam doors; keep a window cracked slightly to equalize pressure; no car wash (especially pressure/automatic) for 24–48 hours; no suction mounts or stickers on the new glass for a day.

Day-planning shortcut for Sawyer: book the mobile install for the start of a workday at Performance Technic. By lunch the cure clock is long done and the car is simply ready — no waiting around anywhere.

3) What the price depends on — and what a shop will ask

A shop cannot quote this car accurately yet. They will ask for, in this order:

  1. Year — TBD ← collect this first; it decides almost everything below
  2. Trim — TBD (LX / Sport / EX / EX-L / Touring, etc.)
  3. VIN — TBD; the VIN pins the exact glass part number and ends all guessing
  4. What's on the glass: a camera housing behind the rearview mirror? Rain-sensing wipers? A heads-up display (Touring)? Lane-departure / forward-collision warnings on the dash?
  5. OEM vs aftermarket (OEE) glass choice

Price factors, in order of impact: - ADAS calibration is the big swing. Rough published ranges (guides checked Oct 2026, NOT quotes): an older Accord with plain glass often lands around $250–$450 installed; a Honda Sensing–era Accord with calibration commonly runs $600–$1,200+. Calibration alone is often $150–$600 of that. - Glass type: OEM Honda glass costs ~20–40% more than quality aftermarket; Honda's own position is that driver-assist systems may not work correctly with non-OE glass, so on a Sensing car, OEM/OEE-grade glass is the safe default. - Features on the glass: acoustic lamination, rain sensor, HUD (Touring) each raise the part price. - Moldings/clips if the old ones don't survive removal — small money (~$25–$75), sometimes unavoidable on a high-mile car.

⚠️ ADAS flag by Accord generation (until this car's year is known): - 2012 and earlier (8th gen and before): No Honda Sensing, no windshield camera. Plain replacement, no calibration. Cheapest case. - 2013–2015 (9th gen, early): Forward Collision Warning was available on some trims (camera-based on some, radar on others) and LaneWatch is a mirror camera (not in the glass). Ask the shop to check by VIN. - 2016–2017 (9th gen, later): Honda Sensing available on most trims, standard on Touring — if this car has it, the windshield camera must be recalibrated after replacement. - 2018–2022 (10th gen): Honda Sensing standard on every trim — calibration is a given; Touring also has a special HUD windshield. - 2023 and newer (11th gen): Sensing standard, larger glass — calibration a given, highest part cost.

So: year + a photo of the rearview-mirror area (camera housing present or not) settles which world this car is in. That's the single most useful fact to collect next.

4) Insurance explainer — how glass usually works

Sawyer's actual policy details are TBD — Dave is sending the insurance info separately, and Astro will relay it here. This section is the general picture so that, once the policy lands, the decision is arithmetic, not research.

  • It's comprehensive, not collision. A cracked windshield (rock, debris, stress crack) is claimed under comprehensive coverage ("other than collision"). Liability-only coverage pays nothing toward the car's own glass.
  • California has no zero-deductible glass law (unlike FL/KY/SC). By default, the comprehensive deductible applies — commonly $250, $500, or $1,000.
  • …unless the policy has separate glass coverage. Many CA policies offer a glass endorsement / full-glass add-on with its own, much lower deductible — often $0, $50, or $100. This is the fact Dave's incoming policy info will settle: does Sawyer's plan have a glass deductible, and how much is it?
  • The decision rule once that's known:
  • Glass deductible ≤ ~$100 → claim it; out-of-pocket is the deductible, insurer pays the rest, and the shop can usually bill the insurer directly.
  • Standard comprehensive deductible $500–$1,000 → compare against the cash quote first. On a plain-glass (pre-Sensing) Accord, the whole job may cost less than the deductible — pay cash, no claim. On a Sensing car with calibration, a claim may still win.
  • No comprehensive at all → cash price; get two quotes (one mobile local, one national) — they can differ by hundreds.
  • Good to know: Comprehensive glass claims are generally not treated like an at-fault accident claim, and many insurers waive the deductible for repairs of small chips — but this is a full replacement (a crack), so the deductible rules above are the ones that matter. Confirm the specifics with the insurer or the shop's claims desk before authorizing work.
  • What the shop/insurer will need for a claim: policy number, date the damage was noticed, and the car's year/trim/VIN — the same facts-to-collect list as §3.
