Fireplace Buddy

Executive summary

Fireplace Buddy keeps Dave's Fiddletown cabin fireplace burning — literally. It tracks the wood supply (Robert's Friday-collect/Saturday-cut rhythm), the split-stack-cover backlog, chimney maintenance, and winter readiness. When the snow comes, the wood is dry, the chimney is clean, and the fire's ready.

The idea

Dave heats with wood at the cabin, and there's a whole operation behind it: Robert cutting, Carl's splitter to borrow, wet wood separated from dry, piles covered before the rain. Fireplace Buddy is the one place that knows the state of all of it — how much wood is ready, what needs splitting, when the chimney was last swept, and whether there's enough seasoned wood to get through winter.

How it works

The buddy tracks: (1) Wood inventory — what's split, stacked, covered, and ready vs. what still needs work. (2) Robert's rhythm — Friday collection, Saturday cutting, confirmed weekly. (3) The borrow — Carl Towne's community wood splitter, with the ask text ready to send. (4) Chimney — sweep history and inspection schedule (currently unknown, to be established). (5) Winter readiness — a pre-season checklist: enough dry wood, chimney clean, fireplace type confirmed.

Revenue potential

None — this is cabin infrastructure. The value is a warm house in January and never scrambling for dry wood in a storm.

Room for growth

Burn-rate tracking (cords per winter, so Dave knows how much to put up). Moisture-meter readings per stack. A 'burn tonight?' check — is there enough dry wood within reach? Integration with the cabin winter-prep checklist.

Out of scope for v1

No automated ordering of firewood or chimney services — Dave handles vendors. No IoT stove sensors (unless Dave gets interested).

Open questions

What type is the fireplace — masonry, insert, or stove? When was the chimney last swept? Is Robert's Friday/Saturday rhythm still active? How many cords does Dave burn in a typical winter?

Fireplace Door Seal Fix — 36-Elite (Travis Industries / Fireplace Xtrordinair)

Date: 2026-10-13 · Unit: Fireplace Xtrordinair 36-Elite (cabin fireplace) Symptom: carbon/soot building up on the inside of the glass; suspected door-seal air leak.

Sources: repo copy of the 36-Elite Owner's Manual (doc 93508091, 2015 ed.), Travis dealer parts lists, retailer listings.

The part to buy

Travis Industries #98500717 — "Door Gasket, FPX Wood Fireplace" (confirmed in the manual's Replacement Parts List, printed p.19)

  • Spec: flat fiberglass tape, 1/16" thick × 5/8" wide × 150" long, no self-stick adhesive. One kit does two 44 or 36 double doors, or one 36 single door.
  • Kit includes the gasket tape, gasket cement, and brush.
  • Reference price: $60 at woodmanspartsplus.com (in stock per their listing — but they state Travis policy restricts them to New England sales only, so this is not a California option).
  • Do NOT buy 98500731 for this job — that is the glass gasket kit (glass-to-door seal), a different part.
  • If the latch itself is worn rather than just misadjusted: #98500712 "Door Latch Assembly, Right Side of Double Door, FPX 36A & 36 Elite."

Where to buy (Dave is in California): 1. A local authorized Travis Industries / Fireplace Xtrordinair dealer — Travis sells hearth parts through its dealer network (dealer locator on travisindustries.com). Fastest path: call ahead with part #98500717. 2. fire-parts.com (Travis dealer site) — check their parts catalog for 98500717. 3. In a pinch: any hearth shop sells generic 5/8" flat fiberglass stove-gasket tape + high-temp stove gasket cement; functionally equivalent, though OEM kit is preferred.

Replacement procedure

Manual guidance (printed p.18, "Check Door Seal, Adjust if Necessary"): the door latch and door gasket work together to seal the firebox; leaking air makes the fire burn too fast and kills overnight burns and burn-rate control. The gasket should adhere to the door and be intact on both doors; it follows the door perimeter along the outside edge (the inside edge has no gasket).

Step-by-step (synthesized from the manual's gasket guidance + Travis's standard door-gasket replacement text):

  1. Let the fireplace go fully cold (at least 2 hours after the last coal).
  2. Open the door(s) and pull the old gasket out of the outer groove of the door retainer. Scrape out all residual cement so the groove is clean.
  3. Run a bead of wood-stove gasket cement into the groove.
  4. Lay the new gasket in, starting at the lower left corner. Do not stretch it — lay it in relaxed, then cut off the excess.
  5. Close the door. The cement fully cures with heat from the stove; you may need to open and close the door repeatedly to get the gasket to seat fully.
  6. Do one hot burn to cure the cement, then check the seal again.

Seal check (dollar-bill test): close the door on a dollar bill at several points around the perimeter. If it pulls out with no resistance anywhere, the seal is still leaking there.

Door latch adjustment (do this too)

Manual, printed p.18 — "Door Latch Adjustment":

  • Closing the handle should pull the doors in to a snug fit. If the latch is loose: move one washer from the inner side of the latch shaft to the outer side. For finer adjustment, tighten the outer nut against the inner nut (9/16" wrench).
  • "Door Latch Rod Adjustment": if the latch rod doesn't keep the bottom of the doors snug against the firebox, bend the rod in slightly for a tighter fit.

Do the latch adjustment after the new gasket is in — the gasket changes how the door sits, so latch tension set on the old gasket will be wrong.

Other things to check (carbon on glass has more than one cause)

  • Wet wood is the #1 cause of sooty glass (manual, "Selecting Wood," p.14): wood over ~20% moisture cools the fire and produces the smoke that deposits on glass. Knock-test or meter-test the firewood.
  • Low, smoldering burns: running the air control at minimum with a cool fire deposits soot; small hot creosote accumulation in the lower corners at minimum air is considered normal, heavy/fast-returning soot is not.
  • Logs too close to the glass / overfilled firebox — flames licking the glass scorch it permanently.
  • Engaging the catalytic combustor too early: keep the bypass open until the unit is hot (15–30 min); closing it on a cool fire makes dirty smoke.
  • Airwash disruption: the 36-Elite feeds glass-cleaning air through holes above the door via the air control (manual p.12). A door leak short-circuits this — cool leak air chills the glass and smoke condenses on it. So the leak and the soot are plausibly linked, but fix the wood/burn habits too if soot returns.
  • After the repair, clean the glass with a cleaner made for fireplace glass, only when the glass is cold.
