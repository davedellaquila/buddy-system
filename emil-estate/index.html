Executive summary

Emil Dell'Aquila — Dave's dad — died July 1, 2026, and Dave is managing the estate. Emil's Estate is the one desk where everything about the estate lands: the asset inventory (Emil's house for sale, the Melody Lane rental with tenant Larry Osana, Emil's 1931 Ford Model A and MG TD, the HomePod Minis from Dad's place), the document flow from the realtor (text and email), and the deadlines that matter — title transfers, the sale, and the eventual inheritance disbursement. The idea

Keep the estate as one living file instead of scattered across texts, emails, and memory. Emil's Estate tracks what the estate owns, what paperwork has arrived, what's still outstanding, and who's involved (executor, attorney, family, the realtor, the tenant). It separates the man from the administration: Emil Buddy is the living dossier of Dave's dad — the person. Emil's Estate is the paperwork of settling his life — the administration. People live on; paperwork settles.

The problem

Estates die in inboxes. The realtor sends documents by text and by email; title-transfer rules (REG 5, probate thresholds) carry eligibility windows and deadlines; a rental property needs rent tracked and a tenant managed; and the inheritance disbursement timing is still unknown — with questions hanging in text messages instead of one place. Without a single desk, things slip: a 40-day window, a lease term, a document that arrived by text and never got filed. Emil's Estate is the single desk.

  • The Melody Lane rental — the house Dave grew up in; tenant: Larry Osana. Address and lease terms still unknown.
  • Emil's cars — a 1931 Ford Model A and an MG TD, moved October 10 from Emil's house to Rachel's (On the Road Again Classics, Morgan Hill/Gilroy) for storage. The 40-day post-death wait has elapsed and the vehicles are excluded from the $208,850 small-estate threshold (deaths after April 1, 2025). Every estate document gets filed against the asset it belongs to — house-sale docs under Emil's house sale, lease docs under Melody Lane — with dates and what's still outstanding.

Revenue potential

None — this is personal infrastructure, not a product. The only money that moves through this buddy is Dave's own: the house-sale proceeds, rental income, and the eventual inheritance disbursement. Per the standing rule, the section stays and says so plainly.

Walkthrough — J.P. Morgan "Internal Transfer or Distribution of Funds from Decedent's IRA"

Prepared 2026-11-18 by Emil's Estate desk. Source record (from Dave's Nov 18 photos): ~/workspace/goals/digitize-paper-records/files/estate/jpmorgan-decedent-ira-form-emil-7719.md

Nothing here is tax advice. The §4 route and the RMD question interact with Dave's wider tax picture (margin-loan interest, a possible AAPL sale) — Daniel (tax adviser) should bless the route before Dave signs. The desk's job is to lay out the choices plainly.

The account

  • Custodian: J.P. Morgan Securities LLC (JPMS)
  • Decedent: Emil Dellaquila — Traditional/SEP IRA ending 7719
  • Balance: $144,151.93 (Oct 23, 2026; will have moved with the market since)
  • Split three ways among Dave, Ron, Emile → Dave's proportionate share ≈ $48,050
  • Each brother files his own form. Dave's elections bind only Dave.

Already done (§1–§3) — no action needed

  • §1 Account type: Traditional/SEP IRA ✔
  • §2 Beneficiary identity: David Dell'Aquila, non-spouse, DOB 11/3/1964, 6929 Lenwood Way, San Jose ✔
  • §3 Election: "Transfer or distribute my proportionate amount" — his designated percentage share of cash + divisible securities; indivisible securities are liquidated to cash once all authorized parties agree ✔

DECISION 1 — §4: how Dave receives his share (pick exactly ONE)

Problem on the photographed copy: 4A, 4B, and 4E are all marked, but the form says "Fill out only one section (A, B, C, D or E)" — and the receiving-account fields are blank in all three. As photographed, the form would likely be rejected. Cleanest fix: start from a fresh copy of the form (v12.6) and complete only the chosen section, rather than crossing things out.

4A — Transfer to a JPMS Beneficiary IRA (inherited IRA)

  • What happens: Dave opens a Beneficiary IRA at JPMS; his ~$48k share moves into it trustee-to-trustee. Nothing is cashed out.
  • Tax: No tax now. Not reported on Form 1099-R. Money stays invested and tax-deferred.
  • The catch — the SECURE Act 10-year rule: Dave is a non-spouse beneficiary, so the entire inherited IRA must be emptied by Dec 31, 2036 (end of the 10th year after the year of death). And because Emil died after his required beginning date (he had a 2026 RMD), annual distributions are also required in years 1–9 (2027–2035) under the final IRS regulations — Daniel to confirm the yearly amounts. Every dollar that comes out is taxed as ordinary income in the year it comes out — but Dave controls the timing and can spread it across up to ten tax years and lower-income years.
  • Flexibility: A JPMS Beneficiary IRA is not a life sentence — it can later be moved to another custodian's inherited IRA by trustee-to-trustee transfer (it must stay titled as an inherited IRA).
  • Step zero: the receiving Beneficiary IRA account number goes in §4A — if Dave doesn't already have one at JPMS, opening it comes first (Beneficiary Services: (800) 648-4782).
  • §5 is skipped entirely on this route (no distribution, no withholding).

4B — Distribution to a Chase checking/savings account (ACH)

  • What happens: His whole share is paid out to a Chase account (account number blank on the photographed copy — Dave would need to supply one; whether he holds a Chase account is not in the estate file).
  • Tax: Fully taxable as ordinary income in 2026 — roughly $48k stacked on top of his other 2026 income. Reported on Form 1099-R in Dave's name. No 10-year rule to manage afterwards; the money is simply out.
  • Requires §5 withholding elections (below).

4E — Distribution by Fed Funds wire ($25 fee)

  • Same as 4B in every way that matters — taxable 2026 distribution, 1099-R, §5 applies — just a different rail (wire instead of ACH) and a $25 fee. Wire instructions blank on the copy.

4C / 4D

  • The remaining distribution methods on the form (the filed photo record doesn't detail them). Any of B–E is a taxable distribution; only A is a transfer.

The trade-off in one line

4A = no tax now, income spread over up to 10 years with required annual distributions, one more account to keep. 4B/4E = one clean payout, but the full ~$48k lands in 2026 income at Dave's 2026 marginal rate. Given everything else in Dave's 2026 tax picture, this is exactly the kind of choice Daniel should bless before signing.

DECISION 2 — §5: withholding (ONLY if a distribution route B–E is chosen)

  • 5A Federal: election via Form W-4R (line 2) — blank on the copy. If no election is made, the IRS default withholding for a nonperiodic IRA distribution applies. Dave picks a rate (or elects out) based on whether he wants tax prepaid out of the payout or settled later via estimates/payment with his return — Daniel's call territory again.
  • 5B State: California election — no option visibly selected on the copy.
  • If 4A is chosen, skip §5 entirely.

§6 — Signature

  • Unsigned on the photographed copy. The signature must be dated within 60 days of submission — so sign last, once §4 is corrected and the form is ready to go, not before.

DECISION 3 — §7: identity verification (pick one)

  • 7A — a Registered Representative at a Chase branch verifies Dave's identity, or
  • 7B — a J.P. Morgan Advisor does it, or
  • 7C — a notary public does it. All three are equivalent; it's whichever is easiest to get to.

⏰ Deadline watch — year-of-death RMD (Dec 31, 2026)

Separate from the §4 choice: if Emil's 2026 RMD was not fully satisfied before his death on July 1, 2026, the beneficiaries must distribute the remainder by Dec 31, 2026 — about six weeks from Nov 18. Miss it and the penalty is 25% of the shortfall (potentially 10% if corrected within the 2-year window). This remainder would be a taxable distribution to the beneficiaries regardless of which §4 route each brother picks for the rest.

Already on Daniel's list (added Nov 18 at Dave's direction): Was Emil's 2026 RMD fully satisfied before July 1? If not, how is the remainder split among the three brothers before Dec 31, 2026, and does taking it change the §4 route each should elect? — This one is time-sensitive; it shouldn't wait in a queue behind non-urgent questions.

  • Dave signs and submits himself — the desk does not submit anything.

Decisions Dave needs to make (summary)
